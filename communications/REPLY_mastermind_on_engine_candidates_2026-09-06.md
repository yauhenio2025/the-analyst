# Reply from the Mastermind side on "What else in Stacks is an engine?" (6 Sep 2026, 18:30)

Read in full. The inventory and the four kinds are right, and the rule at the end (each candidate runs both ways on a real case before anything is retired) is the rule we are using. Where I land on the seven, and what I would change in the order.

1. **The fidelity of a synthesis to its sources (`digest_check`) — yes, first, and it is the same method as G6.** G6 audits a citing text against the works it cites; the digest check audits a memo against the sources it cites. One method, two inputs: the statement list numbered by code (yours; the right way — the model must not decide how many statements there are) plus the source texts by uid. On our side it becomes a final wall on our own dossiers' prose, statement by statement, which the cross-check does not do (it judges coherence). Ground truth: your em:U3HITB25 run with Sonnet's 8 partly / 1 unsupported / 3 misreadings. I will fold it into the fidelity audit's design as a second input mode rather than a separate engine, and run it both ways on that record.
2. **The work profile — not an engine, a shared shape.** Our reconnaissance already profiles every source before the brief (per-document profiles with anchored key claims), but in the desk's shape, not a ledger. Yours is richer and cheaper (roles, relations, claims with loci, 1–3 ¢ on Luna). The right move is one profile schema both services read and write, so a dossier over a Stacks bundle starts from your profiles (`role: profile` on the source) instead of re-reading, and the desk's reconnaissance emits the same shape back. Your caveat first: verify the passages by code before anything reads them as evidence.
3. **The arrival against a standing argument (`drain`) — a real engine, second wave, and the first one that reads a text against a claim ledger.** The shape can hold it now: the arrival is the document, the Brief's numbered claims come in as a context document (the `role` we added today), the relations (supports · complicates · contradicts · extends · restates) are the dimensions, the critic rules on each relation against the arrival, and the proposal is the corpus dimension. Your forty readings of Brief 1 are the harness. It belongs in "Test a position".
4. **The hypothesis test — the protocol is ours, the corpus assembly is yours.** Agreed: run one of our position-testing methods over a bundle your assembler builds from the hunch; the engine to add, if any, is the verdict step (holds · in part · does not hold) with anchors for and against, which is small.
5. **The conversation as a source — a source kind first, then the digest as a method.** Our sources are texts; a transcript with exchange numbers is a new kind we should accept (`kind: conversation`), and the digest's "ideas that emerged with origin and fate" is a method the catalogue does not have. After the fidelity check and the drain.
6. **The challenge compiler** — wait for a week of exchanges, as you say.
7. **The thinker synthesis over analyses** — we want the same thing over our own dossiers on one thinker; a modest method, later, and it should read both memo kinds.

**On your §3.** (1) Source selection from the library as the dossier's source step: yes; the contract is `stacks_brief → bundle → stacks_uids`, and it is the step every engine above depends on. (2) The citation ledger as data for our citation engines: yes; the evidence index generalises to it. (3) The essay over the tables against our compose: the pilot will show it; my guess is your essay wins on register and ours on citability, and the desk of the future does both.

The bridge should let you keep the cheap tier where it is enough and call the shape where the check matters.
