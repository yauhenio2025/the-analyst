# The Stacks' Citation Universe and the Mastermind: three questions that should be engines, and how the two services would talk (2026-09-06)

Asked by the owner while the fourth queue cooks: the Stacks' Citation Universe (`zotero-stacks`, `app/static/cites.html` and `app/cites*.py`) writes memos on how a target thinker engages another thinker, whether that treatment is justified by the cited texts, and where it sits in the secondary literature. Those three activities look like engines. What would it take, and what does "the two services talk to each other" mean concretely? Read: the Stacks' design memos (`communications/2026-09-05_citation_universe.md`, `2026-09-06_citation_memos_beyond_the_ledger.md`), the memo lane (`app/cites_memo.py`, 1,711 lines, and its four system prompts), the materials, reciprocal, context and essay modules, the API contract; on our side the engine map (`TRIAGE_engine_catalogue_CODEX_2026-09-05.md`), the two existing citation engines, the dossier's source kinds and the `src/sources/stacks.py` adapter. The Stacks repo is now cloned at `~/projects/zotero-stacks` (read only; nothing changed there).

## 1. What the Citation Universe is today

Two layers, both real and running.

**The ledger** (`cites.py`, `cites_extract.py`): every reference and every named person in every held text of a thinker, extracted page-chunk by page-chunk (Gemini Flash, about $3 for Riley's 83 texts), consolidated into cited works and cited persons that are the same objects across thinkers, resolved to library records and to Referee papers, aggregated under settings (citations only or citations and mentions, which text types, min count, self-citations), overlaid for two thinkers, pushed to the Referee. This is corpus authority: who cites whom, how often, where, with the first citing sentence and the printed page. Nothing in the Mastermind does this and nothing should; it belongs with the library.

**The memo lane** (`cites_memo.py`, plus `cites_materials.py` for acquisition): for a pair A → P it gathers every passage in A's held texts that cites P (from the ledger, with sentence windows and the section), has Fable plan the memo (sections, questions, warnings, register), reads each citing text once (`DOC_SYSTEM`: the text's argument, P's place in it, the plan's questions, and per passage what is cited, the move, the stance, a reading, a verbatim quote), writes across (`ACROSS_SYSTEM`: the essay to the plan, markers `[ref N]` that become notes with loci and quotes), edits in a register, renders, files the result into Zotero as a meta record with `stacks-` markers and relations to every text read. Two further lenses were designed and built this morning: **accuracy** (`ACCURACY_SYSTEM`: per passage that cites a specific work W of P at a locus, retrieve the page of the held copy of W or search its text, then: attribution kind, what A attributes, what P says there, a verbatim quote from P, verdict accurate / fair / selective / stretched / misattributed / unverifiable) and **reception** (`RECEPTION_SYSTEM`: each held secondary text on P read once for its argument, stance, and position on the themes the engagement turns on, with a quote; then across: where A sits among P's readers, whom A never engages). The materials operation assembles the pair's corpus (A's citing texts; P's cited works, held or wanted; the reception, from the Stacks' semantic layer and the Referee's harvest) as one bundle and fires the fetch for what is not held. Every quote in every stage is verified against the rendition by code and printed "(not found in the text)" otherwise. Cost: a small pair $2–3, Riley → Weber $5–6.

So the three activities the owner named exist as four prompts inside one module, each hand-built and each good: they face the text, they demand verbatim quotes, they carry loci. What they lack is exactly what the shape gives everything else: a critic pass, a findings ledger the desks can lift by id, tables and figures made from that ledger, a cross-check, a validation harness, and a place in the catalogue where a dossier's planner can choose them.

## 2. The three questions in the engine map

They are already on Codex's 81-question map, in the genealogy family, and none is under the shape yet:

| Stacks lens | Map question | State in the Mastermind |
|---|---|---|
| engagement (A on P) | **G5 — Citation and invocation map**: how do the supplied works cite one another and use those citations? Answer: citing work, cited work/thinker, citation context, function, source location, evidence of allusion. Plus the older `aoi_engagement_mapping` (how does the subject author engage each theme from the source thinker: engagement inventory, divergence character) | `citation_invocation_map` v1 exists (references, propositions, uses, clusters, recognition) and `aoi_engagement_mapping` exists; both are excluded from offers; neither has a `process:` block |
| accuracy (is A's treatment justified by P's texts) | **G6 — Fidelity to cited sources**: how faithfully does the text represent the positions of the supplied works it cites? Answer: attributed position, source position in context, faithful / selective / distorted / contested, anchors, inspected coverage | new; no engine |
| reception (is A's reading mainstream) | **G8 — Reception across readers and communities**: how do the supplied responses interpret and evaluate an idea over time? Answer: idea, receiver, interpretation or evaluation, source and date, relation to earlier reception, coverage limits | new; no engine |

The Stacks' prompts are better seeds than the map's preserved questions, because they were built from the ideal output and have run on real pairs. Under the shape they become three corpus methods of the kind P1/P2 and the second queue's G2–G4 already are:

- **Engagement map (G5, seeded by DOC_SYSTEM + ACROSS_SYSTEM).** Documents: A's citing texts. Document dimensions: the place of P in this text; per passage the thing cited, the move (authority / evidence / foil / dialogue / genealogy / illustration / courtesy / self-positioning), the stance (adopts / builds on / qualifies / disputes / mentions), the reading, the anchor. Corpus dimension: P's place across A's work and its trajectory by year, with paired anchors (`doc:`/`doc-b:`). Tables the desks lift: passage × move × stance × locus; the trajectory by text and year.
- **Fidelity audit (G6, seeded by ACCURACY_SYSTEM).** Documents: pairs of (A's passage, P's work at the locus). Document dimensions per pair: attribution kind; what A attributes (anchor in A); what P says there (anchor in P, verbatim); verdict with the reason; edition and locus notes. Corpus dimension: A's citation practice across the checked sample, with the sample's coverage stated. The two-key wall (`anchor:` from A, `anchor-b:` from P) is precisely the evidence rule this method needs; it exists already.
- **Reception map (G8, seeded by RECEPTION_SYSTEM).** Documents: the held secondary texts on P. Document dimensions per reader: argument, stance, position per theme with anchor, whether the reader cites A. Corpus dimension: the readings A is consonant with, argues against, or never engages; the most-cited citers A does not cite (from counts, no model). Tables: reader × theme × position; A's position among readers.

What the shape adds to each: the cheap-tier extraction per dimension in parallel, the critic ruling on every row against the sources (the Stacks verifies quotes; it does not check the readings), the ledger the spine and tables cite by id, the reconcile of table cells after the check, the crosscheck, the release standard and the study harness. What the shape does not have and must receive from the Stacks: the passages with loci (the ledger's gather), the page-window retrieval inside a large cited work (the accuracy lens's `how: page` / `how: search`, indispensable for Economy and Society), the materials and the fetch, the plan's register, the edit pass, and filing into Zotero.

## 3. What "talking to each other" means

Half the bridge exists. The Mastermind already accepts sources by reference to the Stacks: `SourceSpec.kind` includes `stacks_export` (the one-file clean-text export with `===== [n/N] Creator (Year) — Title — [Library · Key] =====` headers, auto-split into documents keyed by the Zotero key), `stacks_view` and `stacks_uids` (the adapter in `src/sources/stacks.py` calls the Stacks' export endpoint at `STACKS_URL`). On Render that URL points nowhere, so today the bridge only works on the desk machine. The Stacks, for its part, already talks to an external service the way we would want: the Referee client (`app/referee.py`: base URL and API key from env, `httpx`, read-only calls, explicit push on a button).

The exchange, concretely:

**Stacks → Mastermind (run a method or a whole dossier).**
1. The Stacks assembles the corpus for the pair as it does now (materials), then calls `POST /v1/dossier/jobs` with `sources` of kind `stacks_uids` (A's citing texts, P's cited works, the secondary texts, each carrying its uid) and an `intent` ("How does Riley engage Weber, is the engagement justified by Weber's texts he cites, and where does it sit among Weber's readers?"), `entry: chosen` with a path of the three engines, `output.tables`, and, when wanted, `figures` and `plates`. For the fidelity audit the Stacks also sends its **evidence index** as one more document: the ledger's passages with ref ids, loci and sentence windows, and for each cited locus the retrieved page window of P's held copy. The engine then reads pairs rather than hunting through a 1,500-page work, and the desks' anchors carry the printed pages.
2. The Mastermind runs the dossier as it runs any other (the brief is skipped when the path is chosen; reconnaissance still profiles the sources), and the Stacks polls `GET /v1/dossier/jobs/{id}` as the live checks do, then fetches the dossier markdown, the tables, the figures.
3. The Stacks files the result as it files its own memos (`file_record`: a meta record, `stacks-` markers, relations to every text read), and adds one marker: `stacks-engine: mastermind dossier-<id>` so the two memo kinds sit side by side in the Memos view and can be compared.

**Mastermind → Stacks (fetch by reference, and know what is held).**
4. `STACKS_URL` set to the Stacks' Render service with an API key, so `stacks_uids` works on the deployed desk; the adapter already handles unavailability with a plain error. Nothing else is needed for texts.
5. Optionally, a `materials` step in the Mastermind's reconnaissance for a pair brief: ask the Stacks `GET /api/cites/materials?author=…&person=…` which of P's cited works are held, and say in the brief what the fidelity audit can and cannot check. Without it the audit simply marks `unheld` rows, as the Stacks' lens does.

**The shared object.** Both sides already speak the same evidence language: a row is a finding with a verbatim anchor and a document key, and the Stacks' verify-by-code is the same normalisation idea as our wall. Two conventions make the join clean: the document key of a row is the Stacks uid (`doc: em:FNI5R43Z`), and an anchor keeps the rendition's page markers (`[p. 17 | PDF p. 31]`) so a table cell can print the printed page. Our walls index the text as given; the Stacks' page-marked rendition already carries those markers.

## 4. Costs, and what changes for the reader

Riley → Weber today in the Stacks: 18 texts, about 30 passages, $5–6. Through the Mastermind: the engagement map over 18 citing texts at standard depth (one strong read plus a critic per text, then the corpus synthesis) about $4; the fidelity audit over 30 passage-work pairs with the Stacks' page windows about $2 (small pairs, mostly the critic); the reception map over ten secondary texts about $2; the desks (spine, tables, compose, crosscheck) $1–2 as in the live checks. **About $9–12 for a memo** that arrives with a checked ledger, three or four tables lifted from it, a cross-check verdict, and, when asked, figures; against the Stacks' $5–6 for prose with verified quotes and notes. The Stacks' memo is faster (minutes) and filed in place; the Mastermind's is slower (30–45 minutes for a corpus of that size) and needs the round trip.

Images, video and audio: the desks make figures and plates now (report-as-diagram is a standalone capability on a finished job); a podcast or a film from a dossier would be a further deliverable kind on the same job, downstream of the same ledger, and belongs to a later commission.

## 5. What I would do, in order

1. **The citation family into the next queue** (Codex, design with the Stacks' four prompts as the seed questions, the map's G5/G6/G8 as the contract): engagement map, fidelity audit, reception map, as corpus methods with document and corpus dimensions as in §2; validate on a pair we hold both sides of (Riley → Weber is in the library; the Stacks' bundle 299 "Castoriadis and Weber" and its Riley–Weber memo are the ground truth to compare against). Release under the standard.
2. **The bridge on the Mastermind side** (plumbing, mine): `STACKS_URL` and a key on Render; `stacks_uids` exercised end to end from the deployed desk; an `evidence_index` source kind (or simply a document with that role) so the fidelity audit reads pairs with page windows; document keys as Stacks uids and page markers preserved through the walls.
3. **The bridge on the Stacks side** (a Stacks session): "memo via the Mastermind" on the memo form, which builds the materials, posts the dossier job with the chosen path, polls, files the result with the `stacks-engine` marker, and shows both memo kinds side by side.
4. **One pilot, both ways**: Riley → Weber written by the Stacks' lane and by the Mastermind's three engines from the same materials; owner's read first, then Sonnet on the two memos in both orders. That decides whether the Stacks' lane is retired, kept as the fast path, or kept as the planner and editor around the Mastermind's ledgers.

Nothing here is blocked on the other service's owner: step 1 is our queue, step 2 is our plumbing, and the Stacks' contract already has the export the bridge reads. The one decision for the owner is whether step 1 goes ahead of the remaining forty-seven questions in the map; I would put it first, because it is the first case where three engines power work a person is already doing.
