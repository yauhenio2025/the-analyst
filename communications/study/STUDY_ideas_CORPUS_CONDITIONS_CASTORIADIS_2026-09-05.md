## A real comparative reading, with a restricted genealogy

This final uses all three documents substantively. Its 29 finding rows include two cross-document pairs, F2 and F13, and its sections repeatedly connect technique, political institutions, and capitalist calculation. The central finding is coherent: Castoriadis disputes the separation of technology from its social ensemble, political form from effective power, and economic calculation from instituted values. The apparatus section also differentiates the essays' objects and occasions. This fulfills much more of the corpus request than the completed Deutschmann Conditions reading, which effectively selected one article.

Several distinctions are sound. F21 retains institution's excess over technique. F22 preserves Western liberties as emancipatory achievements within the criticism of liberal oligarchy. F25 identifies workers' struggles as contributing to capitalist stabilization, without turning resistance into intentional support. The prose acknowledges local technical efficiency, the unfinished democratic-economic programme, and the refusal to prescribe a guaranteed future. F17 correctly identifies the 1997 essay's declared immanent method. The reader gets an integrated interpretation rather than three disconnected summaries.

Its genealogy nevertheless remains mostly a common-framework reconstruction. The self-citation paragraph names several earlier works but misses C90:141's explicit citation of *Technique* as a 1973 text. C90:153 expressly dates economic principles to 1957. These passages would establish continuity and constrain any development story more directly than repeated concepts alone. The final avoids an explicit invented chronology, but its “later texts inherit” formulation does not distinguish edition dates, prior formulations, and demonstrated transmission. References to unsupplied works establish a citation history, not independently verified contents.

## The later essays also argue

The opening says the later texts inherit social constitution “rather than establishing it again.” F2 merges P6.F1 and P6.F9, pairing C90's instituted individual with C97's social imaginary across spheres. A general social-constitution premise plausibly persists. But the prose also says the 1997 signification appears “without fresh definition,” and turns that into a deficiency of operationalization. C97:52 actually develops mastery at length: its relation to psychic omnipotence, its non-exclusive presence in other societies, capitalism's extension across social spheres, and its distinctive rational means. C90:95–115 likewise reasons about social formation and substantive norms. These passages warrant distinguishing an inherited general orientation from the arguments developed under it.

F8 similarly presents regime-defined products and costs as a given, using P6.F5 alongside a document row. Yet C97:74 argues for nonseparability through firm interaction, external effects, collective synergy, legal boundaries, and accumulated history. C90:129–139 independently develops the monetary and imputation argument. F13's final wording, that the inseparability critique “moves” from technical ensembles to economic imputation, is better: it can describe a changed application without requiring silence in the later text. The source offers an even closer bridge through C84:175's exclusion of exhaustion and boredom from accounted costs, then C90's explicitly political imputation rules.

## Two proposed holes need closer source engagement

F28 anchors the observation that conquest-oriented societies also display mastery. The judgment then says the 1997 essay lacks the controlled comparison needed to distinguish capitalist rational mastery. But immediately after that observation, C97:52 supplies two distinguishing features: mastery targets the totality of social life and pursues its ends through rational maximization/minimization. Those distinctions may be contestable; the final needs to evaluate them before concluding that comparative differentiation is missing. A demand for controlled historical comparison adds an evidential standard that this conceptual argument has not itself made necessary.

The treatment of “absolute creation” has a similar problem. The final says its ontological force disappears unless natural analogues and degrees of novelty are comparatively tested. C84:67 already allows an accidental vague natural analogy and limits its claim to crucial instances; 69–71 distinguishes instituting a type from merely changing an empirical state. The question is whether that argument about form succeeds. Counting analogues alone would not settle it. The prose appropriately calls inheritance selective, but “Aristotle supplies ... true reason” should also preserve C84:35–39's criticism of pre-given possibility, followed by the selective retrieval at 67.

The scalar problem is better grounded. F29 quotes the author's own question about millions or billions of citizens. C90's programme does not answer every institutional problem; C90:155 says so. Yet the absence of a demonstrated complete alternative does not invalidate criticism of existing representation. Nor does Castoriadis reject delegation as such: C90:11–15 permits it, while 65–71 targets lengthy irrevocable mandates and control over subsequent options. The final's broad rival grouping can blur that qualification even though its repair sensibly considers delegation and revocation.

The useful next test is a corpus synthesis instruction that preserves every document's distinctive argument and qualification, distinguishes reargument from presupposition, and checks earlier passages before naming innovations. The final's proposal to treat the imaginary as an orientation may be a productive reader reconstruction; it is not established merely by showing that conflict, organization, and material constraints also matter. Those mechanisms are already part of Castoriadis's account. The study should test whether the revised instruction produces a more discriminating genealogy, not merely more paired rows or more confident demands for empirical operationalization.
