## Commitment mapper: what the reader actually accepts

### Harris: eight criticisms, their downstream commitments, and the scope of refusal

A useful reading must go beyond the eight headings to show which standards and exclusions follow from abandoning Honneth's framework.

| Argument | Candidate downstream commitment in Harris's terms | Passage to inspect |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Capitalism is inadequately explained by recognition | An adequate critical theory must explain impersonal economic mechanisms and distinguish distribution from esteem; a recognition account alone cannot supply that explanation. This permits important recognition relations within capitalism. | Harris 393–446, especially “capitalism is patently not solely a recognition order” (442). |
| 2. Recognition does not supply a normative guide | Felt disrespect cannot by itself establish that a demand is justified; critical theory needs grounds for adjudicating competing demands and studying particular pathologies. | Harris 455–489; “justified and unjustified claims for recognition” (464). |
| 3. Feelings are formed through power | Subjects' reports of recognition and injustice are mediated evidence, not an immediate measure of social justice. Satisfaction may coexist with domination; absence of complaint cannot settle absence of pathology. | Harris 500–521 and 535–548; “the absence of a feeling of misrecognition or injustice” (538–539). |
| 4. Recognition capacities have constitutive conditions | An account of power must explain the formation of subjects' cognitive and recognitional capacities, including forces not exhausted by dyadic relations. Recognition's priority, as well as strict monism, is challenged here. | Harris 567–588; “subjects’ fundamental” capacity is shaped by inaccessible forces (574–578). |
| 5. The purported universal is historically particular | Recognition's applicability cannot be assumed across colonial contexts; the framework must face cases where its offered subject positions themselves reproduce domination. | Harris 589–640; “adopt European” epistemologies (619–624). Fanon's claims and Harris's endorsement must be separately attributed. |
| 6. Recognition can displace material repair | An adequate politics must retain material transformation, reparations, and land struggles; legal acknowledgment cannot be treated as sufficient emancipation or as the universal priority. | Harris 643–668; “financial” reparations and “material transformation” (657–659). |
| 7. Social pathology exceeds failed recognition | Diagnosis must include pathological normality and deformation of reason, including pathologies that help reproduce capitalism without presenting themselves as failed recognition. | Harris 691–714; “a ‘polycentric and multilateral’ account of social pathology” (708). |
| 8. Critical theory must enable transformation | Adequacy is also political and methodological: fulfilling existing institutional promises is insufficient where those institutions require structural transformation. The further claim about activist reception needs its own entitlement. | Harris 740–769; “immanent-transcendence is a catalyst for structural” transformation (756–757). |

The map should expose the dependence of these verdicts on Harris's adopted conception of critical theory. At 442–446 he calls the need to explain capitalism “plainly” tautological by reference to Horkheimer; the introduction supplies the transformative standard (69–116). This is a foundational commitment in this text, not a neutral definition every reader already shares. The historical argument about recognition monism is a second pivotal dependency: rejecting its necessity weakens the claim that reform abolishes the theory's identity (339–371). It does not automatically discharge arguments 3 and 4, or the stronger objection to reform in note 3 (828–834).

Four attribution and scope checks are especially revealing:

1. **Accepting the criticism does not entail that recognition never matters.** Harris allows recognition as “a small subcomponent of a different critical theory” (355–364), while denying that this rescues Honneth's distinctive foundation. Note 3 raises the stronger claim that Honneth's framework cannot adequately study even recognition pathologies. The reading should distinguish a phenomenon's significance from the adequacy of this particular framework.
2. **Accepting Harris does not entail adopting every detail of Thompson.** The conclusion recommends Thompson as evidence that alternatives exist, explicitly qualifying the recommendation: “Whether one agrees with Thompson’s approach, or not” (775–792).
3. **Citing an adversary is not wholesale endorsement.** Harris acknowledges borrowing Honneth's account of critical theory's legacy and explains why he sees its promise as betrayed in Honneth's own programme (793–812). This is selective appropriation, with a stated rationale.
4. **Eight objections do not make eight independent proofs.** Arguments 3/4 and 1/7 overlap. The text's cumulative case and its occasionally stronger individual sufficiency claims (726–728) should both survive the synthesis.

### Zambrana: a useful probe for productive tension

The central move is “With and against Rose” (67–75; 443–446): use Rose's account of speculative thinking to challenge Rose's judgment that Marx remains trapped in a dualistic framework. Rose's method and Rose's interpretation of Marx are separable commitments. A reading that declares their combination a contradiction has lost the argument.

Candidate entailments include: treating actuality as a coming-to-be requires relating an object's determinacy to its material historical conditions (115–131); treating fetishism and mystification as real forms of appearance rules out reducing them to errors in consciousness (281–304); and reading self-valorization through Smallwood requires bringing colonial slavery inside capital's constitutive and continuing history, rather than relegating it to a merely precapitalist stage (387–436). Zambrana's extension beyond Ramas San Miguel is expressly marked (313–321), so attribution should preserve both borrowing and revision.

Speculative thinking “tracks” rather than dissolves brokenness (50–54), requiring corrected re-reading (148–167). The engine must distinguish contradiction attributed to an object, tension used by a method, and incompatible claims the paper itself undertakes. A productive-tension row should identify the inference enabled.

### Chen: what follows from the psychologist alternative

Preserve the conditional conclusion: **if** the scientific/social analogy is retained, Chen argues for a community-based, intentionality-relative account of progress (565–580). Using “paradigm” alone cannot entail every claim Chen attributes to Kuhn.

Three candidate commitments deserve explicit treatment:

- Rejecting a neutral algorithm for progress does **not** require abandoning reasons. Chen writes “that does not” mean no reasons or standards remain (574–578); compare Kuhn's qualified defense at 513–524.
- Accepting the constitutive account commits a reader to the stronger claim that counting changes as progressive helps make them progressive, not merely that people disagree about an independently determined progress (549–564). Note 17 names the appropriation of Searle's constitutive rule (655–657).
- This creates a burden concerning which group counts, how collective judgment relates to a majority, and why intentional recognition is sufficient. Chen moves from scientific community decisions to “the majority of group members” (525–531). The reader should see the bridge and the unresolved burden, rather than receiving the analyst's invented resolution.

## Epistemology detector: how these papers earn their claims

### Zambrana: the object is a method, and the paper also enacts a method

E1 should distinguish three levels before assessing coherence: **the object** (Rose's speculative thinking and interpretation of Hegel/Marx), **the procedure enacted by Zambrana** (comparative close reading, conceptual distinctions, correction of interpretations, and extension through historical scholarship), and **the substantive claim reached** (an account of capital's actuality beyond production and wage labor).

The declared three-step procedure at 76–95 is the best anchor. It maps onto actual operations: reconstructing the speculative proposition (97–192), juxtaposing Marx with Lukács and reworking the fetishism/mystification distinction (229–306), and interpreting self-valorization with Smallwood (323–436). Philosophical textual evidence is suitable evidence for the first two operations. It does not become deficient simply because it is not interviews or numerical data. The historical extension brings a different warrant: the authority of Smallwood's historical account, including a cited lecture (the timed citation at 427), rather than archival research conducted in this paper.

“Smallwood’s work is here decisive” (388–393) names the dependence. Compare “I suggest” (189) with the later “will require” Hegel's view of actuality (443–449), while avoiding the assumption that a stronger concluding verb is itself inconsistency.

Self-application: the discussion of trade, plantation slavery, and finance substantially meets the demand to go beyond wage production (402–436). The conclusion calls for tracking their present operation (451–466) without developing a contemporary case; keep this limitation proportionate to an interpretive philosophy paper.

### Chen: whose authority, whose experience, whose “we”?

| Source or voice | Role in Chen's knowing | Local anchors and discriminating issue |
|---|---|---|
| Jaeggi | Primary object of reconstruction and critique; also credited as diagnosing difficulties in competing approaches. | 60–200, 214–247, 331–352. Do not code every quoted claim as Chen's own. Note 2 admits that the historicism critique is Chen's clarifying reconstruction (624–625). |
| Kuhn | Central authority for incommensurability, know-how, and community decisions; passages also supply an attempted reply to Chen's criticism. | 353–409 and 496–524. Preserve the know-how concession and the qualified claim about reasons. |
| Kuukkanen | Interpretive support for knowledge-how as a distributed community skill. | 400–409. Distinguish a scholar's interpretation from direct Kuhn testimony. |
| Roth, Dewey, Lakatos | Opposing proposals and standards used to assess them. | 434–446, 499–524. Roth is labeled flawed and then answered; “dismissed” should name the argument, not imply she receives no hearing. |
| Giddens | Authority for the historical account of disembedding and childhood. | 474–484. This is credited social-theoretical testimony. |
| Wellmer, Searle, Allen | Respectively support the normative/factual distinction, constitutive social facts, and the final problem of progress narratives. | 535–543, 549–580 and notes 16–17, 582–608. Searle's explicit provenance is in notes, so bibliography/footnote handling matters. |
| Enslaved people, abolitionists, families, children, political constituencies | Groups whose interests or evaluative attitudes are described; their first-person accounts are not supplied in these passages. | 427–433, 447–466, 525–531. The sharpest case is “we collectively believe” (527–531): a collective judgment is asserted without showing which collective or its testimony. |

These absences are evidential limits, not demonstrated testimonial injustices. Academic affiliation cannot identify the collective “we.”

For E5, pair Chen's demand for justification of evaluative standards (111–130) with his own declarations about progress in marriage and childhood (447–456; 525–531). Does the later intentionality account justify those judgments, explain their social existence, or both? That is the real self-application issue. Likewise, the claim that Roth's restriction contradicts Dewey (440–446) deserves a close comparison: producing new problems and producing *more serious or intractable* problems are different conditions. A source-faithful reading can flag this without claiming to settle the philosophy of progress.

### Harris as a cross-check on epistemology

Harris states his theoretical position clearly (60–116). The claim that activists and academics are abandoning Frankfurt School scholarship because of perceived neoliberal apologism (758–769) supplies a sharper evidential-limit probe: it gives no reception study or participant account. Acknowledging assertive tone (793–807) does not supply that evidence.

## Corpus mode: candidate genealogies and falsification checks

Cross-document findings need two verbatim anchors with distinct document keys. Locate claims per document, then trace persistence, changed application, revision, or divergence. Recurrence is not proof of intellectual causation. Do not invent an order between the two 2001 Deutschmann papers, or treat the supplied Castoriadis edition dates as established composition dates.

**Deutschmann.** A strong shared-score candidate is that money's open-ended command over possibilities drives growth through socially organized creativity, while the boundary between mundane and transcendent becomes mobile. Anchors: [2001 entrepreneurship paper](../../data/study/sources_ideas/deutschmann2001_capitalism_as_religion.md), 51, 75, 99–103; [2001 absolute-wealth paper](../../data/study/sources_ideas/deutschmann2001_promise_of_absolute_wealth.md), 70 and 128; [2022 chapter](../../data/study/sources_ideas/deutschmann2022_interpretation_of_capitalism_as_religion.md), 69–75. Compare “the option to have options” (entrepreneurship, 51) with the later account of expanding private-property possibilities (2022, 75).

The later chapter explicitly foregrounds society's inability to observe itself as a whole and compares Luhmann and Hayek (39), while distinguishing a sociologist's secular critique from theology (13–15). This may be a changed explanatory emphasis, not a newly discovered epistemic problem: the earlier texts already discuss non-observability and uncertain representations (entrepreneurship, 99–101; absolute wealth, 22–26). Likewise, the 2022 appeal for restraint and containment (83–87) has an earlier counterpart in rejecting new utopias and the obsession with absolute wealth (absolute wealth, 136). A genealogy that reports an unqualified late turn from optimism to sobriety would miss that continuity. Selective use of Marx while rejecting a quantitative law of value is explicit in 2001 (entrepreneurship, 75); agreement with a cited thinker must remain claim-specific.

**Castoriadis.** A promising line connects the social institution of needs and ends to the critique of capitalist mastery and the project of democratic autonomy. [Technique](../../data/study/sources_ideas/castoriadis1984_technique.md), 81, says there are “no obligatory problems”; 83–87 treats technique and social organization as constitutive, rather than neutral tools serving fixed needs. [What Democracy?](../../data/study/sources_ideas/castoriadis1990_what_democracy.md), 33 and 41, links autonomy to self-limitation and effective participation; 115 contrasts the imaginaries of mastery and autonomy. [The “Rationality” of Capitalism](../../data/study/sources_ideas/castoriadis1997_rationality_of_capitalism.md), 52 and 64, characterizes expanding mastery while treating internal contestation as a condition of capitalism's development. P6 should notice the explicit conditions/consequences distinction (1997, 54).

Two cross-document checks prevent a flattened anti-capitalism summary. First, the 1990 account explicitly permits a genuine market within democratic economic organization (151); criticism of capitalism therefore does not automatically entail rejection of every market. Second, the 1997 essay applies the circularity of instituted values to an autonomous society itself (23), maintaining that no society can ground its justification outside itself. Pair this with democratic self-limitation (1990, 33–41) to test whether an output can explain a retained burden without falsely manufacturing a recantation. “Analysis can go as far as to pose these kinds of questions” (1997, 164) marks a practical limit the synthesis should preserve.
