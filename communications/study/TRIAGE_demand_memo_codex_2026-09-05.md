# Engine-catalogue triage: demand memo (Codex, 2026-09-05)

The strongest unmet demand is **a comparison of the supplied cases on shared questions, with the evidence and limits of each comparison preserved**. The catalogue offers several ways to audit an argument's comparisons, concepts and organization, but sometimes sells those as a comparison of the world described by the documents. The briefs then extend this into decisions about the requester's business without the business evidence. These are different gaps: a method/corpus gap, a promise-scope gap, and a handoff gap. Adding more engine names will not fix all three.

This is a file-based demand audit, not an execution-quality assessment or a second supply-side triage. I read the requested studies first, the dossier catalogue/brief/desks, both complete saved sample briefs, capability questions, and selected adjacent definition cards. Counts below are from local files at base HEAD `e7fb336d896e119a7fd53339e5fb2834d711866e`. I made no API or model calls and did not fetch live jobs. The owner's supplied context is **14 executor jobs, a service two days old**, at the [executor jobs endpoint](https://the-analyst-kcuc.onrender.com/v1/executor/jobs). That is too thin to infer demand, engine quality, or retirement priorities; executor jobs are not necessarily independent customer choices. Sample promises are evidence of what the product offers, not proof that their substantive claims are true.

## 1. What can actually be offered

[common.py:engine_catalog](../../src/dossier/common.py) enumerates **capability YAMLs**, not every registered JSON definition. [catalog.py:purpose_catalog](../../src/dossier/catalog.py) joins those keys to [catalog_purpose.json](../../src/dossier/catalog_purpose.json), excludes `aoi_` and `genealogy_`, drops stale purpose entries, and puts otherwise executable unplaced engines in “More.” The files contain **28 capability definitions, 22 purpose entries, six excluded engines, zero stale entries and zero “More” engines**. Thus all 22 below can be offered, subject to corpus-fit guidance. The broader JSON estate is not the user's picker.

`fit=off` remains visible in the catalogue; the brief prompt forbids selecting it. `check_brief` and `validate_steps` validate executable keys, not these semantic fit conditions. “Conditional” is also guidance, not an enforced upstream-input contract. `deliverable_hints` exists in the JSON but has no consumer in the inspected dossier code: it is editorial metadata, not routing. The executable recipe filter checks engine availability, not whether its promised use follows from those engines.

Recipe roles do not create a separate analytical method. `purpose_catalog` constructs steps without preserving the recipe's `role` field; path validation allows a trailing synthesis-engine repeat. Current surface routes for the four redesigned engines omit the critic. Do not read an attractive recipe title or pass-count estimate as assurance that every output has been checked.

## 2. What BriefOption v2 promises, and where those promises land

[schemas.py:BriefOption](../../src/dossier/schemas.py) and [brief.py](../../src/dossier/brief.py) ask for exactly three `you_will_understand` statements, two or three verb-first `you_will_be_able_to` statements, and three or four `questions_answered`. Each understanding/ability points through `supported_by` to a proposed section, table or figure. Tables specify a row unit, columns, expected rows and carrying documents. `not_for` and `evidence_base.thin_or_missing` state limits. `path.steps[].contributes` explains each engine's contribution in reader terms.

**There is no explicit promise → engine → finding support relation.** A valid `supported_by` checks that a shape element exists. It does not establish that its contents will answer the promise; `questions_answered` does not even require a support reference. The brief model sees reconnaissance and the purpose catalogue, before the engine readings. Its current checks include lexical concreteness, vocabulary and overlap heuristics, but no model-based audit of whether a path plus the available evidence can keep each promise. Substituting a fallback engine also does not reassess the surviving promises.

The saved [fashion](../changes/brief-v2-samples/fashion.json) and [state-capitalism](../changes/brief-v2-samples/state_capitalism.json) files contain six options, **16 understandings, 11 abilities and 24 questions**. Some strings end in ellipses, and several options fall below the requested promise counts after repair. I assess the visible text without reconstructing lost endings. In the following tables, U/A/Q numbers are one-based positions in those arrays. “Maps” means method-level fit, not verified substantive truth.

## 3. What the desks consume, including what falls out of the handoff

| Consumer | What it receives and can use | What it cannot infer or mechanically guarantee |
|---|---|---|
| [Spine](../../src/dossier/spine.py), `_option_text`, `_user`, `build_spine` | Chosen promises/questions, analysis prose, compact profiles, `analysis_ledger(job, docs)`; plans a thesis, section claims, row sets and diagrams; sections may name `finding_ids`. Enumerations can become tables; explicit dependencies, contrasts and temporal relations can become diagrams. | It receives no full source text in `_user`, despite source-checking planned quotes later. `finding_ids` is optional; code drops unknown IDs but does not prove support. `_option_text` omits the option's `not_for` and `evidence_base` fields, so limits do not travel as explicitly as promises. |
| [Tables](../../src/dossier/tables.py), `_spine_user`, `_specs_text` | Section claim, row unit, columns, `carries_claims`, planned anchors and finding IDs; reverified ledger, prose, profiles and full documents when total text is at most 600,000 characters. Inventories of claims/terms/cases and comparisons with evidence for their cells are directly useful. | A row needs at least one surviving quote, not proof of every cell or every comparison side. Dimensions and case coverage still require judgment. Above the corpus limit, it relies on supplied anchors. Fewer than two surviving rows makes a commissioned table unavailable, not an analytical failure. Legacy tables use prose/profiles, without the dedicated ledger block. |
| [Figures](../../src/dossier/figures.py), `material_text`, `validate_spec` | Named entities and relations from tables, ledger, prose and profiles; spine commissions an analytical primitive, format and `picture_shows`. Useful inputs specify who/what relates, relation direction/type, and dates/stages where applicable. | **Calls `analysis_ledger(job)` without documents.** Ledger quotes are not reverified there; the figure wall checks against assembled material, which includes analysis and the brief's telling, not the source corpus. Presence of labels/quotes does not establish an edge, causal arrow, scale position or forecast. A concept list alone supplies nodes, not their relations. |

[common.py:analysis_ledger](../../src/dossier/common.py) is a useful but lossy adapter:

- It parses each phase's final `## Findings ledger`, excludes the sections headed `### Rejected by the critic` and `### Check receipt`, and considers up to **160 parsed rows in phase order**. With documents, quotes are checked against a source index; multiple anchors and corpus provenance participate in verification. Unverified/incomplete anchors are separated as `near` quotations, with instructions to re-find the source sentence. A parsed row with neither an anchor nor an anchor parse error has no dedicated rendered entry. This is quote/shape checking, not assessment that a quote supports a finding or that a critic ruled on it.
- The rendered entry preserves the finding ID, engine label, finding sentence and anchors. It **does not preserve dimension, confidence, status, `from` lineage, or separate relation fields such as `depends`/`supports`**. Relations survive reliably in this block only when stated in the finding sentence. Raw final output is also sent as analysis prose, so fields can still be found there; this is not a first-class relation handoff. Multiple anchors lose their role suffixes in the rendered ledger, so counter-evidence versus primary support needs explicit wording.
- `ledger_ids` combines bare IDs across phases; it does not add a phase namespace and is not restricted to the displayed 160 rows. Distinct phases reusing `F1` can therefore make an existent ID ambiguous. Structural scope reports and negative/inconclusive assessments are not rendered as a separate desk contract. “No qualifying finding” can be a correct result, as the argument-family controls show.
- Rejected rows are excluded from the dedicated ledger, but `analysis_prose` still carries raw final output, including rejected-row receipts and potentially stale prose references. Do not say the desks never see rejected material. The figure material additionally truncates its ledger at 24,000 characters.

The desk need is therefore **anchored, attributable findings with explicit relations and usable row units**, plus corpus coverage and qualified negatives. It is not “every engine must produce a novel positive discovery.” A sound inventory can ground a reading guide or table; a single finding may need only prose; no suitable relation means no figure. Promised row counts and figure budgets should not become finding quotas.

## 5. Offered engines for which no purpose needs a separate engine

**No literal orphan exists:** all 22 offered engines have a named purpose; none is stranded in “More.” That metadata was written by the supply side, however, so it is not independent evidence that 22 separate methods are needed. Eleven are outside all recipes: `chapter_role_analyzer`, `concept_centrality_mapper`, `concept_semantic_constellation`, `concept_synthesis`, `conceptual_framework_extraction`, `conditions_of_possibility_analyzer`, `evolution_tactics_detector`, `modal_reasoning_analyzer`, `narrative_structure_analyzer`, `specialized_reasoning_classifier`, and `theory_construction_analyzer`. Two of these—centrality and synthesis—are selected in the samples. Missing recipe membership is not grounds for retirement.

The demand-side candidates for consolidation are more specific:

- **`specialized_reasoning_classifier`:** no sampled promise needs a standalone classification of schools/forms. Its probabilistic, strategic, normative and performative-consistency questions may be useful argument/method dimensions or critic skills. Keep a standalone offering only if triage can name the resulting reader question and artifact beyond classification.
- **`concept_synthesis`:** integration is needed, but no sampled use requires its author-genealogy assumptions. Treat synthesis as a role/corpus method with declared inputs; retain a separate specialized method only if its developmental question remains distinct.
- **`structural_pattern_detector`:** the promised empirical playbook is needed, but the current discourse-structure remit substantially overlaps narrative, chapter and argument analysis. Choose the actual text-structure question or rewrite for the case-comparison demand; do not preserve the mismatch because the recipe is popular-looking.
- **Centrality/taxonomy/framework/theory and evolution/appropriation/tactics:** compare outputs before consolidation. “Which concept is essential?” differs from “what job does this term do?”; meaning change differs from a textual revision device. Overlap alone does not establish interchangeability. Each retained standalone method should serve a distinct reader question; recurring checks should be shared.

Demand does not currently justify automatic retirement of any other offered engine. Use these mappings alongside the model's whole-catalogue judgments to select methods and repair promises **before** migration, with later source-led validation deciding whether a rewritten engine actually keeps its promise.
