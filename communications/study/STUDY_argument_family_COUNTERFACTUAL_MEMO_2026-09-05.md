### Ganzinger — both finals completed

Source: [Hegel's Concept of the Concept](../../data/study/sources_ideas/hegels_concept_of_the_concept_2026.txt), abbreviated G.

The [original final](../../data/study/argument_family_2026_09_05/530df62823ec1915/outputs/counterfactual_analyzer__original__ganzinger.md) already handles the genre appropriately: its opening distinguishes conceptual dependence from alternate history and rejects mandatory world-distance, butterfly-effect and numerical-probability analysis. F4 attributes the philosophy-of-nature requirement to metaphysical interpreters, rather than Ganzinger. F15–17 locate the disputed transition to necessary self-division, while counter-evidence F23 recovers the internal-universal mechanism and F24 the explicit response to nullity (G:636–650, 711–754). The original therefore does not merely treat an unsupported assertion as the entire argument.

The [candidate final](../../data/study/argument_family_2026_09_05/530df62823ec1915/outputs/counterfactual_analyzer__candidate__ganzinger.md) makes the conditional structure more useful. F3 correctly identifies “Once one accepts” as an endorsed antecedent, not an even-if concession or automatic burden shift (G:668–678). Its fixed/changed-condition analysis names the additional requirement that the formative act enter the resulting concept, and distinguishes this conceptual requirement from temporal inevitability. F7 reconstructs Pippin's actual alternative: original complexity changes the starting point instead of independently explaining the first generation of difference (G:451–485). The counter-evidence preserves note 11's qualification of external synthesis, and F6 includes note 12's limit on starting every transition anew from empty unity.

F9 is the strongest added analytical result: failure of the positive emptiness-to-content derivation does not by itself defeat the separately argued objection that reification reintroduces an epistemic gap (G:368–377). Conversely, that objection does not establish the positive derivation or prove the metaphysical interpretation's exclusivity. This is an explained distinction between support routes, beyond a new robustness label. F8's later-synthesis survivor is more limited: it preserves a conditional explanation **once determinations exist**, not an independently established route to their first production.

A reference range weakens the otherwise careful ownership. The survival prose says the stronger positive conclusion “depends on [F1]–[F5],” which includes F4—the opponent's reported and rejected philosophy-of-nature requirement. That requirement is not a premise of Ganzinger's positive content derivation. The surrounding tables usually distinguish the routes correctly; the inclusive reference should not inherit an inference its prose does not justify.

F7 is retained with an unverified anchor, and its scope is accordingly inconclusive. The relevant source passage is present across the page break at G:451–459, with editorial capitalization, and the ensuing discussion explicitly supports the interpretation. The failure of the displayed quotation to pass the current wall is an evidence-presentation limitation, not proof that the alternative was invented. The candidate's main improvement is more explicit source-based conditional comparison and surviving support; the original's genre restraint and mechanism counter-evidence remain substantial strengths.

### Elling — both finals completed

Source: [Amphibian Habits](../../data/study/sources_ideas/elling2025_amphibian_habits_hegel_second_nature.txt), abbreviated E.

The [original final](../../data/study/argument_family_2026_09_05/530df62823ec1915/outputs/counterfactual_analyzer__original__elling.md) already distinguishes the conceptual inquiry from empirical causal generalization, recognizes the even-if fully free society and its “whether or not” qualification (F17–19), and retains actual unfreedom's contingency. These are not candidate discoveries. Its weaker first row, however, calls avoiding or removing habit a central rejected requirement for freedom. E:144–157 presents the strong critical view as acknowledging habit's freedom-enabling role while insisting that new unfreedom accompanies it; this is not an advocated anti-habit prescription. If the row intended an analyst's derived conditional, it needed to mark that role rather than assign this central test to Elling.

Original F16 also treats the “only way” to recover objective fixity as stronger than surrounding risk language warrants. E:606–609 adds the specific goal of escaping subjective instability through objective fixity; a necessary means conditional on pursuing that goal can coexist with contingent entry into that response. The exclusivity claim is contestable, but the difference from general risk language alone does not establish the objection.

The [candidate final](../../data/study/argument_family_2026_09_05/530df62823ec1915/outputs/counterfactual_analyzer__candidate__elling.md) gives more useful conditional comparisons. F4 preserves Hegel's explicit rejection of the modern aging analogy (E:441–456). F6 identifies habitual embodiment as held fixed when institutional adequacy is granted, avoiding the inference that all subjective problems were assumed away. F10 and F12 explain why the general spiritual-risk route survives withdrawal of historical finality or stagnation (E:635–642, 925–930); they do not pretend that this independently establishes the historical likelihood of authoritarian submission. The tooth-brushing alternative is appropriately limited to its object and scale, and the candidate does not invent a habit-versus-no-habit recommendation.

The remaining problems concern the same modal discipline. Added F14 says the earlier/modern contrast qualifies the **necessity** of the threat. The cited contrast concerns degrees of externalization and intensity of ambiguity; E:355–396 and 631–634 retain structural susceptibility while varying its historical salience. The scope report's qualitative-difference language is more defensible than the row's stronger formulation.

F7 proposes an additional subjective response before stagnation could cause spiritual death. This is a reasonable issue to investigate, but its presented warrant needs refinement: E:422–424 already supplies a dependence of individual vitality on living practices and explicitly states the stronger arrested-progress conditional. General contingency under habituation (E:391–396) does not by itself rebut necessity under that additional condition. The analysis should test that dependency or distinguish the relevant cases, rather than assume every later contingency statement weakens the earlier conditional. The candidate marks its step as reconstructed, which should be preserved when assessing it.

All five candidate scope reviews are reported supported. That status is consistent with retained findings, but does not resolve the F14 interpretation or certify the proposed F7 reconstruction. The candidate's strongest gains are explicit aligned comparisons and independent survival; the original already performs much of the important modal reading.

### Harris — both finals completed

Source: [Eight Arguments Against Honneth](../../data/study/sources_ideas/harris2026_eight_arguments_against_honneth.txt), abbreviated H.

The [original final](../../data/study/argument_family_2026_09_05/530df62823ec1915/outputs/counterfactual_analyzer__original__harris.md) identifies a particularly useful actual counterfactual: Harris's suggestion that Honneth would have conceded if reconstruction were possible (F3/4; H:325–332). Honneth's refusal does not by itself prove logical impossibility. The reading also distinguishes the definition-based identity claim from broader causal reception claims, preserves reparations displacement as a risk (F19), Fromm's reception explanation as partial (F22), and recent Honneth developments in counter-evidence. These are strong source-sensitive checks.

The all-to-any critique needs a more developed reconstruction. The original expressly names additional explanatory-priority and omitted-structural-cause premises, and F12 recognizes antecedent power; it does not entirely miss the separate route. But its blanket description of those premises as asserted rather than independently demonstrated underdevelops H:567–578's reasoning about the formation of recognitional capacities, maintained even where other approaches are used. Note 3 (H:828–834) explicitly connects the stronger claim to that criticism. Its adequacy is contestable, but the reader should assess this route rather than reduce the issue to extrapolation from counterexamples against monism.

Two other claims need qualification. Calling Harris inconsistent for combining multicausal subject formation with a direct account of theoretical influence requires showing that the latter is asserted as exhaustive; a partial contribution can coexist with causal complexity. F23's preferred successor package also needs H:786's “Whether one agrees with Thompson’s approach, or not”: Thompson is offered as evidence that alternatives exist, rather than an obligatory complete replacement programme.

The [candidate final](../../data/study/argument_family_2026_09_05/530df62823ec1915/outputs/counterfactual_analyzer__candidate__harris.md) materially improves that reconstruction. F1 explicitly grants the non-monist revision while preserving the explanatory-priority and subject-formation objection. F9 and the related prose identify the disputed no-supplementation premise, so independent survival does not become an unexplained all-eight-arguments claim. F12 distinguishes the failure of one monism argument from the survival of other criticisms. F13 correctly explains that withdrawing Thompson's particular proposal leaves the negative criticisms available (H:775–792). These are substantive gains over the original's underdeveloped alternative route, not the first recognition that any additional premises exist.

The checker introduces serious inconsistencies. It rejects F4 because the pathological-recognition examples contain no literal conditional or suppositional form. H:535–548 nevertheless derives the possibility of successful recognition that remains pathological and uses this as a counterexample to recognition's adequacy. Calling this a reductio rather than a reconstructed counterexample is debatable; absence of an explicit if–then is not a sound reason to exclude its inferential work. The preceding prose still analyzes F4, so the delivered product contains a prose/ledger disagreement rather than simply omitting that reading.

F5, F7 and F11 are rejected as non-verbatim because their code-trimmed anchors end mid-sentence. A source prefix can be verbatim without being a complete sentence; its adequacy for the claim is a separate issue. Added F15 and F16 reproduce the same displayed trimmed anchors as rejected F7 and F11 respectively, while preceding prose continues to cite the rejected IDs. The final therefore presents unchanged evidence as both disqualifying and sufficient. F14 usefully adds the genuine Honneth-would-have-conceded counterfactual, but does not examine its weak behavioral-to-logical bridge as the original's F3/4 do. The added instance is coverage, not an improved test of its warrant.

The survival scope is marked inconclusive after an added row is dropped, despite retained F12/F13 and substantial robustness prose. That administrative status should not replace assessment of the surviving argument. Conversely, the strengthened route analysis does not excuse the rejected-ID references or source-prefix rationale. This pair combines a clear gain in independent support with check-induced regressions and a weaker treatment of one important actual counterfactual.

Here, the other reasons and direct source reading establish the narrow negative. The result does not infer absence in the folders' unknown contents or treat a missing extraction as successful analysis.

### Initial reader assessment

The candidate's most consistent gain is explicit comparison of conditions and surviving support, particularly Ganzinger's independently supported anti-reification objection and Harris's explanatory-priority route. The original already supplies substantial genre restraint and modal qualifications. Candidate scope labels do not settle source fidelity: the Elling necessity qualification remains too strong, Harris's checker mishandles inferred counterexamples and truncated quotations, and several delivered references fail to coordinate with their final ledger.
