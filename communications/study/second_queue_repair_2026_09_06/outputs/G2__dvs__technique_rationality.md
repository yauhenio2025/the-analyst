# Technique, rationality, and the politics of ends

## Position map

The most consequential continuity across the two works is the rejection of efficiency as a sufficient account of reason. **“Technique” (1984)** begins with the historical meanings of *techne* but ultimately argues that technique is neither a neutral instrument nor an autonomous cause: it is a constitutive dimension of society’s creation of its world. Its distinctive role is to relocate technology inside social-historical creation, conflict, and political responsibility. Its chief qualification is scalar: technical activity considered by itself can be judged solely by efficiency, even though neutrality becomes untenable at the social-historical level [F1].

**“The ‘Rationality’ of Capitalism” (1997)** argues that capitalism legitimates itself through a historically specific, quantitative conception of rationality—maximizing its own definition of product while minimizing its own definition of cost [F2]. Its distinctive role is immanent critique: it tests capitalist theory and practice against capitalism’s own claims to rational coherence [F3]. Its qualification is that productive effectiveness is real but restricted: capitalism efficiently pursues a particular output while excluding environmental and human damage from its accounting [F4].

The dated order, 1984 and 1997, permits a chronological comparison. It does not by itself demonstrate that the later essay directly takes up the earlier one. The supported trajectory is therefore a **chronological extension of an argument**, not documented textual inheritance: the earlier work politicizes technique by refusing the separation of means, ends, and social organization; the later work makes that same means/ends problem central to its critique of capitalist “rationality.”

## The local inventory of “Technique”

### From fabrication to social creation

“Technique” first prevents the concept from collapsing into modern machinery. Its linguistic reconstruction traces *techne* from “production” and “material fabrication” toward effective making/doing, know-how, and method [F5]. The older material sense is not simply abandoned. Rather, the semantic expansion establishes that technique has always included relations among production, knowledge, efficacy, and action.

The essay then reconstructs a stronger meaning through Plato and Aristotle: technical making concerns what might be otherwise and can bring something into existence. Castoriadis radicalizes this inheritance with “absolute creation.” A technical object is not merely a changed natural object; creating one means “to constitute a universal type, to posit an eidos” [F6]. “Absolute,” however, is relational rather than ex nihilo. Technique begins from existing materials and uses natural regularities, but the organization, end, and universal type need have no natural precedent. This qualification prevents “creation” from meaning production out of nothing.

The concept expands again from objects to worlds. Technique is “an essential dimension” of every form of social life because it helps constitute what each society accepts as its “real-rational.” That move is condensed in the topological metaphor: technique is “neither an instrument nor a cause” but an “everywhere dense sub-set” [F9]. The metaphor’s force is analytical. Technique cannot be removed from society and placed beside it as either a tool that society freely uses or a mechanism that independently determines society.

This expanded meaning does not erase narrower uses. The text concedes that technical activity, “considered in and for itself,” evaluates means by efficiency. A bridge can be well or badly calculated regardless of whether the bridge ought to be built. Yet the concession is local: once the object becomes a society’s aggregate technological ensemble, neutrality is “sophistical” [F1]. Installations, routines, specialized agents, knowledge, work organization, and significations constrain what can be chosen and what kinds of relations can be sustained.

### Against technological determinism

The Marxist thesis supplies the essay’s principal adversary: the state of productive forces determines relations of production and, indirectly, social organization. The response does not deny association. A technique or organization of labor may “go together with” a type of social life. It denies that association establishes a separable determinant and determined term [F15].

The “technical fact” cannot be isolated because a technical object exists only in a technical ensemble and through the bodily and mental dexterities that make it usable [F12]. Those ensembles are themselves embedded in economic and social organization. Consequently, neither the galley nor slavery can be assigned a simple one-way causal priority; nor can capitalist technique be separated from the capitalist economy whose practices, institutions, and conflicts make it operative.

This argument also explains why social organization counts as a technique without being reducible to one. The “regulated network of social relations” is called the mightiest engine, but the institution remains “far more than technique” [F19]. Organizational procedures are technical components of an instituted world, not the total meaning of the institution.

### Technique as instrument and stake of conflict

The non-determinist account does not reduce the relation between society and technology to vague mutual influence. In capitalist production, the selection and organization of techniques follow a specific conflict. Management seeks to depend “not on men, but on machines” when workers struggle over speed, quality, and working conditions [F20]. Technology is therefore both an instrument and a stake of class struggle. It rearranges power in production, while conflict helps determine which possibilities from the available technical range are adopted.

The political conclusion follows from this constitutive account. If technique embodies an organization of work and a form of social life, changing ownership while retaining the same technology cannot suffice. “Nowadays, it is technology itself” that is beginning to be called into question [F17]. A socialist transformation would require workers and technical staff consciously to transform the labor process and the technical ensemble. The essay does not claim to possess a blueprint: universality of production, economies of scale, communications, ecological balance, and the size of self-governing communities remain open political problems.

## The local inventory of “The ‘Rationality’ of Capitalism”

### From rational operation to capitalist legitimation

The later essay begins with a deliberately minimal definition: rationality is conformity of an operation to a goal [F7]. This definition immediately opens a gap between the rationality of means and the rationality of the goal. Weberian instrumental rationality takes the goal as granted, so it has “no value in itself” [F16]. The examples of poison and the H-bomb demonstrate that greater efficiency can intensify rather than overcome the horror of an end [F13].

Capitalist ideology nevertheless transforms this limited operational standard into a principle of social legitimacy. It identifies welfare with an economic maximum or claims that welfare will follow from one. Rationality is thereby reduced to maximizing a regime-defined “product” and minimizing regime-defined “costs” [F2]. The qualification “regime-defined” is decisive: capitalist calculation does not neutrally discover what counts as output or cost. The institution establishes the categories and their monetary valuations.

The concept then acquires a broader social-historical form. Capitalism is characterized not by accumulation alone but by continual transformation of production and labor to increase output and reduce cost [F21]. Castoriadis connects this process to mastery, then describes capitalism’s core imaginary as the unlimited extension of “rational mastery” [F10]. Production is its initial but not exclusive field. The same signification extends into consumption, education, law, political life, the state, and the formation of calculative subjects.

This expansion is qualified twice. First, mastery is not exclusive to capitalism; other societies organized around conquest display it as well. Capitalism’s specificity lies in its quantitative, calculative means and unlimited social extension. Second, the quotation marks around “rational mastery” block its acceptance as genuine or universal reason. It names what capitalism institutes as rational, not an external validation of that institution.

### Immanent rather than merely moral criticism

The essay’s method is explicitly immanent. It asks whether economic theory is coherent and whether the capitalist economy resembles what the theory says about it [F3]. This distinguishes the argument from a critique based only on externally asserted values.

Several targets follow. Separate imputation falsely treats social products as if they could be assigned to isolated individuals, firms, capital, or labor, although production depends on collective cooperation. *Homo oeconomicus* fictitiously converts persons and organizations into perfect calculating machines. Mathematical economics treats heterogeneous and discrete magnitudes as measurable continuous functions. Equilibrium theory turns determinate mathematical solutions into a picture of optimized social reality.

Against these constructions, the essay offers approximate regularities, uncertain expectations, oligopolies, incomplete information, bureaucratic conflict, worker resistance, and technically unforeseeable change. It does not claim that economic life has no regularities. Rather, their interpretation belongs to practical judgment—an “art”—instead of a system of rigorous universal laws. This is another continuity with “Technique”: both works reject univocal determination without denying patterned relations.

### Relative efficiency and excluded costs

The strongest counter-position within the later essay is capitalism’s demonstrated ability to “deliver the goods.” Castoriadis does not answer by denying all productive success. He narrows its force. Capitalism pursues “a certain kind of production” and counts costs restrictively, excluding environmental destruction, damaged lives, degraded work, and urban ugliness [F4]. Its technology is adequate to ends such as control, mass consumption, and elimination of the producer’s human role, but success by those standards cannot establish comprehensive rationality.

Capitalist resilience also depends on forces that resisted its own spontaneous logic. Working-class struggles forced wage increases and reductions in working time, expanding consumer demand; political reactions produced state intervention and demand management. Thus the system’s long postwar expansion cannot be attributed to pure capitalist rationality. It depended partly on constraints imposed against employers and orthodox economic doctrine.

The later phase, dated in the essay from approximately the early 1970s, retains the earlier structural problems while globalization and political changes upset the framework. Neoliberalism removes controls created through prior struggles, frees capital movement, and privileges short-term profit. Under those conditions the text says there is “no point” in discussing capitalist rationality [F18]. This is not a claim that calculation disappears. It means that the remaining calculation—predominantly short-term profit-seeking—cannot sustain the regime’s larger claim to rational legitimacy.

The strongest counter-passage to a simple thesis of total non-neutrality is the earlier concession that technique “does appear as value-free” in a restricted respect [F1]. The strongest counter-passage to a thesis that the later work simply dismisses capitalist achievement is its acknowledgment that capitalism increases a certain production and reduces its chosen costs [F4]. These passages do not overturn the shared argument. They define its exact structure: local efficacy is real, but its standards are insufficient for evaluating a social order.

What is settled is that both works preserve the distinction between operational efficiency and substantive judgment. “Technique” embeds technical efficacy in social creation and struggle; “The ‘Rationality’ of Capitalism” shows how capitalism elevates that restricted efficacy into an ideology of legitimacy. What remains unsettled is whether the 1997 formulation was consciously developed through the 1984 essay, rather than independently restating and extending a longstanding position. The supplied dates establish order, but no citation or uptake passage links the two texts directly.

## Judgment

Across the collection, the core position persists: no society can derive its ends, legitimacy, or organization from the efficiency of its means. In 1984, this claim reorganizes the concept of technique. Technique becomes creative, socially embedded, conflictual, and politically transformable. In 1997, the claim reorganizes the critique of capitalism. Capitalism converts an instrumental criterion into a historically specific ideology of quantitative mastery, while its actual operation violates even its own theoretical image.

The later essay therefore broadens and sharpens the earlier problem without demonstrably replacing it. The concept shifts from the **social being of technical means** to the **capitalist legitimation of means-oriented reason**. Its argumentative role persists: both concepts expose the political choices concealed when an instituted social orientation presents itself as neutral necessity.

### Counter-evidence

- [F1] preserves a genuine local neutrality concession: isolated technical operations can be judged by efficiency.
- [F4] preserves a genuine productive-efficiency concession: capitalism is effective relative to selected outputs and restricted costs.
- [F15] permits association between technical ensembles and social forms even while rejecting univocal determination.
- [F19] prevents the argument from reducing all institution and social meaning to technique.

### Open questions

- Do the 1997 formulations directly take up “Technique” (1984), or do both restate a broader position developed elsewhere?
- How exactly should an autonomous society evaluate ends when its criteria must themselves be instituted?
- What technical ensemble could reconcile worker control, ecological balance, universal production, and local adaptation?
- How far does “rational mastery” function as a metaphor, and how far as a literal account of institutional power?
