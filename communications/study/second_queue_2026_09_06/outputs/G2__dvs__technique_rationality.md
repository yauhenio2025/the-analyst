# Reading: Technique and capitalist rationality across the two works

## Position map

The most consequential continuity is that both works reject the treatment of technology or economic rationality as a neutral, autonomous mechanism. They instead locate both within a society’s instituted ends, values, conflicts, and forms of mastery. The important difference is argumentative scale. **“Technique” (1984)** reconstructs technique as creative and constitutive making/doing, then uses that reconstruction to reject both technological determinism and neutral instrumentalism. **“The ‘Rationality’ of Capitalism” (1997)** concentrates on capitalism’s claim to rational legitimacy and argues that its allegedly rational economy reduces reason to quantitative maximization and rests on fictive theoretical assumptions. The later work applies rather than abandons the earlier critique of neutrality: what “Technique” states about the inseparability of means and ends becomes an immanent critique of capitalism’s own definitions of products, costs, efficiency, and welfare [F1].

These dates establish publication order—1984 and 1997—but not by themselves an argumentative trajectory. There is no explicit citation of “Technique” in the 1997 text and therefore no demonstrated uptake between the supplied works. The comparison supports persistence, sharpening, and a change of principal object, not a claim of inevitable intellectual development.

## 1. The local inventory of “Technique”

### From effective fabrication to creative institution

“Technique” begins by loosening the opposition between speech and tools. Both detach activity from its immediate context, establish distinctive orders and temporalities, and gain a certain mastery over users who nevertheless employ them. The concept therefore cannot initially be confined to material instruments.

The semantic history of *techne* supplies the first reconstruction. The term moves from fabrication to “making/doing which is effective, adequate in general,” including know-how and method [F6]. Its proximity to *episteme* stresses established knowledge, while its oppositions to education, chance, and nature preserve its practical and productive boundaries. Plato’s *poiesis* and the Aristotelian creative disposition then connect technique with bringing about what may or may not exist and whose origin resides in the maker.

The text subsequently radicalizes this inheritance. In crucial cases, technical production does not imitate a natural model. A technical creation constitutes a type that has no natural equivalent, although it remains materially constrained and makes use of physical regularities [F6]. “Absolute” therefore does not mean creation without conditions. It means absence of a precedent that already specifies the technical object’s form, operation, purpose, and combination of materials.

This meaning performs a larger argument. Technique actively discovers and combines possibilities that otherwise would remain separate. It helps divide reality into what resists every intervention and what resists only in determinate ways. Because no fixed list of human problems or needs dictates which possibilities must be actualized, technique also participates in forming historical needs. The text’s image of technique as “the pick” digging the gulf between biological necessities and historical needs is immediately declared inadequate: technique is not simply a tool used by an independently constituted imaginary, since its particular forms help determine what is dug.

### Technique as a dimension of the social world

The decisive expansion occurs when technique becomes part of society’s constitution of its “real-rational”: what that society posits as imposing itself upon it. This category is explicitly variable; magic can belong to one society’s real-rational. Technique is therefore not identical with modern scientific calculation. It participates in each society’s practical articulation of reality.

The same expansion prevents reduction of a technical fact to an object. An object is technical only within an ensemble of tools, gestures, bodily and mental dexterities, economic relations, and social significations. Social organization itself can consequently count as a technique: coordinated relations and divisions of labour operate as productive arrangements. Yet the institution remains “far more than technique.” The expansion does not make every institutional meaning merely technical.

This is why the text’s governing metaphor is topological rather than mechanical: technique is an “everywhere dense sub-set” of social creation [F2]. Density indicates presence throughout the constitution of the real-rational; it does not assign technique an independent causal sovereignty.

### Neutrality, determination, and politics

The concept’s chief negative role is to dissolve the antithesis between a neutral instrument and an autonomous destiny. At the narrow level, technical activity does evaluate means through efficiency while bracketing the value of the proposed end. But the aggregate technological ensemble cannot be detached from installations, routines, knowledge, specialists, values, and forms of authority. Hence the text’s stronger social-historical conclusion: “ends and means, significations and instruments, efficiency and value cannot be separated” [F3].

The same reasoning opposes technological determinism. A material object cannot determine a social form when its identity and operation already presuppose skills, institutions, conflicts, and economic arrangements. Association is not determination. The text consequently criticizes both a Marxist account in which productive forces determine social organization and an inverse account in which technique becomes an autonomous catastrophe.

Its political outcome follows directly. Emancipation cannot consist solely in transferring control of an unchanged technological apparatus. It requires conscious transformation of the work process by workers and technical staff, so that work might become a field of collective creativity. This proposal remains qualified: neither a return to traditional techniques nor quasi-autarkic small communities necessarily solves ecological balance, economies of scale, or political coordination.

## 2. The local inventory of “The ‘Rationality’ of Capitalism”

### Rationality as instituted legitimacy

The 1997 work changes the principal object from technique in general to capitalism’s claim that its regime is rational. Rationality initially names a form of legitimacy: unlike mythic, religious, or traditional validation, capitalism presents its institutions as conforming to reason. The text historicizes that criterion. Capitalism instituted it, even though its recent institution is treated ideologically as proof of universality.

The first conceptual division is between the efficiency of an operation relative to a granted goal and the rationality of the goal itself. Instrumental rationality can select an efficient poison or weapon without justifying murder. It therefore has “no value in itself” [F7]. Capitalist ideology nevertheless identifies welfare with an economic maximum or assumes welfare will follow from one. Economic rationality then becomes maximization of “product” and minimization of “costs”—categories whose contents and measures the regime itself supplies [F4].

The text does not define capitalism through accumulation alone. Its distinctive feature is continual transformation of production and labour processes to increase output and lower cost. This is connected to the “unlimited extension of ‘rational mastery’”: a socially pervasive movement of calculation, control, and extremization [F10]. Technique is central to that movement, but production has no exclusive privilege. Bureaucratic organization, consumption, law, education, and political life are seized by the same signification.

### Immanent critique and the failure of separability

Unlike the broad conceptual history in “Technique,” the 1997 work announces an immanent procedure: capitalism’s economic theory and practice are tested against their own criteria [F9]. The theoretical criticism targets separability, *homo oeconomicus*, mathematization, functional laws, and equilibrium.

Separability assumes that firms, individuals, and factors of production can be isolated and assigned independent contributions. Against it, the text argues that the social product results from cooperation by a collectivity with “fuzzy” boundaries. No worker, firm, or item of capital produces apart from inherited knowledge, institutions, infrastructures, and the activity of others. Separate imputation is thus conventional rather than a transparent discovery of discrete contributions.

The model of *homo oeconomicus* similarly imagines a perfectly calculating actor. Actual consumers have limited information and socially formed tastes; firms are governed through incomplete information, expectations, and struggles among bureaucratic groups. Mathematization then gives these fictions an appearance of exactitude. Economic quantities are heterogeneous and often discrete, while a mathematical function requires rigid relations between determinate variables. The text permits approximate regularities and practical judgment, but not strong laws guaranteeing equilibrium.

The effective economy confirms the critique. Investment depends upon expectations that may or may not induce firms to use productive capacity. Prices, profits, credit, wages, and labour output are shaped by oligopoly, power, incomplete information, institutional arrangements, and conflict. Capitalism can therefore be productive within its own restricted ends without being socially optimal or coherently rational.

### Conflict as a condition of capitalist resilience

The work also refuses to attribute capitalism’s relative stability entirely to its own logic. Workers’ struggles forced wage increases and reductions in working time, creating mass consumer markets that helped prevent the system from “drowning in its own production” [F11]. Later political interventions moderated instability. Capitalist resilience thus depends partly on forces that oppose or constrain its spontaneous movement.

The contemporary diagnosis is harsher: neoliberal dismantling of controls, global capital mobility, insecurity, speculation, and environmental destruction undermine even the limited case for governable economic rationality. Yet this is not a prediction of automatic collapse. The closing claim leaves outcomes dependent on the “reactions and actions” of populations [F5].

## 3. What persists, what changes

### The persistence: neither means nor measures are neutral

The strongest continuity is structural. “Technique” argues that, at the social-historical level, efficiency cannot be isolated from value; “The ‘Rationality’ of Capitalism” argues that capitalism itself determines what counts as product and cost. The later text therefore gives a specific economic form to the earlier general claim [F1].

The objects differ. In 1984 the immediate target is the neutral-instrument/autonomous-destiny antithesis. In 1997 it is the ideology of rational legitimacy and academic economics. But the inferential role persists: both concepts expose a concealed institution of ends. A tool or process cannot be socially assessed merely by whether it works, just as an economy cannot be called rational merely because it maximizes what its own institutions designate as output.

This is more than shared vocabulary. The paired passages make the same argumentative move at different levels: the first denies separability of efficiency and value in social organization; the second shows that the purportedly quantitative objects of calculation are institutionally selected [F1].

### The sharpening: from the real-rational to “rational mastery”

In “Technique,” the “real-rational” is plural and society-specific. It includes whatever a society posits as imposing itself upon it; modern scientific rationality is only one possible constitution. In the later text, the focus narrows to capitalism’s specific imaginary: the unlimited extension of “rational mastery.” The relation is therefore one of specification, not simple synonymy [F12].

“Rational mastery” combines the earlier themes of technique, social organization, and the historical constitution of needs. It names a movement extending calculation and control across production, consumption, administration, and subject formation. The later work also makes the quantitative element more explicit: mastery proceeds through maximization and minimization.

This can be called a conceptual sharpening in the unordered comparative sense: the second formulation is more specific to capitalism and economic calculation. It cannot be called a demonstrated development without evidence that the 1997 text takes up and revises the 1984 formulation.

### The repeated claim about labour

The clearest verbal and argumentative persistence concerns capitalist technology’s orientation toward reducing the worker’s human role. The 1984 text describes capitalist technological development as directed toward suppressing that role; the 1997 text says technical evolution is predominantly oriented toward its reduction and elimination [F13]. The second work presupposes and restates the diagnosis rather than rearguing the whole earlier account of the technical ensemble.

The argument surrounding the claim also persists. Human labour is difficult to master because workers act, resist, and organize informally. Management therefore favors machinery and arrangements that reduce dependence on them. Yet the work process remains indeterminate because no organization can fully eliminate human activity. In 1997, this technological direction becomes one element in capitalism’s broader push toward mastery and one reason its restricted efficiency produces other irrationalities.

### Change in method and force

The first work’s method is reconstructive and expansive: etymology and Greek philosophy establish multiple senses; anthropological and historical examples expand technique beyond objects; political analysis turns that concept against determinism. The second work’s method is more tightly immanent: it begins with capitalism’s own claims, identifies their conceptual assumptions, and compares them with economic operation [F8] [F9].

The rhetorical force also changes. “Technique” preserves a positive concept of creation and projects a possible conscious transformation of technology. “The ‘Rationality’ of Capitalism” concentrates on ideological incoherence, institutionalized calculation, and contemporary regression. Nevertheless, it does not replace politics with fatalism: both works leave transformation dependent on collective action, not on autonomous technological or economic laws.

The strongest counter-passage to a simple continuity claim is the narrow concession in “Technique” that technical activity “considered in and for itself” does not evaluate its assigned ends [F3]. That concession might appear to preserve neutral instrumental rationality. It does so only locally. The text expressly withdraws the concession at the scale of technical ensembles and social history, which is precisely the scale at which the 1997 critique operates.

What is settled is that the supplied works share an anti-neutral, anti-determinist account: technical and economic efficiency are constituted within social meanings and conflicts, and capitalist technology is repeatedly described as reducing the human role in production. The exact remaining question is whether the 1997 concepts of quantitative rationality and “rational mastery” consciously revise the 1984 account of technique and the real-rational. The supplied texts contain no explicit uptake evidence capable of settling that question.

## Judgment across the collection

Across the supplied collection, technique remains bound to social creation, and rationality remains socially instituted rather than self-validating. The later work does not discard the earlier positive account of technique; it changes the foreground. Creative and socially embedded making/doing becomes the technological component of a specifically capitalist project of quantitative mastery. The concept’s argumentative role correspondingly shifts from reconstructing what technique is to exposing what capitalist “rationality” excludes from its calculations. What persists is the refusal of neutrality and one-way determination. What changes is the object under examination and the method of attack—from a general ontology and politics of technique to an immanent critique of capitalist economic legitimacy. Publication chronology permits that comparison, but the collection does not demonstrate textual uptake or a necessary developmental sequence.

### Counter-evidence

- The strongest qualification to the anti-neutrality thesis is local: “There can be no doubt that technical activity, considered in and for itself, takes no account of the value of the ends proposed to it.” — doc: technique [F3]
- The 1997 text prevents a binary contrast between rational capitalism and irrational predecessors: “Not that these societies would have been positively “irrational” on the plane of their organization of work or of their relations of production.” — doc: rationality
- The later work also blocks a simple equation of capitalism with maximization: “But simple maximization (of wealth, of production) does not, as such, suffice to characterize capitalism.” — doc: rationality [F5]

### Open questions

- Does the 1997 text consciously take up the 1984 concepts of technique and the real-rational, or does it independently restate a longstanding position?
- How far does the 1997 emphasis on technoscience modify the 1984 distinction between technique, science, and social institution?
- Does the less programmatic ending of the 1997 work indicate a changed political position, or only the narrower task of that essay?
- The supplied works cannot establish an order between particular internal formulations beyond their publication dates, nor can they establish composition-stage development without further dating evidence.
