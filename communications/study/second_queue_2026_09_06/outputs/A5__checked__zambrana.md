# Reading

## Central relation: with and against Rose

The article is organized by a qualified disagreement with Gillian Rose. Rose’s criticism, as represented here, is that Marx and Marxism preserve a thought/actuality distinction and therefore fail to present capital speculatively. Zambrana accepts Rose’s account of speculative method but rejects its application to Marx: Marx’s account of self-valorization already treats value as capital’s active or “dominant” subject [F1].

This is not a straightforward contradiction within Zambrana’s position. It is an acknowledged selective appropriation: Rose supplies the criterion—speculative thinking presents actuality through its coming-to-be and forms of misrecognition—while Marx’s analysis of value is argued to satisfy that criterion despite Rose’s verdict [F2]. The article’s “with and against Rose” formula therefore marks qualified compatibility between Rose’s method and a revised assessment of Marx, not acceptance of Rose’s entire interpretation.

## Positions, owners, and kinds of opposition

Rose’s attributed position concerns Marx’s relation to Hegel: Marx’s attempt to distance himself from Hegel allegedly leaves the distinction between thought and actuality merely assumed. Zambrana’s counter-position concerns Marx’s analysis of self-valorization, especially interest-bearing capital: this analysis presents value as an organizing activity realized through production, circulation, and finance [F1].

A second opposition concerns Lukács. Rose criticizes Lukács for treating reification as remediable through consciousness, thereby retaining a neo-Kantian subject/object framework. Zambrana preserves part of that criticism but revises its basis: the decisive problem is “not so much a dualism” as Lukács’s conflation of objectification with mystification, which restricts the logic of value to commodity fetishism [F3]. This is a revision of the diagnosis, not a wholesale rehabilitation of Lukács.

The essence/appearance relation supplies the main distinction through which the article blocks an apparent contradiction. Appearance is not a veil opposed to an independently hidden essence: “Essence must appear,” and the forms of appearance are real effects and manifestations of capital [F4]. Consequently, capital’s apparent autonomy in interest-bearing capital can be both mystifying and real in its social effectivity. The article does not claim that appearance is simply accurate; it claims that inversion and irrationality are objective forms through which capital operates.

A third organizing tension is between an account centered on industrial production and wage labor and an expanded account of capital’s historical coming-to-be through slavery, trade, and the money-form. The article treats these not as logically exclusive propositions but as competing scopes of explanation. Smallwood’s account is used to revise the designation of slavery as capital’s “prehistory”: the trade-plantation complex already instantiated capital’s general formula and helped constitute its infrastructures [F5].

## Response, transition, and warrant

The principal response has two stages. Negatively, the article argues that Rose and Lukács miss the reach of Marx’s analysis when commodity fetishism becomes shorthand for capital’s entire logic. Positively, it distinguishes fetishism from mystification and reads interest-bearing capital as evidence that Marx analyzes value’s autonomous-seeming, socially effective movement [F6]. The negative criticism alone would not establish the positive successor. The positive bridge is Marx’s description of interest-bearing capital as the completed capital fetish and as value apparently valorizing itself independently of reproduction. This supports the claim that Marx examines more than production and commodity exchange.

The transition from Marx to Smallwood is broader and less fully demonstrated. The stated bridge is the general formula for capital: Smallwood argues that transatlantic circulation put money into circulation to produce surplus value and more money [F7]. This supports inclusion of colonial slavery within capital’s circuits rather than relegation to a completed preliminary stage. Yet the inference from formal conformity to the general formula to the stronger claim that slavery belongs to the capitalist system itself also depends on Smallwood’s historical account of legal, commercial, financial, and labor infrastructures. If that historical bridge were challenged, the article’s speculative reading of Marx and its fetishism/mystification distinction would remain independently supported; the expanded genealogy of capital would be the affected route.

## Dependence and self-application

The appropriation of Rose is explicit and coherent in scope. Zambrana does not use Rose’s authority to establish Rose’s own assessment of Marx; she applies Rose’s methodological criterion against that assessment [F2]. Withdrawing Rose’s terminology would weaken the characterization of Marx as speculative “in Rose’s sense,” but the textual route through Marx’s analyses of value, money, and interest-bearing capital would survive.

The Smallwood route is more dependent. It supplies the article’s decisive historical expansion from the commodification of free labor power to the genesis of the money-form and capitalist market infrastructures in the circulation of enslaved Africans [F7]. Without that resource, the paper could still argue that self-valorization exceeds industrial production, but it would not have the same stated basis for locating capital’s genesis in the trade-plantation complex.

The paper also applies its own anti-reduction criterion to Marx. Although it defends Marx against Rose, it does not treat Marx’s account as exhaustive: the conclusion says Marx’s conception of value should guide an understanding that moves beyond Marx’s own restriction to industrial capital and wage labor [F8]. This is consistent selective use rather than exemption of Marx from criticism.

## Resolution, remainder, and consequence

The article claims a revised position and continuing mediation, not final synthesis. It preserves Rose’s severe style, Marx’s account of self-valorization, and Smallwood’s genealogy as mutually corrective resources. Its practical conclusion is correspondingly modal and programmatic: understanding revolutionary practice “would” have to track capital beyond production and wage labor, toward institutions and relations in which the genesis of capital remains operative [F9].

What remains unresolved is implementation and evidential reach. The article explicitly limits its Marxian examination to interest-bearing capital and leaves fictitious capital aside [F10]. It also does not provide within this article a full empirical demonstration of which institutions, protocols, and social relations from trade and plantation slavery remain operative today. That is an analyst-identified burden prompted by the conclusion, not an acknowledged refutation.

### Counter-evidence

- Rose’s criticism of Marx is mediated through Zambrana’s summary and selected quotations. The supplied article does not reproduce enough of *Hegel Contra Sociology* to determine independently whether Rose’s position has further qualifications.
- Formal conformity of colonial slave circulation to M-C-M′ does not by itself prove every stronger historical claim about the constitution of capitalism. The article relies on Smallwood’s historical reconstruction to supply that additional warrant.
- The article’s acknowledgment that Marx remained restricted by industrial capital and wage labor qualifies, but does not directly contradict, its claim that Marx already pursued speculative thinking.

### Open questions

1. What historical evidence would establish which institutions and protocols of the trade-plantation complex remain operative in contemporary capitalism?
2. Does the fetishism/mystification distinction remain adequate when extended from interest-bearing to fictitious capital?
3. Is Rose’s verdict on Marx overturned by Marx’s account of self-valorization generally, or only by the selected passages on money and interest-bearing capital?
4. What criterion distinguishes a constitutive condition internal to capitalism from a historical condition of possibility without merely relying on conformity to the general formula?
