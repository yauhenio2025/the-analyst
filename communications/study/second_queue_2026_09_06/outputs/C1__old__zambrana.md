## Reading

Zambrana’s central semantic constellation links **severe style**, **speculative thinking**, **actuality**, **actualization**, **negativity**, **re-cognition**, and **lack of identity**. These terms describe neither an austere tone nor a method that reconciles contradictions into identity. The severe style is fidelity to the object’s own brokenness; speculative thinking is the mode of exposition that follows an object through the material and socio-historical conditions of its becoming. Its negative pole is **representational thought**, clustered with reification, fixed dichotomies, external predication, and neo-Kantian distinctions between thought and actuality, subject and object, essence and appearance.

Within this field, **actuality** is sharply distinguished from mere existence or immediate reality. It means effectivity: what acts, produces, manifests itself, and becomes determinate through its relations. **Necessity** consequently does not mean teleological inevitability. Real and absolute necessity name the retrospective intelligibility of an actuality arising through contingent conditions. “Lack of identity” and “porosity” prevent actuality from becoming a closed totality: determinate content never exhausts the conditions of its emergence.

A second cluster translates this Hegelian vocabulary into Marx’s critique of political economy: **value**, **capital**, **self-valorization**, **value in motion**, **value in process**, **dominant subject**, **metamorphosis**, and **forms of appearance**. Value is not merely a quantitative property of commodities. It functions as capital’s organizing activity across production, circulation, and finance. Self-valorization is its distilled speculative form: value becomes subject-like by organizing the processes through which it increases itself.

The distinction between **fetishism** and **mystification** is the article’s crucial semantic refinement. Fetishism remains associated with thingification, personification, and the objective social appearance of relations as things. Mystification has broader scope: it names the real irrational forms through which self-valorizing value manifests and produces effects. Both are forms of appearance, not subjective errors or veils concealing a separately existing essence. The governing Hegelian maxim is that “Essence must appear.” Zambrana thereby shifts the opposition from appearance versus hidden truth to misrecognized appearance versus speculative comprehension of appearance’s real constitution and effectivity.

A third cluster—**genesis**, **coming to be**, **trade-plantation complex**, **colonial slave capitalism**, **unfree labor**, and **contemporaneity**—expands the field of capital beyond industrial production and wage labor. Its negative terms are **prehistory**, **originary accumulation**, and **primitive accumulation**, insofar as these imply that slavery is external or merely prior to capitalism proper. Smallwood’s account bridges historical genesis and structural logic: the circulation of African captives is presented as formative of the money-form, capitalist market protocols, and the circuits summarized by capital’s general formula.

Several boundaries remain deliberately porous. “Condition of possibility” sometimes means a historically external prerequisite and sometimes an immanent component of actuality; Zambrana seeks to replace the former with the latter but continues to use the phrase. Likewise, “mystification” moves between a specific Marxian form of inverted appearance and a broad name for capital’s socio-historical manifestations. These shifts are productive because they let the argument connect logical form, historical genesis, and present effectivity, but they also create the risk that every manifestation beyond commodity fetishism becomes “mystification.”

The article’s decisive bridge concept is **coming to be**. It connects Hegelian actuality, Marxian value, Smallwood’s history of slavery, and revolutionary practice. The result is an argument that capital’s meaning lies not in an isolated definition but in its full constellation of production, circulation, finance, coercion, racial slavery, historical persistence, and practical transformation.

### Counter-evidence

- Rose’s reported position sharply separates Marx from speculative thinking, countering Zambrana’s reconstruction of Marx as Hegelian. — anchor: "Marx and Marxism retain a distinction between thought and actuality that forecloses a proper account of capital" — confidence: high

- Zambrana acknowledges that Marx’s own account remains restricted by its emphasis on industrial capital and wage labor, even while she treats value as capable of exceeding that restriction. — anchor: "Marx’s conception of value ought to guide our understanding of capital beyond his own restricted account tied to industrial capital and wage labor." — confidence: high

- Fetishism is sometimes defined narrowly as thingification, but elsewhere the commodity is said to express exploitation, expropriation, dispossession, and originary accumulation; its boundary with mystification is therefore not entirely stable. — anchor: "It is the embodiment of a social relation of exploitation, but what has been seen as originary accumulation as well." — confidence: medium

- The claim that capital frees itself from material limits is qualified by the insistence that apparent autonomy remains historically and materially mediated. — anchor: "The truth of capital, as value in motion, is its capacity to free itself from the organic, material limits of production and circulation" — confidence: medium

### Open questions

- Does “mystification” retain a precise boundary once it includes all forms through which value organizes the social totality beyond production?
- Is the trade-plantation complex constitutive of capitalism because it follows the general formula for capital, because it generates the money-form, or because its institutions persist? The text invokes all three relations without fully ranking them.
- Can “condition of possibility” be retained without reintroducing the externality that the language of actuality and immanent genesis is intended to overcome?
- How exactly do coerced labor and freely alienated wage labor differ within the value-form once both are located inside capital’s actuality?
- If necessity is retrospectively articulated through contingent conditions, what prevents speculative history from treating whatever occurred as necessary?
- What criteria distinguish a real form of mystification from any other historically effective manifestation of capital?
