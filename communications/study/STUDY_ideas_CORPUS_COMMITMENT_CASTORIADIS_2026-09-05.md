## The dependency paragraph imports a criticized account of possibility

The most concrete attribution error occurs behind F8. The final says the technique argument begins from two presuppositions—possibility and effective reason—and derives technique as “a non-natural actualization of possibilities.” It then makes that derivation support the later political consequence. But C84:31–41 reconstructs Aristotle's conception and its limits. At 35 the possible and true reason belong to that reconstruction; at 37 the non-natural actualization is explained within it. At 39 Castoriadis criticizes the reduction of novelty to an already given possible as a “canonical and vacuous tautology.”

C84:67 explicitly proposes recovering and reworking threads that Aristotle and Marx let drop. The positive account at 67–75 concerns instituting a new type within a materially resistant world containing regularities and indeterminate interstices. It does not require that the new type already exist as a possibility waiting to be actualized. F8's own ledger quote about the world's rational make-up and interstices is genuine and relevant, but it does not verify the different derivation asserted in the prose. This is a source-position error hidden behind a sound nearby quotation, not a failure to acknowledge material constraints.

## The proposed single failure does not reach every route

F5 identifies the definition of capitalist rationality as quantitative maximization/minimization as the main point of failure. Selecting this definition as a first question for C97 is reasonable. The final overreaches when its closing paragraph says that, if the reduction fails, the corpus's strongest bridge from technical non-neutrality to autonomy loses its support.

The final itself has already reconstructed independent chains. C84:89–97 argues about the inseparability of inherited means and social ends; 145–169 connects technical selection, workplace conflict, and workers' participation. C90:33–49 develops self-limitation and effective participation, and 129–153 connects political imputation to democratic economic decisions. These arguments do not all depend on C97:19's characterization of capitalist legitimation. Rejecting that characterization may weaken one immanent critique without defeating the anti-determinist argument or the normative case for autonomy.

The hypothetical alternative capitalism, already furnished with revisable ends, ecological costs, political equality, and collective judgment, also requires careful interpretation: it incorporates several of the author's proposed changes. It is not enough to imagine that alternative and infer that the earlier independent arguments have lost their grounds. The reader needs the particular route affected and what remains supported.

## Instituted justification is not an unnoticed inconsistency

F17 still labels the relation between no external foundation and substantive autonomy/equality an incompatibility. The prose calls it an unresolved tension, then states the source's own answer: these values are instituted and affirmed rather than deduced from a standpoint outside society. C97:23 expressly applies the institutional circle to autonomous society. It adds that such a society can justify itself downstream through its works, while acknowledging that their evaluation still depends on instituted criteria. C90:33–39 likewise explains rights and limits as acts of self-limitation without a transcendent guarantee.

This remains a consequential philosophical commitment, open to criticism. It does not become an unaddressed incompatibility merely because it lacks the external warrant it explicitly rejects. The final should distinguish the acknowledged burden of defending instituted values from the practical question of how people formed under heteronomy create autonomous institutions. The latter is a real transition problem that F26 appropriately leaves open. Similarly, C97:27 says the immediate critique will “basically” be immanent; the text selects a route, rather than establishing that every criticism must use only capitalism's criteria.

A smaller paired-evidence problem reinforces the need for semantic checking. F12 pairs historically indeterminate needs in C84 with omitted ecological harms in C97. The second passage does not directly establish the first claim's continuation. C97:76's social influences on preferences would offer a closer comparison. Conversely, F6 improves on rejected X6.F6 by pairing actual ecological-cost criticism with C90's prudence, instead of using the inadequate quasi-autarky anchor.
