## Deutschmann: each essay and the comparative trajectory

### D01E: from money's paradox to organized innovation

The target is an economic sociology capable of explaining the creation and destruction of institutions. Religious ethics influencing economic conduct is distinguished from capital possessing intrinsically religious qualities (21–23). Parallel failures to define religion and money motivate the comparison: functional definitions are too expansive, institutional definitions too restrictive; faith or trust cannot be replaced by the institutions supporting it (25–33). The disciplinary division between economics and sociology helps explain why money's difficult status was neglected (31).

Simmel supplies the intermediate concept: *Vermögen* combines command over actual and imaginable goods, social independence, and temporal/spatial options (41–51). “the option to have options” (51) is valuable beyond any purchase. This is not a claim about precious-metal backing. Nor do equal monetary quantities have equal significance for rich and poor: subsistence dependence removes the latter's freedom of choice (53). Money both signifies value and must itself be privately transferred as something scarce; identifying it solely with communication loses this paradox (55).

Deutschmann reconstructs Marx's capital circuit as a practical response: hoarding fails to realize monetary power, consumption dissipates it, while investment promises its return with an increment (57–61). Finite sums cannot exhaust qualitatively unlimited possibilities; growth repeatedly displaces the contradiction. Schumpeter then supplies innovation and temporary monopoly, but his heroic entrepreneur neglects employees and the coalitions needed to realize an innovation (69–73).

The proposed Marx–Schumpeter combination is selective. Creative labour is indispensable, but hours of labour do not guarantee profit; Deutschmann explicitly rejects Marx's quantitative law of value (75). Workers' freedom and capital's dependence on their cooperation qualify a purely command-based account (77). Technological and organizational myths coordinate heterogeneous participants, motivate cooperation, and legitimate asymmetries. Their social resonance enables bounded rationality without supplying an independently rational foundation for the paradigm itself (79–81).

Institutionalization stabilizes knowledge and markets, yet locks in investments and reduces innovative profitability. Success thus prepares decline; successor myths reorganize predecessors' blind spots and material legacies (83–91). The conclusion preserves the religious analogy while distinguishing capitalism's moving boundary of transcendence from religions' comparatively fixed one (99–103). Its final criticism is already disenchantment with the religion of money (107), not celebration of unlimited innovation.

### D01P: cosmological institutions and the possible exhaustion of mediation

Promise shares much of that sequence, but its distinctive passages matter. Benjamin supplies the opening challenge; Tenbruck supplies a critique of supposedly context-free concepts of religion. The proposed common term is explicitly “a *negative* one” (28): both phenomena determine the conditions of their observation. This is a methodological restriction, not an exhaustive definition equating every religion with capitalism (20–42).

The Simmel–Marx argument again joins generalized monetary possibilities to class asymmetry, creative labour, and compulsory accumulation (48–74). Promise more explicitly argues that money crosses subsystem boundaries because politics, science, art, and religion require financing (66). It also distinguishes profit from interest and rejects the sufficiency of higher exploitation through the example of an economy producing only cement (80).

Civilizational theory gives the entrepreneurial argument a further level. Institutions represent cosmological horizons imperfectly; the gap permits their criticism and transformation. Castoriadis, Eisenstadt, and Arnason are credited for this approach, while Giddens's duality is judged insufficient here (86). Deutschmann's extension is to treat absolute wealth as a secular cosmological horizon underlying private property (88; note 154). Money's capacity to circulate between institutionalized markets creates the indeterminate “meta-market”; entrepreneurs interpret this horizon through manageable projects (90).

Myths now include consumption and lifestyles, and the account explicitly limits the observer to reconstructing actors' creation and transformation of structures (98–116). Myth-mediated rationality is historically achieved, not an all-purpose explanation of development. The detailed cycle culminates in a trade-off between rational stabilization and profitability (106–110).

The ending tests whether that cycle can continue. Financial globalization and market radicalism demand immediate success while weakening the institutions and reciprocity that entrepreneurship needs (130–134). Withdrawal into financial assets is offered as a possible symptom, not established through a new empirical investigation. If creative destruction stops, dependence of ordinary reproduction on growth threatens a depressive spiral. The conclusion rejects another redemptive utopia and proposes relinquishing the collective obsession with absolute wealth (136). The conditional diagnosis should survive any synthesis.

### D22: unbounded markets and society's missing observer

The chapter identifies its own position as secular sociological critique, distinguishing it from theological denunciation of idols (11–15). Capitalism means a society organized through unbounded markets; the existence of markets alone is insufficient (21). A reconstructed history links Enlightenment projects, subsequent liberal narratives, and unintended institutional consequences. Smith is specifically distinguished from later reception that turned his approach into a social construction recipe (23–27).

Spatial, social, material, and temporal unbounding explain why money cannot remain a specialized economic medium (29–31). Access to productive conditions, especially creative labour, expands property claims from what exists to what could exist. Cooperative arrangements remain boundary conditions. The slavery note acknowledges unfree labour's central historical role, then restricts the free-labour argument to the cooperation required by subsequent industrial revolutions (95).

The new organizing problem is society's inability to observe itself as a totality. Luhmann supplies the internality of observation; Hayek supplies dispersed knowledge that no observer possesses in full. Deutschmann draws the parallel without alleging influence between them (37–39). Liberalism's success produces a society-wide self-description that cannot simply depict the whole it invokes (41). Religions supply a constructed external observer; the comparison explicitly concentrates on Abrahamic traditions (47). Their contributions to universal ethics, rationalization, and scientific distance are acknowledged (49).

Removing theological authority leaves the observation problem intact. New economic and sociological descriptions can conceal the unity they claim to represent (51–57). Capitalism's universal inclusion rests on minimal exchange reciprocity, not the absence of sociality; universality and moral minimalism are connected (63–65). Money then assumes functions of representing social unity and managing contingency (67).

Creative labour and changing imaginations retain the mobile-transcendence argument (69–75). Technological achievements can satisfy earthly wishes while continually generating more. The principal driver is expanding private-property possibilities, not science or instrumental reason acting independently; science and technology help actualize those possibilities (75). The closing prescription is practical containment and relatively impartial sociological discussion, with neither total observational mastery nor historical reconciliation promised (81–87).

### Deutschmann: cross-document findings and limits

**Continuity with changed explanatory role.** The detailed definition of monetary possibility in `deutschmann2001_capitalism_as_religion`, 51, “the option to have options”, can be paired with `deutschmann2022_interpretation_of_capitalism_as_religion`, 75, “expansion of the possibility space of private property rights”. The early concept explains money's intrinsic value and growth; the later formulation also explains why science is subordinate to a property-driven dynamic. This is strong conceptual continuity, not proof that the later chapter presupposes this particular article: 2022 re-explains the mechanism and cites Deutschmann 2019 (27, 67, 119).

**Luhmann's role changes without a simple conversion.** `deutschmann2001_promise_of_absolute_wealth`, 66, criticizes a money theory that “lacks sensitivity for the hidden influence of money”, while already borrowing the notion of cipher. `deutschmann2022_interpretation_of_capitalism_as_religion`, 37–39, makes internal observation and self-description central; 67 nevertheless retains the criticism of restricting money to scarcity regulation. Acceptance of one component does not entail endorsement of the entire theory.

**The comparison is reformulated and delimited.** `deutschmann2001_promise_of_absolute_wealth`, 28, rules out an abstract, suprahistorical common definition. `deutschmann2022_interpretation_of_capitalism_as_religion`, 47, says “I am not speaking of all religions,” while 57 develops the parallel through social intransparency. This is a demonstrable methodological reformulation and explicit scope qualification. Neither passage establishes a recantation of the earlier negative comparison. Non-observability was present from the start.

**The normative ending persists.** Pair `deutschmann2001_promise_of_absolute_wealth`, 136, “no new utopias will show the way out”, with `deutschmann2022_interpretation_of_capitalism_as_religion`, 87, “What is needed is not new promises but sobriety and realism”. The later text specifies containment and sociology's role; it does not discover sobriety after youthful optimism. The 2001 financialization warning and 2022 ecological crisis also have different immediate objects.

**Free labour receives an explicit historical qualification.** `deutschmann2001_capitalism_as_religion`, 77, says the worker “must be free in order to be creative”. `deutschmann2022_interpretation_of_capitalism_as_religion`, 95, acknowledges that “unfree labor, especially slave labor, played a central role” in colonial capitalism and the first industrial revolution. It then locates the need for formally free cooperation in later industrial revolutions. This genuinely qualifies the historical scope of the thesis, without announcing a self-revision or abandoning the creativity mechanism.

**Universalism changes the comparison's scale, not its recognition of power.** `deutschmann2001_promise_of_absolute_wealth`, 62, treats poverty and wealth as differing “in qualitative terms”; 66 already extends money's influence across all subsystems. `deutschmann2022_interpretation_of_capitalism_as_religion`, 65, joins “Moral minimalism and social universalism” while explicitly preserving structurally conditioned power inequalities. The later emphasis is worldwide inclusion through exchange rather than richer individuals' expanded options. Universal inclusion does not imply equal effective freedom; this is not an explicit reversal of the earlier class analysis.

**There is a tension to investigate, not a demonstrated reversal.** `deutschmann2001_promise_of_absolute_wealth`, 134, says radicalism “tends to paralyse the working of the myth cycle”; `deutschmann2022_interpretation_of_capitalism_as_religion`, 75, says the promises' dynamic “cannot be stopped” from within. The former is a qualified claim about productive coordination and a possible halt; the latter concerns structural pressure to expand. Stalled realization and persisting imperative can coexist. Calling these flatly inconsistent requires additional argument.

**Inheritance must remain claim-specific.** `deutschmann2001_capitalism_as_religion`, 75, rejects a quantitative Marxian theory while rehabilitating creative labour. `deutschmann2022_interpretation_of_capitalism_as_religion`, 75, closes with “nothing needs to be added to Marx's insights even today”, specifically after discussing freedom-guaranteeing property becoming collective compulsion. This does not reinstate the rejected law of value. Likewise, citation of Castoriadis at Promise 86 establishes use of a civilizational approach, not uptake of any particular essay in the separate Castoriadis corpus.

## Castoriadis: each essay's argument and its inferential stakes

### C84: technique as an instituted dimension of making and doing

The opening rejects the choice between technique as a neutral instrument and technique as autonomous destiny: both permit society to evade responsibility for its creations (15–19). This is already a political argument, not a preliminary technical phase before a later discovery of politics.

The historical conceptual reconstruction is selective and adversarial. Aristotle supplies the creator as origin of a technical object, the realm of what could be otherwise, and the possible creation of what nature cannot accomplish (31–41). Castoriadis credits these openings while criticizing their enclosure within imitation and already-given possibility. Marx supplies self-engendering through social labor, but Castoriadis argues that the conception contracts into technique's supposedly rational unfolding between fixed nature and fixed needs (47–59). He explicitly says Marx's texts are richer and cannot simply be reduced to the determinist thesis (53; 101). Heidegger and Ellul identify harms and challenge neutrality, but their counterposition risks retaining the same autonomous essence with its evaluative sign reversed (61–65). Citation here transfers individual insights, not the entire cited framework.

The positive account combines creation with constraints. Technical creation posits an eidos that was not a natural model waiting to be imitated (67–71). Yet a resistant reality has regularities and “immense interstices” (73), so creation neither escapes material constraints nor follows a predetermined inventory of possible inventions. Technique helps constitute what a society takes as real and rational (73–79). Human needs lack a fixed final list: “there are no obligatory problems” (81). Institution exceeds technique, though social technique is indispensable to it (85–87).

This yields a carefully scoped account of neutrality. An engineer can calculate an efficient bridge, and that technical adequacy differs from whether the bridge should be built (89). At the scale of a society's inherited technical ensemble, means, ends, practices, expertise, and installed systems cannot be separated in this way (91–97). The essay therefore retains local technical judgments while rejecting global social neutrality. An X6 account that says Castoriadis refuses every technical distinction between means and ends loses an explicit qualification.

The determinism argument then becomes institutional and economic. There is no isolated technical object outside skills, ensembles, and social organization (103–109); capitalist technique and economy mutually belong to a social world without either becoming a separable sufficient cause (111–129). The long critique of economic technique selection distinguishes Sraffa's internal refutation from Castoriadis's objection to quantification, separability, perfect information, and bureaucratic decision processes (131–143). Sraffa is credited without being adopted as the final method.

Class conflict supplies the most concrete mechanism. Management selects techniques to reduce dependence on workers (145–147); technical selection is both “an instrument of class struggle” and a stake in it (151). Official work organization depends on workers' informal activity that it also tries to control; work-to-rule and counter-management expose the resulting indeterminacy (153–157). These passages are crucial for later claims about labor output and capitalist adaptation.

The proposed consequence is workers' participation in transforming technology, including cooperation with technical staff and elimination of a separate managing bureaucracy (165–169). It is conditional and unfinished: inherited technology constrains transformation (95), economies of scale are not all fictitious (175), isolated self-sufficient communities do not solve every coordination problem (177), and the population must decide what counts as adequate information for its own decisions (179). The conclusion refuses to mistake present desires for knowledge of future culture (181).

### C90: democracy requires social capacities, substantive decisions, and self-limitation

The occasion is the proclamation of democratic triumph after Eastern European communism's collapse (9). Castoriadis distinguishes democracy from Rousseau's impossible identity of collective legislation and collective execution; delegation is compatible with democracy (11–15). He also distinguishes it from Tocqueville's equality of conditions: formal status equality can coexist with political oligarchy (19–31). His positive standard is effective participation in making common laws (41–49), requiring access to information, judgment, education, and time. Those conditions reconnect political power to economic organization.

Because there is no external or transcendent rule that guarantees the limits of self-instituting power, democracy must be the “regime of selflimitation” (33–39). Human rights are valuable acts of collective self-limitation, not an unrevisable guarantee that removes politics. The treatment of tragedy stresses the permanent possibility of hubris, not immunity from violence. Politics concerns judgment and doxa rather than a science of the right answer (57), but “not all the doxai are equivalent” (59). Rejection of political science as ultimate authority does not imply that any opinion is equally good.

The critique of contemporary representation concerns the effective privatization of public decision, irrevocable mandates, party apparatuses, and the production of future electoral options by earlier decisions (45–87). It does not follow merely from the existence of delegates. Western regimes retain real, if limited, emancipatory gains and possibilities for contestation (61–63); calling them liberal oligarchies does not equate them with every other regime.

The individual is also an institutionally formed person, not an asocial unit to whom neutral procedural rights can simply be added (95–113). Norms have substantive consequences; provision for children, taxation, inheritance, and social continuity cannot be derived from a contentless injunction to preserve individual freedom (101–111). The capitalist imaginary organizes consumption and the individual who chooses it (113–115). This is the political application of C84's argument that means, needs, and their social subjects cannot be treated as independent givens.

The economic digression is load-bearing. Castoriadis separates markets from capitalism, disputing the identification of existing oligopolistic arrangements with a genuinely competitive market (117–125). He then argues that technical novelty changes not merely coefficients but the space of possible products (129–135), and that imputation of costs rests on conventional property boundaries rather than independently separable contributions (137–141). These are developed arguments here, not merely silent imports from an earlier essay.

The consequence is an explicit democratic economy: consumer sovereignty and a genuine market, producers' self-management, rejection of unearned rents, collective decisions about cost imputation, and a decision whether and how fast to grow (151–155). His equal-labor-hour convention at 153 is proposed as a political rule; it is not evidence that he has reverted to a naturally true labor theory of value.

The ending makes the continuity of liberal oligarchy depend on two exhaustible inheritances: ecological reserves (155–161) and socially formed capacities such as integrity and responsibility which its money-centered norms cannot reproduce (163–173). Its empirical illustrations include a newspaper report of studies and interview testimony (167–171), not only philosophical claims. Their scope should remain visible. Autonomy now faces a problem of desire and capacity, not just of institutional possibility (175–183). The provisional consequence is criticism and exemplary action; a collective movement cannot be produced by a few people's will (181).

### C97: an immanent critique that explains capitalism's resilience through contestation

The essay explicitly limits its immediate task despite its diagnosis of contemporary regression (13–17). It refuses to derive a decisive external refutation from capitalism's circular legitimation: an autonomous society, too, institutes the values by which it judges itself (19–23). Hence the declared method is “basically” immanent: test economic theory for coherence and its account of practice against capitalism's own criteria (27–32). This does not erase the author's evaluative position; it specifies the selected argumentative route.

Capitalism's specificity is the elevation of economic production, consumption, and monetary evaluation into central social values (38–50). The idea of rational mastery extends across social spheres; treating non-economic instances as merely derivative would repeat the criticized Marxist reduction (52). Its emergence requires a conjunction of technical change, state bureaucracy, nations, anthropological change, and internal contestation (54–64). The text explicitly distinguishes these conditions from consequences (54).

The economic critique revisits separability, homo oeconomicus, mathematization, prediction, and equilibrium (74–88). The limitations have scope: basic arithmetic, engineering calculation, qualitative comparisons, and approximate regularities remain possible (80; 84; 86). The paper then itself offers a deliberately qualified reconstruction of distribution, investment, demand, and growth (104–108). A reading should compare that reconstruction's stated approximations with the exactness claimed by its rivals, rather than declare every economic explanation self-contradictory.

The strongest genealogy concerns capitalism's dependence on its opponents. Labor struggles and political intervention countered pressures toward low wages, excess production, and crisis, creating markets and conditions for growth (152–156). They did not stop being oppositional because their effects helped capitalism survive. The contemporary weakening of those limits changes the problem: uncontrolled globalization can coexist with continuing corporate success and broader social/ecological damage (158–162). The ending explicitly declines to predict a necessary general economic collapse; consequences depend on populations' actions (164).

### Apparent contradictions that require scope analysis

- **Technique both lacks neutrality and permits technical efficiency:** compare C84:89 with 91–97. Local engineering and the social ensemble are different objects.
- **Democracy permits delegation but rejects representation:** compare C90:11–15 with 65–71. The targeted delegation is lengthy, irrevocable, and able to determine subsequent options.
- **A democratic economy uses prices after the critique of economic measurement:** compare C90:129–139 with 151–155. Publicly decided conventions do not claim to discover natural quantities or a guaranteed optimum.
- **Capitalism has market relations but no genuine market:** C90:117 uses “market” polemically for a genuinely competitive institution; C97:150 also denies that capitalism has ever had a perfect market. This is not by itself a reversal.
- **Technoscience becomes autonomous although technical autonomy was rejected:** C84:63–65 attacks an absolutely independent determining essence; C90:177 and C97:56 describe an autonomized social movement. This could expose a burden about institutional control, but the levels must first be distinguished.
- **Criticism is immanent although autonomy has normative value:** C97:23–27 acknowledges the circularity for autonomy and chooses an immanent route. It does not claim a value-free place outside all institutions.

No explicit retraction of one of these central commitments was located in the three supplied essays. Their substantial changes concern the object and occasion of the argument, the function of a premise, and the practical problem to which it is applied. A future output claiming a substantive reversal should supply the exact paired commitments and explain why these qualifications fail.

## How to use the notebook for the four actual corpus memos

For each output, select a few consequential findings, rather than scoring how many phrases match this notebook. Record the finding id, the full document keys, both source passages, what the row claims across them, and how the synthesis uses it. A code-verified quotation establishes textual occurrence, not inheritance, entailment, incompatibility, or causation.

P6 should distinguish an explicit self-citation, a plausible continuity, a later compression of an earlier argument, and a later reargument. Silence about an earlier alternative is a coverage observation; two selected snippets alone cannot prove corpus-wide absence. X6 should identify shared commitments without merging distinct scopes, and should be allowed to find no demonstrated incompatibility.

The later memos should compare source reading with actual extractor rows, critic rulings, verified ledger, and final synthesis. A useful genealogy explains why a position can keep its commitments while changing their role; three chapter summaries or an invented conversion narrative would not do that. No output quality or study success is asserted here.
