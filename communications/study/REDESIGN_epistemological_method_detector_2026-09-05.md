# Redesign: Epistemological Method Detector (2026-09-05)

> Fourth engine under the shape. Lineage Haraway (situated knowledges), Harding (strong objectivity), Fricker (testimonial and hermeneutical injustice), Quine (naturalized epistemology), Dewey (warranted assertibility), Husserl (epoché). Current definition: six dimensions that are a checklist of schools (phenomenological method deployment, standpoint positioning, situatedness practice, reflexive depth, epistemic justice awareness, epistemological coherence); on most texts four of the six find nothing to say, and the questions ask what "a Husserlian critique would reveal" rather than what the text does. The method is real: every text that claims to know carries a theory of what counts as knowing. The redesign asks the text how it knows what it claims.

## 1. The ideal output

**One paper.** (1) What counts as evidence here: the kinds the text uses (press releases, interviews, statutes, other scholars) and treats as sufficient, against what the text says its method is; (2) whose testimony is credited: the sources quoted as authorities, the accounts dismissed and by what device, the accounts absent whose experience the argument concerns; (3) where the text speaks from: its declared position and limits ("we", its discipline, its acknowledged partiality) and the claims that exceed what that position could know; (4) professed against practised: the modesty or fallibilism the text professes against its evidential verbs ("clearly", "attests", "what is discernible"), and the framework it uses in one section and drops in another; (5) self-application: whether the standard the text applies to others applies to itself, and where it exempts itself; (6) the synthesis: the text's implicit theory of knowledge, where it breaks, and what a reader should ask before trusting the central claim. Tables: evidence kinds (kind, count of uses, what it is asked to establish), testimony (who, credited or dismissed, device), professed vs practised (professed, practised, anchors).

**Corpus.** Which theory of knowledge the texts share; whose testimony a whole literature credits; the standard one text applies that another fails.

**Depth.** The evidence inventory is complete; every dismissal is anchored with its device; every excess claim is paired with the position statement it exceeds; the self-application test is run on the central critique.

**Weak output.** "The text does not practise phenomenological bracketing"; scores on situatedness; sentences about the authors' identity or motives.

## 2. Dimensions and questions
- **E1 evidence**: what kinds of evidence the text uses; what each is asked to establish; what the text says its method is and what the evidence actually is; what the text treats as settled knowledge without evidence.
- **E2 testimony**: who is quoted as an authority and for what; who is dismissed and by what device (a label, a group citation, "vague"); whose account the argument concerns and never hears.
- **E3 position**: what the text says about where it speaks from and what it cannot know; which claims exceed that position (a generalisation from two cases; an intention attributed to an actor without a source); whether the text uses a standpoint to detect others' bias.
- **E4 professed and practised**: the epistemic modesty the text professes (hedges, "partial", "tentative") against its evidential verbs; the framework used in one section and dropped in another; what would revise the central claim, and whether the text says.
- **E5 self-application**: the standard the text applies to rivals, applied to the text; where the text exempts itself; where reflexivity is a gesture with no effect on a claim.
- **X6 (corpus) shared theory of knowledge**: evidence kinds and testimony patterns across texts.

## 3. Method cards
- **Fricker (E2).** Do: list who speaks in the text with authority and who is spoken about; for every dismissal name the device; ask whose experience the argument concerns and whether it is heard.
- **Haraway and Harding (E3, E5).** Do: locate the knower: quote the text's statements of position and limit; test each large claim against what that position could know; find the standpoint the text uses to see others' bias and ask whether it turns the standpoint on itself.
- **Quine and Dewey (E1, E4).** Do: treat the text's knowledge as a practice: inventory the evidence and the verbs; compare the professed method with the practised one; ask what would revise the claim and whether the text says.
- **Husserl (E1, only if the text brackets).** Do: where the text claims to suspend assumptions, quote the suspension and the first assumption it does not suspend.

## 4. Desks
Evidence and testimony tables are the reader's audit; the professed/practised pairs are the spine's crux candidates.
