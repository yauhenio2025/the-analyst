## Pair

In `source_aukus.txt`, the regulatory-control discussion (roughly lines 368–487) distinguishes Pillar I's submarine-delivery uncertainty from Pillar II's beneficiaries. Australia is designated a domestic source under Title III of the US Defense Production Act; the article gives financing awards, export-control changes, export-credit institutions, and Japanese/Korean regulatory changes. The venture-capital section (roughly 681–776) describes state-backed intermediaries, named agencies and investments. The conclusion's explicit exclusion of an assessment of regional conflict (roughly 778 onward) cannot support the separate claim about submarine delays around lines 395–401. Announcements, eligibility, awards, and demonstrated outcomes must remain distinct.

In `source_subsea.txt`, the SeaMeWe-6 account (roughly lines 568–687) distinguishes the supplier selection from the consortium members: China Telecom and China Mobile withdraw; China Unicom remains. The PEACE/EMA passage around lines 662–683 says PEACE became operational in 2022 and describes another project, EMA, following exclusion from SeaMeWe-6. The conclusion (around 908–913) specifically attributes a direct reaction to US interference to EMA; PEACE is described as mirroring the route. Parallel geography does not establish a shared causal history.

The France case around lines 727–859 preserves selective engagement with US and Chinese projects. ASN's 80% acquisition for €350 million through APE, with an intention to buy the remaining 20% after transition, is stated around 807–813. Forecast revenue growth is not realized profitability. French supply/manufacturing strength exceeds its project-planning and financing position; EU fiscal constraints, US sanctions and reliance on US tech companies limit the strategy. This differs from the AUKUS partners' alliance-linked industrial engagement. Shared room for maneuver is a broad question, not evidence of identical positioning.

## Castoriadis

The Technique passages on neutrality and the technical ensemble distinguish the technical system from isolated devices and efficiency criteria. The constructive proposal requires workers' management, changed work processes, and collaboration between workers and technical staff. A quote merely denying neutrality cannot carry this whole alternative. The closing discussion explicitly concedes that economies of scale are not always fictitious and that quasi-autarkic communities do not remove the universalization problem.

What Democracy? explicitly describes robotization as capitalism's response to enterprise contradictions and says it merely defers or displaces the question to parts not yet robotized. This passage was missed in the first P2 control. The economic sketch proposes consumer sovereignty and a genuine market, producer self-management, rejection of unearned rents, and collective political decisions about production-cost conventions. These are separate propositions requiring adequate evidence. Its autonomy/self-limitation argument does not equate every historical regime or dismiss existing rights.

Rationality distinguishes the critique of capitalist ends and economic calculation from relative efficiency, flexibility and resistance. Its discussion of national-product/index comparisons expressly preserves broad statements about output falling or working-class consumption increasing while rejecting spurious decimal precision. Its workplace-opacity argument names both bureaucratization and worker resistance. None of the three supplied dates by itself establishes an intellectual progression.

## Deutschmann

Capitalism as Religion (2001) qualifies the religious analogy: religious myths claim timeless validity, while economic myths grow and decline and make the mundane/transcendent distinction dynamic. Its critique of Schumpeter stresses the entrepreneur's dependence on workers' creative capacities, including small innovations and ordinary contingencies. Money's general capacity and labor's creativity must not be collapsed into a single mechanism without marking the reconstruction as an inference.

The Promise of Absolute Wealth (2001) already argues that market radicalism can undermine the institutional, social and worker cooperation on which entrepreneurship depends. Its closing warning about the myth cycle and withdrawal into financial markets precedes its explicit rejection of new utopias and proposed departure from absolute wealth. A 2022-versus-2001 contrast must retain this earlier qualification.

The 2022 interpretation uses society's intransparency, unbounded markets, Simmel's absolute means, and competing market-liberal and republican freedoms in a comparative argument. Its conclusion calls for practical containment beyond piecemeal reforms, sobriety, and restraint in sociology's new self-descriptions. It supplies neither an implemented program nor grounds to label rejection of new promises a first-time departure from the earlier texts. Its institutional growth account and the earlier myth-cycle account may be compared as different emphases; compatibility alone does not prove one jointly endorsed causal model.
