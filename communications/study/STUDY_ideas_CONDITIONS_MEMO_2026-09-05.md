## Harris: better distinctions, an overstated terminal counterfactual

Both versions correctly find that Harris adopts Horkheimer's transformative programme as a standard and makes monism constitutive of Honneth's theoretical identity. The baseline's self-citation focus is grounded: old F1–F3 and F6 correspond to Harris's account of the earlier book and the review prompting this paper (160–218). This is not an invented genealogy. But old F4/F8 turn reported reception into a prepared audience and costly reputational retreat; neither inference follows from the cited passages. Old F20–F21 also risk turning Thompson's proposed successor into Harris's required programme, despite “Whether one agrees with Thompson’s approach, or not” (775–792).

Checked D4.F4–F6 deliver a more useful diagnostic map: Honneth's temporal development (note 2, 822–827), the recent engagement with labor (note 5, 837–839), and the difference between inability to explain **all** pathologies and inability to explain **any**, including recognition pathologies (828–834). The last distinction makes the article's strongest inferential burden explicit. D4.F7 also distinguishes quiescence attributed to monism from Honneth's substantive endorsement of capitalist institutions (715–750). These are concrete gains over an account governed mainly by personal intellectual trajectory, although the baseline's counter-evidence already notices the first two qualifications.

The final subtraction still overstates its result. Checked D5.F5 and the prose say the terminal rejection collapses without the inherited frame and unbreakable monism. Harris separately argues that granting recognition **primacy**, even when supplemented, cannot explain the forces constituting subjects (567–578), and note 3 expressly extends the objection to a rescoped theory. Removing the definitional-monism claim weakens one route to unsalvageability; it does not remove those additional arguments. A stronger reading would test each route separately rather than treat the conclusion as wholly secured by definition.

The checked ledger's D1.F3 remains unverified by the current normalizer, but its quotation is verbatim after joining the source's soft-hyphen (U+00AD) line split; the receipt's paraphrase label is inaccurate. Conversely, the fact that an abstract announces its conclusion (D1.F4) supplies little evidence that the body leaves the conclusion unargued. Front-matter position should not substitute for assessing the ensuing argument.

## Zambrana: a substantive frame audit, followed by a false critic addition

Old F1–F6 identify a real dependence on Zambrana's earlier work: the paper explicitly invokes previous accounts of negativity, action, and necessity (115–131; 168–172). However, old F6's “already prepared readership” and the prose's reputational explanation of revising Rose exceed what self-citation establishes. Old F23–F24 use note 14's Caribbean political-economy references (506–510) to pose a valuable alternative genealogy; the suggestion that these categories could organize the argument is appropriately a counterfactual, not a demonstrated suppressed influence.

Checked F5/F18 preserve the essential distinction: Zambrana adopts Rose's speculative method while revising Rose's judgment of Marx; Marx's methodological resources can exceed his historical scope. This is productive appropriation, not simple inconsistency. F17/F23 also make the frame-removal question concrete: Smallwood's coercive, legal, bureaucratic, and commercial mechanisms can describe Atlantic circuits without necessarily adopting value as an active subject (402–427). F19 locates an actual scope limit in the omission of fictitious capital (485–486). The checked reading therefore finds what the frame enables and which descriptions remain available without it.

Yet checked F24 again subtracts too much. The prose treats slavery's constitutive relation to capitalism, rather than mere historical importance, as collapsing without speculative ontology. Smallwood's identification of the trade as part of capitalism and its circuits as already displaying capital's logic supplies another argument for internality (402–427). Removing Hegel more securely removes the **specific account of actuality and value-as-subject**; it need not remove every historically argued claim that slavery is internal to capitalism. Similarly, the proposed primitive-accumulation rival should not be reduced to a superseded prehistory when the paper cites continuing-expropriation debates (487–492).

The check adds a specific error. Final F25, inherited from critic V.F3, claims that historical evidence comes **entirely** from a single Smallwood lecture video. Source 70–75 cites Smallwood **2020 and 2008**; the bibliography identifies the 2008 book *Saltwater Slavery* (591), and the final prose itself lists book and lecture. The detailed quoted circuit largely comes from the lecture, and the paper presents no original archival inquiry; those narrower propositions are supportable. A verified fragment from note 4 does not establish the exhaustive source claim. This addition makes the final ledger less reliable on that point while leaving the prose unchanged. F26's added commodity-as-hieroglyphic finding is supported by 347–358, but it does not cancel F25's error.

## Chen: a stronger rival reconstruction, insufficiently qualified additions

Both readings find the consequential distinction between explaining collective progress-attribution and justifying progress. Old F8 is grounded in the self-citation of Chen's 2024 formula (523–524), but the prose speculates that alternatives were less attractive because they threatened intellectual continuity. Old F17 calls preservation of the science–society parallel “insistence” even though its anchor is conditional; old F7 correctly preserves that conditional. The baseline's own qualifications therefore limit its personal lock-in story.

Checked F3/F4/F26 relocate the issue to the text's constitutive claim and unspecified majority (525–564). F20 preserves Chen's insistence that reasons and standards remain (574–578), avoiding a crude irrationalist reading. The strongest gain is F22 and the rival section: Jaeggi's criterion concerns **systematic blockage**, not merely absence of problem-solving (313–316). Showing that Nazis or populists construct problems does not by itself show their processes can learn rather than systematically block experience. This directly tests Chen's counterexample using a rival already present in his text.

The five critic additions do not consistently carry that distinction forward. F30 says the Nazi example reveals that Jaeggi's formal criteria cannot discriminate; F31 treats abolition's lost experiences as contradicting enrichment. These are Chen's disputed conclusions (364–377; 457–466), while the final prose and counter-evidence supply reasons to limit them. They should be explicitly attributed and scoped, not allowed to look like independent confirmation of the very inferences under criticism. F29 usefully records Searle's intentionality-relative premise, but substantially overlaps F3/F26 rather than resolving their burden.

Neither version uses note 2 (624–625) to qualify its account of Jaeggi's historicism critique: Chen admits that this part is his clarifying reconstruction, whereas Jaeggi's direct criticism addresses moralism. That is a concrete missed distinction between the opponent's stated position and the critic's reconstructed object.

## Implications for the next tweak

The redesign supports more discriminating, text-facing criticism, but the final deliverable still needs scrutiny at the point where a source-supported observation becomes a necessity or exhaustive claim. “This frame formulates the conclusion” does not show “without this frame no surviving evidence supports it.” The two observed over-subtractions justify revisiting `process.dimensions[key=rivals].questions` and the subtraction item in `process.steps[key=synthesize].brief`: distinguish loss of this formulation or argumentative route from loss of every surviving route to the conclusion. F25 justifies a stronger contextual test of words such as “entirely,” not treating occurrence verification as semantic confirmation. Actual pairwise outcomes remain pending.
