## The sole genealogical row overstates its trajectory

F15, derived from P6.F9, pairs D01E:61's “power of a religious promise” with D22:65's statement that money takes God's place in representing social unity. The prose says the earlier formulation “becomes” the later proposition and that the later chapter “inherits its strongest conclusion as scaffolding.” The paired passages establish related arguments. They do not establish that religious substitution originates later or that the later argument silently presupposes the particular early article.

D01E:21 already describes capital forms as taking “the place of religion in modern societies”; D01E:99 compares money and God as universal symbols. The omitted D01P makes the connection more specific: at 66 money occupies a place close to that reserved for religion, while at 88 it can fill the apparently empty “holy cosmos.” These are consequential counterpassages, not peripheral anticipation. D22:57–67 does expand the account through society's missing observer, worldwide market inclusion, and contingency management. It also explains the mechanism again. Its explicit nearby self-citations point to 2019 (27, 67), outside the supplied corpus. The sound conclusion is continuity with a changed organizing problem and explanatory role; direct transmission through these particular articles remains unproved.

The missing D01P would also have prevented a flattened history of the framework. Its civilizational account explains institutions as imperfect embodiments of cosmological horizons (86–90), and its negative comparison explicitly limits abstract definitions of religion (26–28). D22's restriction to Abrahamic traditions (47) is therefore a qualification to compare with an existing methodological difficulty. It is not a simple late discovery of non-observability. Neither 2001 paper has an established priority over the other.

## What a successful corpus synthesis would have tested

Three source-backed comparisons would materially improve the judgment. First, D01E:77's claim that workers must be free to be creative meets D22:95's explicit acknowledgment of slave labour's historical centrality and its restriction of the cooperation thesis to later industrial revolutions. This is a historical scope qualification, not an announced abandonment. Second, D01P:136's rejection of new utopias persists in D22:87's sobriety and practical containment: there is no simple conversion from optimistic entrepreneurship to late pessimism. Third, D22 still rejects Luhmann's restricted account of money (67), while using internal observation centrally (37–39). The change is selective theoretical use, not wholesale conversion.

The standalone criticism also needs one qualification: F10's initial refusal of a Weberian route does not “forbid” all Weberian causal contributions. D01E:61 explicitly returns to Protestant asceticism, and D22:49 and 67 credit Weber again. Choosing an immediate analytical object is weaker than declaring a rival explanation incompatible.

Similarly, the alleged failure to settle whether monetary contradiction or creative labour generates growth needs a closer argument. D01E:75 explicitly distinguishes capital's claim on absolute wealth from labour's capacity to fulfill it; 77 and 101 explain institutional mediation. Structural compulsion and contingent realization can operate at different levels. Asking what explanatory work the religious comparison adds is productive; requiring these levels to compete for a single causal priority risks manufacturing the hole. The final repair proposal should remain a reader's possible reconstruction, not an established necessity imposed by the source.

The evidence supports revisiting `process.steps[key=synthesize].brief`, whose singular “text” and thematic sequence allowed one document to become the whole object. A bounded corpus instruction should require an evidenced relation involving every supplied document, distinguish recurrence, reargument, presupposition, qualification, and explicit self-citation, and organize the final judgment around those relations. P6's question and method card also need safeguards against treating repeated vocabulary as silent inheritance. Quote and identifier checks remain useful, but neither textual occurrence nor correctly namespaced rows guarantees the relation asserted between documents. This memo proposes no runtime change or fresh invocation.
