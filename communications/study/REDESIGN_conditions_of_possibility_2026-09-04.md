# Redesign: Conditions of Possibility (2026-09-04)

> Designed from the ideal output backwards, without API spend. Evidence base: the study outputs under `data/study/outputs/` (AUKUS paper, Wijaya & Hayes 2025), three rounds of blind judgments, the two readers' memos, and the definition at `src/engines/capability_definitions/conditions_of_possibility_analyzer.yaml`. Premise, per the owner: the current definition is faulty. It was written for an author's corpus ("author with 3-5 prior works", "prolific authors with 10+ works") and for a weaker model; run on one paper it asks the model to write intellectual biography, and the judge punished exactly that ("performs meta-commentary on the paper's intellectual genealogy as an abstract exercise", claims about the authors' intentions the text does not support).

## 0. What the method is for

A conditions-of-possibility reading answers one question: **what had to be already in place for this argument to be sayable, and to be received as a contribution, and what does that scaffolding cost the argument?** Foucault's version asks what rules of formation let these statements count as knowledge; Skinner's asks what the author was *doing* with the available vocabulary, against whom; Lovejoy's isolates the unit-ideas doing the work. None of the three licenses claims about the authors' motives or careers from a single text. All three are readings of *what the text presupposes, borrows, renames, dismisses and cannot see*. The text is the archive. That is the correction that drives everything below.

## 1. The ideal output

### 1a. One paper

An expert reader (a historian of ideas, a critical political economist, an editor deciding whether the paper is a contribution) wants to know, in this order:

1. **The givens.** The premises the text needs and does not argue *here*: concepts used as already defined, debates treated as settled, a causal ontology asserted as description. For the AUKUS paper the reading found one that everything hangs on: neoliberalism redefined as a project of organising capitalist power that survives state intervention, so that subsidies and protected procurement can be "a mutation of neoliberalism" rather than its negation. Anchored: "we see state developmentalism as constitutive of neoliberal globalisation". And what collapses without it: the paper falls into the "crisis of neoliberalism" position it attacks.
2. **The inheritance, declared and undeclared.** Which prior works the text says it extends, adopts, narrows, applies (anchored to the sentences: "Extending Wijaya and Jayasuriya's (2024) work", "Here, we adopt a narrower understanding of conceptions of regulatory control"); which it dismisses and by what device (a label: "geopolitical fetishism"; a group citation; a single sentence); and which traditions supply vocabulary the text uses without citing them (anchored to the vocabulary: "midwifing", "pilot agency", "condensed", "spatial fix", "power container", "weaponised interdependence"), each tagged as a **provenance hypothesis**, never as a claim about what the authors knew or hid.
3. **The conditions visible in the text's own apparatus.** What the evidence consists of and its date window (a stream of 2023-24 announcements: DPA Title III "domestic source", the Defence Trade Controls Amendment Act 2024, AUD 840m for Arafura); what had to happen for the argument to be makeable (Pillar I's "severe delays", which enable the pivot to Pillar II); whose vocabulary the text borrows from its object ("de-risking", "friendshoring", "technological sovereignty") and whether it marks the borrowing; what the venue, title, abstract, keywords and reference list show about the audience the text assumes and the network it cites (the same journal published "geopolitical fetishism" three years earlier; the concept is applied here for the first time; the reference list's self-citation cluster). These are textual facts, not biography.
4. **What the frame lets the text see, and what it cannot metabolise.** The findings only this framework produces (military agencies "now hold the most decisive control over financing mechanisms and procurement"; Pillar II as "a business in its own right"), anchored. And the text's own evidence that explains something by a mechanism the frame does not own: Labor's support attributed to "a fear of being 'wedged' on defence" (electoral, not class); Korea's reforms driven by "the Russia-Ukraine war"; regulatory control defined as "motivated by geopolitics" when geopolitics is supposed to be surface. Plus what the text brackets ("beyond the scope of this paper", "This is not to say that AUKUS ... is completely geopolitically fetishist") and what each bracket protects.
5. **The rivals the text itself makes available, and the counterfactual residue.** The rival readings the reference list and the dismissals name (economic statecraft, Weiss and Thurbon; "crisis of neoliberalism", Gerstle; critical geopolitics, borrowed for one paragraph then demoted); what each would say about the same evidence; what in the text's own commitments rules each out (anchored). Then the subtraction: remove the inherited frame and say which sections survive unchanged (the empirical map of Pillar II beneficiaries, the export-credit machinery, the VC "intermediaries" claim) and which collapse (the "constitutes", the Japan/Korea extension).
6. **The judgment, with the hole and the repair.** Enabling or constraining, on balance, with the specific gain and the specific cost; the *one* hole if there is one (the study's best synthesis: the definitional gap and the causal inversion are the same hole seen from two faces); and the repair stated in the text's own vocabulary ("market-creating actions", "reconstituting the market"), which is what an editor or the authors could act on.

The artifact: a reading of 10-18K characters that argues one line through items 1-6, citing findings by id and quoting anchors, followed by the findings ledger (15-30 rows), counter-evidence and open questions, and two or three tables the desks can lift: the givens (premise, where assumed, what depends on it), the inheritance (work or tradition, relation to it, anchor, declared or hypothesised), the visibility ledger (what the frame sees, what its own evidence cuts against).

### 1b. A five-paper corpus

The corpus is where the method was born and where "path dependence" stops being a metaphor. With five papers by an author or a school the ideal output adds, *evidenced across texts*:

- **Premise drift**: a claim argued in paper 1 and presupposed in paper 5, with both anchors. This is the path-dependence chain the current definition asks for and cannot deliver from one text.
- **Vocabulary drift**: a term defined in one paper, used undefined later, or renamed (rhetorical redescription across the corpus).
- **Debts made checkable**: paper 3's idea presented as new in paper 5, with both anchors, still without motives.
- **Branching points**: a paper that considers and rejects an alternative that a later paper no longer mentions.
- **Network and authority**: the self-citation density per paper and what each self-citation carries (a concept, a finding, a licence to dismiss).
- **The rival that recurs**: which rival framework each paper engages, dismisses or ignores.

Tables become matrices: premise × paper (argued / presupposed / absent), term × paper (defined / undefined / renamed), rival × paper (engaged / dismissed / absent), each cell anchored. The reading is then a genealogy of the position: where the givens were made, where they hardened, and what the latest paper can no longer say.

### 1c. What "depth" means here

Not more passes. Depth is: (a) the givens ledger is *complete*, which a hunt-for-misses pass can check; (b) every finding names the mechanism in the text (the sentence where the given is assumed, the label that does the dismissing, the bracket that protects); (c) the reading finds the one hole rather than a list of tensions; (d) the rivals are the text's own, from its references, not the analyst's favourites; (e) the repair is in the text's vocabulary. Surface depth is items 1, 4 and 6 with a short ledger; standard is 1-6 on one paper; deep is the corpus.

### 1d. What a weak output looks like

Headings that are the dimension list; the same six citations listed under "enabling", "path dependencies" and "debts"; sentences about the authors (their school, their motives for not citing, their reputational stakes, "embarrassment", "strategic concealment"); a proportion ("two-thirds inherited") with no basis; tradition names dropped without a sentence of the text to hang them on; critiques that would fit any Marxist IR paper; process narration ("the architecture pass established"). The study's harness outputs did all of these; the one-shot did the first two.

## 2. The questions that produce it

The current eight dimensions collapse to five plus a synthesis step. Overlaps dropped: enabling / path_dependencies / unacknowledged_debts asked one question three ways (the outputs listed the same citations under each); alternative_paths and counterfactual_analysis were one thought experiment; cross_domain_transfers was undeclared inheritance from another field; synthetic_judgment is the write step, not a dimension. Path dependence survives as a corpus-level dimension (P6) that only runs when there is more than one text by the same hand.

Every question is about the text. Each answer, when anchored, has a fixed shape so the extraction step can return ledger rows and nothing else.

### D1. The givens (what the text presupposes)
- Which terms does the text use as if already defined, and where is the first sentence that relies on that definition doing work?
- Which claims are stated in the abstract or introduction as findings before any evidence is given? Quote the verb ("constitutes", "we argue", "is not simply").
- Which debates does the text treat as settled, signalled by a citation in place of an argument, "as X has shown", "it is well established", or a label in place of engagement?
- What causal order does the text assume (what is "underlying", what is "surface", what "obscures" what) and where is that asserted rather than shown?
- Which of these givens, if withdrawn, would collapse the argument into a position the text opposes?

Answer shape: `[F] The text presupposes <premise> — anchor: "<the sentence that relies on it>" — depends: <what falls without it> — confidence`.

### D2. The inheritance (declared and undeclared)
- Which prior works does the text say it extends, adopts, narrows, applies or corrects? Quote the sentence and name the relation.
- Which works or schools does it dismiss, and by what device: a label, a single sentence, a group citation, "not simply"? Quote the device.
- Which technical vocabulary appears without citation, and which tradition uses those words that way? Name the tradition; tag the row `provenance: hypothesis`; do not say the authors borrowed it knowingly.
- Where does the reference list cite the same authors or their co-authors, and what does each such citation carry into this argument: a concept, a finding, a licence?
- Which frameworks from other fields shape the argument's structure (an optimisation habit, a legal template, a geographical concept applied to a sector)? Anchor to the move, not to the authors' training.

Answer shape: `[F] The text <extends|adopts|narrows|dismisses|uses-without-citing> <work or tradition> for <what it carries> — anchor: "<verbatim>" — provenance: declared|hypothesis — confidence`.

### D3. The conditions in the text's own apparatus (material, discursive, institutional)
- What does the evidence consist of (kinds of source: press releases, legislation, news, interviews) and what date window does it fall in? What had to happen in the world for the argument to be makeable?
- Which of the text's own facts does the argument depend on most (the best fact) and does the text use it?
- Whose vocabulary does the text borrow from the object it analyses (official terms in quotation marks) and does it mark the borrowing or adopt it?
- What do the title, abstract, keywords, journal and section structure show about the audience assumed and the contribution claimed (first application, extension, correction)?
- What does the text say about its own method ("archival sources and secondary data") and what does the evidence actually consist of?

Answer shape: `[F] The argument requires <condition> — anchor: "<verbatim>" — kind: material|discursive|institutional|methodological — confidence`.

### D4. Visibility (what the frame lets the text see, and what it cannot metabolise)
- What does the text find that a reader without its framework would not have looked for? Quote the finding.
- Where does the text's own evidence explain something by a mechanism the framework does not own (electoral, strategic, exogenous, accidental)? Quote it and say what the framework says instead.
- Where does the text's own definition or evidence run against its causal order (the thing that was supposed to be surface appears as motive)? Quote both sides.
- What does the text explicitly bracket ("beyond our scope", "this is not to say", "only partially resembles") and what does each bracket protect?
- Where does the text rename a familiar thing (protectionism as "neoliberal mutation"; consensus as "state form") and what does the renaming gain?

Answer shape: `[F] The frame <sees|cannot metabolise|brackets|renames> <what> — anchor: "<verbatim>" — counter-anchor: "<verbatim, if the text has one>" — confidence`.

### D5. Rivals and the counterfactual residue
- Which rival explanations does the text name (cited then set aside, or attacked)? For each, what would it say about the same evidence?
- What in the text's own commitments rules each rival out? Quote the sentence that forbids it.
- If the inherited framework were removed, which sections and claims survive unchanged, and which collapse? Name them; give the anchor of one survivor and one casualty.
- What would the text have to add to hold its strongest claim against its strongest rival (a criterion, a test, a comparison it could have made from its own material)?

Answer shape: `[F] Rival <name>, available from <anchor of citation or dismissal>, would read <evidence> as <reading>; the text forbids it by "<verbatim>" — confidence` and `[F] Survives|collapses without the frame: <section or claim> — anchor — confidence`.

### P6. Path dependence (corpus only; runs when two or more texts share an author or school)
- Which premises are argued in an earlier text and presupposed in a later one? Both anchors.
- Which terms are defined once and later used undefined or renamed? Both anchors.
- Which alternatives are considered in an earlier text and absent later?
- How does the self-citation carry change across the texts: what each later text takes from each earlier one?

Answer shape: `[F] <premise or term> argued|defined in <doc A> — anchor A — presupposed|undefined|renamed in <doc B> — anchor B — confidence`.

### S. Synthesis (the write step, not a dimension)
Given the verified ledger: the one hole (or the honest statement that there are two); the balance of enabling and constraining with the specific gain and cost; the repair in the text's own vocabulary; and, for a corpus, the genealogy of the position. The synthesis cites findings by id and does not introduce a claim without a ledger row.

## 3. The step sequence

The shape is **extract → verify → synthesize**, with the findings ledger as the only hand-off. The study showed that four re-readings on the same strong model do not beat one call; the redesign changes what the extra calls *do* (different work, not the same reading under a new mood) and *who* does them (cheap models for the parts that are reading with a checklist, one strong model for the part that is judgment).

### Step 1. Extract (cheap model; parallel; one call per dimension; corpus: per document per dimension)
Input: the source, one dimension's method card (see §5) and questions, the anchoring law, the ledger row shape for that dimension. Output: a ledger only (8-20 rows), plus `### Counter-evidence` and `### Open questions`; no essay. The model is told that everything it writes will be checked against the source by code and by a critic, and that an unanchored row is a hypothesis to be labelled as one.
Wall (code): every anchor verified verbatim against the source (`walls.verify_anchor`, trimming allowed); ids unique per dimension (`D1.F3`); rows whose anchor fails are returned to the model once with the failure ("re-anchor or drop"), then dropped. No judgment in the wall.
Why cheap models can do this: the task is reading with a specific question and copying sentences. Luna and DeepSeek V4 Pro cost a tenth to a thirtieth of Fable; five calls in parallel finish in the time of one.
What it hands on: five ledgers (D1-D5), about 60-90 rows before verification.

### Step 2. Verify (mid or cheap model; one call; corpus: one per document, then one across documents)
Input: the source and the merged extraction ledgers. Task, in this order: (a) for every row, read the anchor in its context and rule `confirmed | weakened | rejected` with one line of reason ("the anchor says X, the finding claims Y"); (b) reject any row that is about the authors rather than the text (motive, career, school, what they knew); (c) merge duplicates across dimensions, keeping the better-anchored row and listing the merged ids; (d) **hunt for misses**: for each dimension, what the ledgers do not contain that the text supports, added as new rows `V.F<n>` with anchors; (e) name the three rows the synthesis must not lose. Output: the verified ledger.
Wall (code): anchors of new rows verified; every referenced id exists; no row status missing.
Where this earns its cost: whenever extraction ran on a cheap model (stretched anchors, over-reading), whenever the method invites over-reading (this one does), and on a corpus (cross-document consistency). Where it does not: when extraction ran on Fable or Sonnet and the desk will re-verify anchors anyway; the frontier study's condition (d) (cheap read, strong write, no critic) tests precisely this.

### Step 3. Synthesize (strong model; one call)
Input: the source and the verified ledger (no extraction prose exists, so there is nothing to narrate). Task: write the reading of §1a for a reader, one line of argument through the givens, the inheritance, the apparatus, the visibility and the rivals, to the hole and the repair; cite findings inline by id (`[F7]`) and quote their anchors; then close with the final `## Findings ledger` (renumbered `F1..Fn`, each row carrying `from: D1.F3, V.F2` so the receipt is traceable), `### Counter-evidence`, `### Open questions`, and `### Tables` naming which finding ids form each of the two or three tables of §1a. Rules: no claim without a ledger row; no sentence about the authors' minds; no process narration.
Wall (code): every id cited in the prose exists in the final ledger; every final anchor verifies; the ledger heading matches `split_ledger`.
Cost: one strong call on ~30K input tokens and 5-6K output.

### Corpus variant
Extract per document per dimension (5 docs × 5 dims = 25 cheap calls, parallel); P6 runs across the merged per-document ledgers (the cheap model reads ledgers, not five sources); verify per document, then one cross-document verify over the P6 rows with the sources available; synthesize once with all verified ledgers and the sources (1M context on the strong model). The corpus reading costs about the price of one Fable one-shot on one paper.

### What the sequence hands from step to step

```
- [D1.F3] The text presupposes that neoliberalism is a project of organising capitalist power rather than a market doctrine — anchor: "we see state developmentalism as constitutive of neoliberal globalisation" — depends: the "mutation" thesis; without it the paper is the "crisis of neoliberalism" position — confidence: high
- [V.F2] MISS (D4): regulatory control is defined as "motivated by geopolitics, which in turn reshapes market mechanisms", which makes geopolitics a motive where the frame needs it to be surface — anchor: "motivated by geopolitics, which in turn reshapes market mechanisms" — confidence: high
- [F1] The definitional gap and the causal inversion are one hole: the text needs geopolitics to be surface and its own definition makes it motive — anchor: "motivated by geopolitics, which in turn reshapes market mechanisms" — from: D1.F3, V.F2 — confidence: high
```

## 4. How the desks consume it

- **The spine** (`src/dossier/spine.py`) reads the final pass's prose and plans anchors per section. With the ledger as contract it reads the ledger first: a section's `claim` is a finding or a group of findings, its `anchors_planned` are copied from ledger anchors (already verified, so the spine's own wall trims nothing), and the section keeps the ids so the composed dossier can show "[F7]" in its receipts. The `buried_crux` field of the read is the synthesis's "one hole" row.
- **Tables** (`src/dossier/tables.py`) are built from ledger rows, not re-discovered from prose: the synthesis's `### Tables` block names the row set (`table: givens — rows: F1, F3, F5, F9`), and the tables desk turns those rows into cells, copying the anchors. The wall still checks verbatim membership; it should now drop nothing.
- **Figures** (`src/dossier/figures.py`) take findings that carry a relation (`depends:`, `survives|collapses`, `rival → reading`) or a number; the D1 dependency rows give a dependency diagram, the D5 rows a survivor/casualty split.
- **Under the hood** on the desk shows the extraction and verification ledgers as receipts (which cheap model found what, what the critic rejected and why), never as the product.
- **The story desk and downstream engines** consume the final ledger, which is what `consumes_from` should carry between engines; the prose is the reasoning.

## 5. Lineage as method cards (what the names make the model do)

The definition currently renders lineage as five tradition names and seven concept names; the `intellectual_grounding.method` and `indicators` blocks that would make the model *look* like the tradition are not rendered at all. A method card is two to four imperative sentences per step, plus the indicators to hunt for.

**Foucault, archaeology (D1, D3).** Do: list what the text treats as sayable without argument; for each, find the sentence that constitutes the object (what "AUKUS" is made to name; who is authorised to "observe"); note the rules of the venue (what an abstract in this journal must claim). Indicators: technical vocabulary without definition; an argument that begins mid-conversation; a citation where an argument would be; methodological moves executed as if naturalised.

**Foucault, genealogy (D4, D5).** Do: find contingency presented as necessity ("constitutes", "underlying"); find where the text's own evidence gives another origin for the same thing; treat every "obscures" as a claim to test against the text's own causal sentences. Indicators: a mechanism the frame does not own explaining a key case; a definition that contradicts the frame's ordering; a rival cited then set aside.

**Skinner, what the text is doing (D2, D4).** Do: for each dismissal, name the target and the device (label, group citation, "not simply") and ask what the move accomplishes in this venue; for each renaming, name the familiar thing renamed and what the redescription gains; attribute nothing beyond the text's own vocabulary; label every motive a hypothesis. Indicators: "not simply X"; a coined label doing the work of an argument; a hedge that concedes the rival's point while keeping the frame ("this is not to say").

**Lovejoy, unit-ideas (D2).** Do: isolate the conceptual building blocks the argument runs on (state as condensation, spatial fix, power to structure, pilot agency); for each, name the tradition that uses it that way and the sentence where it works here; note when a unit-idea from another field is made to carry a load it was not built for (a geographical concept applied to a sector). Indicators: a term in scare quotes attributed to no one; a concept invoked with a citation but without its conditions.

**Path dependence (P6, corpus only).** Do: for each premise, find where it was argued and where it became a given; measure nothing you cannot anchor twice.

## 6. What changes from the current definition, in one table

| current | redesign |
|---|---|
| 8 dimensions, 3 of them the same question | 5 text-facing dimensions + 1 corpus dimension + synthesis |
| questions about the author's prior work, motives, reputation | questions about what the text presupposes, cites, borrows, dismisses, brackets, renames |
| "proportion novel vs inherited" | survivor / casualty list under subtraction of the frame, anchored |
| reasons for non-acknowledgment (embarrassment, concealment) | provenance tagged `hypothesis`; no motives |
| lineage as names | method cards rendered per step |
| four stances re-reading the whole text | extract per dimension (cheap, parallel) → verify and hunt for misses → one strong synthesis |
| prose hand-off, ledger appended | ledger is the hand-off; prose only in the final step |

## 7. What the frontier study should confirm or refute

- That the rewritten questions in one call beat the old questions in one call on the same model (paper 1 has the old-questions one-shot on disk).
- That extract-verify-synthesize on cheap models plus one strong synthesis matches or beats the strong one-shot at a fraction of the cost.
- That the verify step changes the final reading (rows rejected, misses added that reach the synthesis); if it does not, drop it for this engine.
- That anchor verification rates (code, not judge) are higher under the ledger-only extraction than under prose passes.
