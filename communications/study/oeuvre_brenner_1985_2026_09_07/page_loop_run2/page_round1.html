The warrant threading all four labels: 1977 already rejected trade-led transformation; the focal text sharpens the problem; 1986 and later supply the mechanism. See the verdict chips [L2.F1].

What the paper culminates

the focal text codifies, not discovers, an anti-commercialization argument already won by 1977 [L1.F3]

[L2.F4] pull-quote · the paper's own words at the point the memo stakes its "culmination" claim

Read backwards, the focal text does not discover the anti-commercialization argument; it codifies an argument already won. It inherits the 1972 claim that commercial opportunities acquire effects only through socially structured actors, and generalizes that objection into a critique of exchange as an automatic solvent of property relations [retrospective_reading/F1]. The 1972 study, the 1976 agrarian-class-structure study, and above all the 1977 critique — which states flatly that trade cannot determine the transformation of class relations — together establish the negative thesis the focal paper inherits rather than invents. What the focal text adds is not the priority of class and property relations over demographic and commercial forces, already established in 1976, but a restatement of that priority as a theory of relations that structure societal reproduction [retrospective_reading/F2]. Its distinctive move is exegetical: it relocates an established anti-Smithian argument inside Marx's own oeuvre, diagnosing an early model that repeatedly assumes capitalist mechanisms — competitive production, market dependence, self-transforming private property — in order to explain capitalism's emergence. Its own positive conclusion remains narrow: it enables the problem of transition to be posed "in an unprecedentedly sharp manner," not solved. The pull-quote captures this posture directly, mid-argument about the second model of the rise of capitalism [L2.F4].

What it seeds

the focal text poses a problem that 1986, 2001, 2006 and 2007 texts answer with a reproduction-rules mechanism [L1.F4]

Read forwards, the focal article poses a problem it does not itself answer: if commerce cannot dissolve feudal relations automatically, how do lords and peasants transform those relations while attempting to reproduce themselves as feudal classes? Because the text identifies itself as Part I only, the collection cannot show whether its own announced continuation supplied the answer [prospective_reading/F2]. Four held texts supply it instead, in sequence. em:XTTG43Z5/1986 formalizes the focal text's reproductive conception of property relations into an explicit causal theory: property form structures rules of reproduction, which in aggregate produce development or non-development [prospective_reading/F2]. em:ATQRKQMC/2001 gives that formalization comparative historical flesh, showing capitalist relations emerging from feudal actors executing feudal reproduction strategies. em:PWVE5QPN/2006 states the result most sharply: transition is the unintended consequence of feudal agents pursuing feudal ends in feudal ways. em:C3E3GHEL/2007 codifies the whole chain — property relations, reproduction rules, aggregate patterns, crises — as one causal sequence. Together these four texts answer the single question the 1985 article leaves open. The idea map, in-to-out from unsolved problem to mechanism, would trace exactly this sequence [L2.F5].

The rupture test: persists vs. breaks

the paper is a deepening — method changes, the explanatory object does not [L1.F5]

[L2.F6] two-halves · whether the paper is a break, and exactly what changes at it, without reading the ledger

The rupture test asks whether the focal date marks a break in method, object, question, or interlocutors, or whether the explanatory object simply persists under a changed method. The verdict is deepening, not rupture [epistemic_rupture/F1]. What persists: social and property relations remain the principal means of explaining how demographic and commercial forces produce divergent developmental outcomes, from the 1976 agrarian study through the 2007 synthesis. The claim that trade cannot by itself transform pre-capitalist relations into capitalism persists from 1977 through 1989. Transition remains the transformation of property relations through class conflict both before and after, merely specified later as an unintended outcome of feudal reproduction strategies. Market dependence persists as the condition making competitive accumulation systematic. Independent rank-and-file organization persists as the alternative to reformist officialdom, entirely outside the transition thread. What breaks is narrower: the focal text temporarily substitutes exegetical reconstruction of Marx for comparative causal history before later work returns to the same property-relational problem, and the transition argument's apparatus shifts from class-conflict outcomes to feudal reproduction rules, a specification the focal text poses but does not complete. The 1972–1984/1985–2025 periodization is the collection's most defensible reading of that persistence and that break. Two established figures are carried into new roles across the focal date. Marx moves from supplying the settled contrast between free-wage-labour capitalism and pre-capitalist class systems to becoming the internally divided object of a two-model reconstruction, his later work now grounding transition in property relations [citation_shift/F6]. Smith moves from being an external target of market-centred transition theory to becoming the diagnostic source for early Marx's own model, since early Marx is shown closely mirroring Smith's mechanism [citation_shift/F7]. Several sources enter the record without falling inside the collection's own verification: The Poverty of Philosophy is first cited to delimit the early corpus for the first model [citation_shift/F1]; Cohen's Karl Marx's Theory of History is first cited as a rival functionalist strategy, later picked up in 1986 [citation_shift/F2]; Social Science and the Ignoble Savage documents the four-stages framework but is never held [citation_shift/F3, F9]; Meek is cited for the same genealogy and likewise sits outside the Referee [citation_shift/F4, F11]; minor Marx texts — the Communist Manifesto, Pre-Capitalist Economic Formations — supply passages the collection cannot itself check [citation_shift/F8, F10].
