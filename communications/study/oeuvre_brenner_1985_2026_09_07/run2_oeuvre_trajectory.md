# Reading

## Position map

The supplied collection contains 58 dated texts. It is substantial but not the whole oeuvre. It lacks, among other writings named in the profiles, **“Dobb on the Transition from Feudalism to Capitalism” (1978), “The Agrarian Roots of European Feudalism” (1982), “The Rises and Declines of Serfdom” (1996),** and **“Property Relations and the Growth of Agricultural Productivity” (1997)**, as well as other uncollected writings, interviews, and collaborations. Every supplied document can be assessed, although only the focal text is supplied in full; representations of the others rely on their checked profiles and verified passages. Texts sharing a year are not placed in developmental order.

## The focal text’s place

**Position label: middle—theoretical hinge.** *Marx’s First Model of the Transition to Capitalism* neither inaugurates nor completes the transition agenda. The 1976–78 works had already made trade subordinate to class relations and demanded an account of divergent class conflict. The focal text’s distinctive act is reconstructive: it redescribes that prior anti-Smithian argument as a conflict between two historical materialisms within Marx. Its first model moves from productive forces and exchange through the division of labour to class and property; its second begins from property relations that organize social reproduction and therefore makes their transformation the actual historical problem.

The later work formalizes and applies this distinction. *The Social Basis of Economic Development* recasts property relations as rules for reproduction; *Bourgeois Revolution and Transition to Capitalism* uses the distinction to reassess the English Revolution; later comparative works extend it to the Low Countries, the Yangzi delta, feudalism, and Mann’s European Dynamic. The same-year *Agrarian Roots of European Capitalism* cannot be placed before or after the focal text. Accordingly, the focal should not be called the first statement of the mature position. It is the moment when an already operating class-relational method becomes an explicit argument about what historical materialism must explain. [F12]

Across the supplied collection, the most persistent position is that opportunities, techniques, institutions, policies, and ideologies do not explain their own realization. Their consequences depend on social relations that determine how classes and organizations reproduce themselves. The principal turns change the object and scale of that method—from merchants and agrarian classes to reformist organizations, bureaucratic systems, world profitability, interstate power, and political capitalism. The focal text sits at the conceptual centre of that continuity: it states why a transition theory must explain the transformation of property relations rather than smuggle capitalist behaviour into pre-capitalist society.

### Open questions

- The supplied texts do not settle whether political capitalism will remain an auxiliary circuit of capitalist accumulation or consolidate into a more distinct configuration.
- The precise degree of autonomy assigned to merchant capital between the 1972 study and the later agrarian account cannot be fully reconstructed without the omitted transition writings.
- The relation between the focal text and *The Agrarian Roots of European Capitalism* cannot be developmental rather than comparative because both are dated 1985.
- Conclusions concern this 58-text sample, not every publication in the oeuvre.
