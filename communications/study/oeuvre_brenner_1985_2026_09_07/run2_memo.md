### Formation before 1985

### The focal hinge and immediate codification

- **em:CBT7B8CL/1985, *Marx’s First Model of the Transition to Capitalism***: early Marx’s Smithian division-of-labour model and later Marx’s social-property model are incompatible. Its distinctive role is exegetical codification: it relocates an established anti-Smithian argument inside Marx’s oeuvre. Its decisive qualification is incompleteness—it is Part I and sharpens rather than solves the positive problem of transition.
- **em:9RJCZ6KQ/1985, *The Agrarian Roots of European Capitalism***: divergent agrarian class structures explain divergent European responses to feudal crisis. - **em:3JSL5XP4/1986, *Austerity & Interventionism***: military Keynesianism and intervention temporarily sustain demand while deepening industrial decline. It connects economic policy to socialist strategy; stimulus is effective in the short run but self-undermining.

## The paper read backwards: what it culminates

Read backwards, *Marx’s First Model* culminates an argument already established through historical controversy. The 1972 study had made reactions to commercial opportunity depend on merchant groups’ social and political character. The 1976 article made class power determine the effects of demographic and commercial change. Most decisively, the 1977 critique stated that trade cannot determine the transformation of class relations. The objects move from English merchants and comparative agrarian structures to Marx’s texts, but the inferential direction persists: opportunities, exchange, and productive forces cannot explain their own social conditions.

The focal paper’s distinctive contribution is therefore not the discovery of social-property explanation. It is an **exegetical codification** of that explanation. Its object is the coexistence of two incompatible transition models in Marx; its scope is the movement from feudalism to capitalism; and its diagnostic role is to show that the early model repeatedly assumes capitalist mechanisms—competitive production, market dependence, and self-transforming private property—in order to explain capitalism’s emergence. Its positive conclusion is narrower: later Marx “enables us to pose the problem of transition in an unprecedentedly sharp manner”.

This backward reading also defines the paper’s limits. The political-strategic route—from *A New Social Democracy?* through the reformism, election, and rank-and-file writings—crosses 1985 without relying on Marx exegesis. Its continuing insistence on independent organization is a parallel application of class-relational explanation, not an inheritance demonstrably produced by this article. The focal text codifies one central historical-theoretical programme without reorganizing every branch of the oeuvre.

## The paper read forwards: what it seeds

Read forwards, the paper is a way station between an established negative argument and a later positive mechanism. Its outstanding question is precise: if commerce does not dissolve feudal relations automatically, how can lords and peasants transform those relations while attempting to reproduce themselves as feudal classes? Because the published text identifies itself only as Part I, it does not establish whether its own projected continuation supplied that answer.

The immediate abstract development comes in em:XTTG43Z5/1986, *The Social Basis of Economic Development*. There the argument is formalized as a movement from property relations through actors’ rules for reproduction to long-term development or non-development. This is not merely repeated terminology. The focal article diagnoses why productive-force and exchange explanations fail; the 1986 chapter supplies the mediating level—socially rational reproduction strategies—through which property structures generate aggregate outcomes.

The historical mechanism then becomes explicit. em:ATQRKQMC/2001, *The Low Countries in the Transition to Capitalism*, says that capitalist social-property relations had to result from feudal actors carrying out feudal rules of reproduction. em:PWVE5QPN/2006, *The Origins of Capitalism*, states the result still more sharply as the unintended consequence of pursuing feudal goals in feudal ways. The 1977 formulation had located transition in divergent outcomes of class conflict; the later formulation explains how actions oriented toward reproducing an existing order can unintentionally undermine it. That is the principal conceptual deepening around which the focal paper stands.

The article also clarifies a contrast later used outside transition history: exchange is not the same thing as market dependence. Once capitalist social-property relations compel producers to exchange competitively, competition produces systematic innovation and labour-process transformation. The long-downturn writings presuppose this condition and ask about its contradictory aggregate effects—overinvestment, overcapacity, falling profitability, and failed adjustment. Their immediate object and evidential route differ from those of the focal article, so conceptual continuity does not establish direct descent.

The same caution applies to political capitalism. The 2025 synthesis argues that capitalism’s productivity-enhancing investment dynamic can itself produce secular stagnation. That is compatible with the focal concern for unintended aggregate outcomes, but the supplied evidence does not show whether it was derived from the 1985 exegesis or independently developed within the broader social-property framework.

## The paper read whole: joined verdict

Read whole, em:CBT7B8CL/1985 is best placed as a **middle-period exegetical reorientation, retrospective culmination, prospective way station, and theoretical deepening—not an epistemic rupture**.

It is a **culmination** because the decisive anti-Smithian claim precedes it: trade cannot itself transform relations of production. It is a **reorientation** because it changes immediate method and object, moving from comparative causal history to sustained interpretation of early and later Marx. It is a **way station** because it sharply poses a problem that the held later texts formulate through rules of reproduction and unintended consequences. It is a **deepening** because the direction of explanation persists while the mediating mechanism becomes more explicit.

What persists across the collection is not vocabulary alone. In the earlier historical work, class relations determine how demographic and commercial pressures affect development. In the mature codification, social-property relations impose rules of reproduction whose aggregate execution generates developmental patterns and crises. The object of inference remains the relationship between historically specific social structures, individually rational conduct, and collective outcomes.

What breaks is more limited. The focal text foregrounds Marx’s internal development and the incompatibility between two historical materialisms. Later works return to comparative history, extend comparison to the Low Countries and Yangzi delta, and develop distinct routes concerning bureaucratic collectivism, capitalist crisis, imperialism, political strategy, and political capitalism. Those routes do not all presuppose the focal paper. The political writings continue a pre-1985 argument about self-activity; the long-downturn works reargue capitalist competition through profitability and fixed capital; the political-capitalism writings extend analysis to state-mediated rents. Direct inheritance from the focal article remains unestablished.

Across the supplied collection, the position is therefore continuous but increasingly differentiated. The earliest works reject self-acting opportunities and locate causal power in social groups, property relations, and class conflict. The focal article gives that position its clearest Marxian genealogy and its sharpest statement of the unresolved transition problem. Later works supply the reproduction-rule and unintended-consequence mechanism, expand the comparison, and carry related explanatory principles into other modes of production and contemporary capitalism.

The most defensible periodization is consequently **1972–84: formation and application of class-relational explanation; 1985–2025: explicit codification, comparative enlargement, and extension**. This is a judgment about the supplied corpus. The unheld Part II could alter the focal paper’s prospective placement, and the collection cannot establish the chronology of formulations in omitted work.

## Open question

The principal unresolved question is whether the announced Part II existed, circulated, or appeared under another title. If it contained the rules-of-reproduction and unintended-consequence mechanism, the focal project itself was closer to a mature culmination; if not, the published article remains chiefly a problem-sharpening way station.

A second question concerns the later independent routes. The collection establishes compatibility between the focal concern with socially structured reproduction and the later account of capitalist investment producing stagnation, but it does not establish a compositional or citational line between them. Drafts, composition records, or explicit retrospective citations would decide whether the long-downturn and political-capitalism programmes inherited the focal argument or independently extended the broader social-property framework.
