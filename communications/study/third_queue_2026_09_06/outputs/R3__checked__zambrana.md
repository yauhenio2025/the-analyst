# Reading

The article’s central vocal distinction is between **Rose’s judgment about Marx** and **Zambrana’s “with and against Rose” reconstruction**. Rose supplies the methodological authority: speculative thinking exposes rather than erases actuality’s lack of identity. Yet her claim that Marx retains a thought/actuality opposition becomes the article’s principal objection. Zambrana adopts Rose’s method while reversing that assessment, arguing that Marx’s account of self-valorizing value is itself speculative [F1] [F2].

Smallwood then provides the decisive historical extension. Her account of the circulation of African captives gives Zambrana grounds for treating slavery not as capital’s prehistory but as constitutive of the money-form and capital relation [F10] [F11]. Hegel, Marx, Lukács, and Ramas San Miguel appear mostly through quotation or reported interpretation: Hegel authorizes the account of actuality; Marx supplies the concepts of fetishism, value, and self-valorization; Lukács supplies a restricted position under criticism; and Ramas San Miguel supplies the distinction between fetishism and mystification that helps revise that position [F3] [F5] [F6] [F7] [F8].

### Counter-evidence

Zambrana does not simply reject Rose: Rose’s speculative account remains the article’s methodological framework [F1] [F2]. Nor is Lukács dismissed without qualification; the narrator says, “Certainly, there is much truth to this rendering of Marx,” before identifying its limitation. Finally, the Marx–Smallwood relation is constructed by Zambrana; the supplied text does not establish a direct exchange between them.
