### Overall assessment

Castoriadis’s periodization is strongest when it identifies institutional mechanisms rather than isolated dates. The emergence of capitalism is appropriately treated as an “enigmatic synergy of a host of factors,” and the postwar boom is tied concretely to “regular wage increases and state regulation of overall demand.” His account also handles continuity well: the new globalized phase does not invalidate earlier characteristics, because “all the foregoing remains true.”

The principal weakness is temporal inconsistency around the 1970s. The “classical” phase lasts “until around 1975,” but the “present phase” begins “since approximately the early 1970s.” Elsewhere the decisive political rupture is placed “since the early 1980s.” These dates can be reconciled only by positing an unstated transition from roughly the mid-1960s through the early 1980s. The broad category of classical capitalism is also internally overloaded, encompassing nineteenth-century laissez-faire, labor reform, the Depression, intervention after 1933, and the postwar regulated boom.

### Counter-evidence

- The capitalist/precapitalist contrast is softened by the admission that accumulation and large-scale exploitation predate capitalism: “Hoarding is practiced in many historical societies.”
- The drive toward mastery cannot itself serve as an absolute capitalist boundary because it is “not…something exclusively specific to capitalism.”
- Markets also cannot mark capitalism’s simple beginning because the market is described as “an institutional mechanism of the greatest antiquity.”
- The post-1933 interventionist rupture was incomplete: the capitalist establishment “violently combated these crazy innovations.”
- The postwar settlement did not end suddenly; inflationary pressure “starting in the 1960s” coexisted with continued expansion and regulation.
- The early-1980s neoliberal rupture did not eliminate the state but redirected it through central banks, budget rules, and liberalization policies.
- The essay’s global phase retains earlier structures: “all the foregoing remains true,” explicitly favoring layered continuity over total rupture.
