## Analysis

### `concept_analyzed`

- **term:** Capitalism, especially money-capital as a secular religion promising “absolute wealth.”
- **metaphor_density:** **saturated** — the argument is built through sustained mappings among capitalism, religion, divinity, machinery, movement, and mythic cycles.
- **dominant_source_domain:** **Religion** — capitalism is framed not merely as resembling religion but as possessing an “essentially religious” character.

---

### `root_metaphors`

#### RM1 — **CAPITALISM IS A RELIGION**

- **source_domain:** Religion: cult, worship, faith, salvation, debt, transcendence.
- **target_domain:** Capitalism as a social order and motivational system.
- **prevalence:** dominant
- **mappings:**
  - **cult → continuous capitalist activity:** Capitalism is a “‘purely cultic’ religion.”
  - **continuous worship → uninterrupted economic discipline:** “the cult is permanent and uninterrupted by ‘workdays’.”
  - **salvation → financial success/absolute wealth:** Market radicalism emphasizes “financial ‘salvation’.”
  - **sin or guilt → debt:** “The cult of capitalism does not lead to salvation but to an ever-increasing indebtedness.”
  - **faith → confidence in money and innovations:** Money is anchored in “generalized ‘trust’ or ‘faith’.”
  - **religious myth → technological, organizational, and consumption paradigm:** “Sacred scripts as well as economic myths lay the ground for ‘rational’ action.”
  - **transcendence → growth beyond existing limits:** Limits must be “constructed, surmounted and reconstructed in the never-ending process of ‘growth’.”
- **entailments:**
  - Capitalist participation satisfies existential as well as material needs because it answers the same “‘sorrows, pains, anxieties’” once addressed by religion.
  - Economic rationality rests on non-rational commitments because capitalist myths “lack any rational foundation by themselves.”
  - Growth is an obligation rather than an optional policy because “Capital can only choose between growth or decline.”
  - Capitalism secularizes transcendence by turning it into the expansion of terrestrial possibilities: a “radically this-worldly utopia.”
- **hidings:**
  - The frame may understate capitalism’s legal, administrative, and coercive specificity by translating heterogeneous institutions into components of one religion.
  - It can blur differences between voluntary faith and material dependence, although the text partly restores them through the poor’s experience of money as “a disciplining medium.”
  - It risks making capitalism appear culturally unified despite the claim that empirical markets “exist only in the plural.”
- **quotes:**
  - “capitalism as a religion”
  - “an ‘essentially religious’ character”
  - “The cult of capitalism does not lead to salvation but to an ever-increasing indebtedness.”

#### RM2 — **MONEY IS GOD / DIVINE OMNIPOTENCE**

- **source_domain:** Divinity, omnipotence, sacred representation, hidden god.
- **target_domain:** Money as generalized capacity and social power.
- **prevalence:** dominant
- **mappings:**
  - **God beyond opposites → money combining heterogeneous possibilities:** Money is “located beyond all opposites of the world.”
  - **divine omnipotence → command over options:** Money promises “individual command over absolute wealth, over the totality of human possibilities.”
  - **God represented by symbols → money identical with represented value:** “Just as money *is* what it symbolizes, ‘god’ represents no other reality than that attributed to the symbol itself.”
  - **faith in God → trust in money:** “Money as well as god is anchored…in…‘trust’ or ‘faith’.”
  - **hidden deity → indeterminate market success:** Market radicalism preaches “the idea of *deus absconditus*.”
  - **god of a domain → universal exchangeability:** Money is “‘the god of commodities’…because it provides the key for almost everything.”
- **entailments:**
  - Money derives authority from collectively sustained belief rather than an intrinsic commodity substance.
  - Ownership of money approximates omnipotence because it gives “the option to have options.”
  - Monetary power reaches beyond goods into “social power, time and space.”
- **hidings:**
  - Divine language can obscure the institutional production and unequal distribution of monetary authority.
  - It can imply equal access to money’s generalized possibilities, though the article stresses that “a given quantity of money means utterly different things to different people.”
- **quotes:**
  - “the god of commodities”
  - “Money as well as god”
  - “divine omnipotence and absolute wealth”

#### RM3 — **MONEY IS A KEY / TICKET / CARRIER**

- **source_domain:** Instruments granting access or transporting claims.
- **target_domain:** Money’s capacity to open choices and move rights across contexts.
- **prevalence:** common
- **mappings:**
  - **key → access to objects:** Money is “the key for the individual to get control over the world of objects.”
  - **key → social autonomy:** It provides a “combined key” to wealth, freedom, time, and space.
  - **ticket → access to goods and services:** Money is “not just a ticket for goods and services.”
  - **carrier → portable property claims:** It is “a carrier of generalized individual property rights.”
  - **condensed portable object → claims detached from personal ties:** Actors carry claims “‘along with him in a condensed, potential form’.”
- **entailments:**
  - Money opens domains otherwise separated by material, social, spatial, and temporal barriers.
  - Its fungibility emancipates individuals from inherited dependence while also enabling impersonal debt.
- **hidings:**
  - The neutral instrument frame conceals that keys are unequally distributed.
  - “Ticket” language makes access appear transactional and voluntary, downplaying compulsion to sell labour.
- **quotes:**
  - “the key for getting control of almost everything”
  - “a combined key”
  - “a carrier of generalized individual property rights”

#### RM4 — **CAPITAL IS A MACHINE IN MOTION**

- **source_domain:** Machinery, engines, circulation, dynamic systems.
- **target_domain:** Capital accumulation and systemic reproduction.
- **prevalence:** common
- **mappings:**
  - **machinery → capitalist system:** The capital–labour tension “keeps the capitalist machinery in motion.”
  - **motion → accumulation:** Money must “flow back…with an increment.”
  - **engine stopping → economic depression:** Creative destruction may “grind to a halt.”
  - **driving force → growth imperative:** Capitalism “revolves around and is driven by the capital properties of money.”
  - **sclerosis → institutional rigidity:** The economy approaches “rationally crystallized ‘sclerosis’.”
- **entailments:**
  - Capitalism requires movement; stasis becomes decline.
  - Labour and innovation supply the energy for accumulation.
  - Institutional stabilization eventually impedes further profitability.
- **hidings:**
  - Mechanical necessity can obscure agency and political choice.
  - The machinery image risks treating conflict and suffering as functional components rather than contestable relations.
- **quotes:**
  - “keeps the capitalist machinery in motion”
  - “flow back…with an increment”
  - “grind to a halt”

#### RM5 — **CAPITALIST DEVELOPMENT IS A CYCLE / WAVE / SPIRAL**

- **source_domain:** Natural and temporal recurrence: cycles, waves, rise and decline.
- **target_domain:** Innovation, institutionalization, crisis, and renewal.
- **prevalence:** dominant in the innovation analysis
- **mappings:**
  - **birth/generation → invention of a paradigm:** Myths undergo “generation-diffusion-institutionalization and decline.”
  - **diffusion → expansion of adoption:** Communication “accelerates the diffusion of the myth.”
  - **crystallization → stabilization into standard knowledge:** “The crystallization of the paradigm” attracts investment.
  - **decline → loss of innovative aura:** The paradigm “can no longer open up new horizons.”
  - **new cycle → creative destruction:** “The decline of the old myth thus becomes the starting point of a new one.”
  - **waves → clusters of technological change:** The text invokes “long waves.”
- **entailments:**
  - Rational order is temporary rather than capitalism’s permanent essence.
  - Myths are productive during emergence but lose profitability when institutionalized.
  - Crisis can generate renewal by activating the prior paradigm’s “blind spots.”
- **hidings:**
  - Cyclical language may naturalize crises and make their social costs seem inevitable.
  - Recurrent form can obscure irreversible ecological or social damage.
- **quotes:**
  - “generation-diffusion-institutionalization and decline”
  - “The decline of the old myth…becomes the starting point of a new one.”
  - “the myth cycle”

#### RM6 — **THE ENTREPRENEUR IS A PROPHET, PRIEST, OR REVOLUTIONARY**

- **source_domain:** Religious leadership and political revolution.
- **target_domain:** Entrepreneurial interpretation and mobilization under uncertainty.
- **prevalence:** common
- **mappings:**
  - **prophet → interpreter of transcendental promise:** The entrepreneur “interprets the promise of absolute wealth.”
  - **priestly mediation → manageable economic projects:** He transforms monetary inexhaustibility “into a form that is manageable by social action.”
  - **revolutionary → destroyer and creator of structures:** The entrepreneur is the “‘revolutionary of the economy’.”
  - **congregation → workers, financiers, customers, and supporters:** He must “mobilize his social environment.”
  - **religious movement → innovation coalition:** He needs to forge “‘coalitions’ in favour of his projects.”
- **entailments:**
  - Entrepreneurship is interpretive and charismatic, not simply calculative.
  - Innovation depends on collective belief and mobilization under uncertainty.
  - Experts and consultants function as secondary clergy interpreting capitalist myths.
- **hidings:**
  - The prophetic frame risks heroizing entrepreneurs.
  - The article counters this by stating that innovation is “always a social process, never a purely individual performance.”
- **quotes:**
  - “comparable with the role of prophets and priests”
  - “interprets the promise of absolute wealth”
  - “‘revolutionary of the economy’”

#### RM7 — **ECONOMIC PARADIGMS ARE MYTHS**

- **source_domain:** Myth, sacred narrative, charisma, discipleship, ritual orientation.
- **target_domain:** Technological, organizational, and consumption visions.
- **prevalence:** dominant
- **mappings:**
  - **myth → paradigm lacking external rational foundation:** Paradigms “lack any rational foundation by themselves.”
  - **faith → commitment to innovation:** “‘faith’ of the promoters and their disciples” is vital.
  - **disciples → adopters and cooperating actors:** Innovations spread through promoters’ capacity “to communicate it to the larger society.”
  - **sacred script → technical or organizational model:** “Sacred scripts as well as economic myths lay the ground for ‘rational’ action.”
  - **loss of aura → institutional maturity:** A paradigm loses “its original innovative aura.”
- **entailments:**
  - Innovations become economically real through socially resonant expectations.
  - Myth is generative rather than merely false.
  - Rational action operates within symbolically selected horizons.
- **hidings:**
  - “Myth” may blur differences between empirically testable technologies and theological narratives.
  - It may underplay material effectiveness, though the author says myths link “a concrete technological artefact” with imagined possibilities.
- **quotes:**
  - “economic ‘myths’”
  - “‘faith’ of the promoters and their disciples”
  - “its original innovative aura”

#### RM8 — **ABSOLUTE WEALTH IS A DRUG / COLLECTIVE OBSESSION**

- **source_domain:** Addiction and psychopathology.
- **target_domain:** Society’s attachment to unlimited monetary possibility.
- **prevalence:** occasional but decisive in the conclusion
- **mappings:**
  - **drug → promise of absolute wealth:** The alternative is “the weaning off the drug of absolute wealth.”
  - **obsession → collective pursuit of omnipotence:** Society must take leave of “the collective obsession with divine omnipotence and absolute wealth.”
  - **disillusionment → emancipation:** A critique of money becomes “the true religious disillusion of society.”
- **entailments:**
  - Capitalist desire is compulsive and cannot be satisfied internally.
  - Reform may require withdrawal from the promise itself, not merely improved regulation.
- **hidings:**
  - Addiction imagery can individualize or medicalize a structurally organized social order.
- **quotes:**
  - “weaning off the drug of absolute wealth”
  - “collective obsession”
  - “true religious disillusion”

### `competing_framings`

#### CF1 — Capitalism’s basic character

- **contested_aspect:** Whether capitalism is rational-secular or essentially religious.
- **framing_a:**
  - **metaphor:** CAPITALISM IS A RATIONAL CALCULATING SYSTEM
  - **source_domain:** Formal calculation
  - **advocates:** Weberian and conventional social-scientific interpretations
  - **implications:** Money enables accounting; capitalism drives secular rationalization; equilibrium and calculation are central.
  - **values_served:** Predictability, scientific objectivity, formal rationality.
  - **anchor:** money is “‘the perfect medium of accounting.’”
- **framing_b:**
  - **metaphor:** CAPITALISM IS A RELIGION
  - **source_domain:** Cult and faith
  - **advocates:** Benjamin and Deutschmann
  - **implications:** Capitalism rests on faith, promises transcendence, demands permanent growth, and generates debt.
  - **values_served:** Critical reflexivity and recognition of non-rational foundations.
  - **anchor:** capitalism has an “‘essentially religious’ character.”
- **stakes:** Whether capitalist dynamism can be explained through calculation or requires analysis of belief, myth, and transcendental aspiration.
- **author_preference:** framing_b

#### CF2 — Money’s ontological status

- **contested_aspect:** Whether money is merely a neutral medium or itself embodies power.
- **framing_a:**
  - **metaphor:** MONEY IS A SYMBOLIC MEDIUM
  - **source_domain:** Language/communication
  - **advocates:** Neoclassical economics, Parsons, Luhmann
  - **implications:** Money communicates values or scarcity but does not itself possess intrinsic value.
  - **values_served:** Analytical neutrality and functional differentiation.
  - **anchor:** “money were *only* a medium of communication.”
- **framing_b:**
  - **metaphor:** MONEY IS GENERALIZED POWER
  - **source_domain:** Capacity and sovereignty
  - **advocates:** Simmel, Marx, Deutschmann
  - **implications:** Money can be privately appropriated, grants freedom of choice, and embodies social power.
  - **values_served:** Attention to ownership, class, and asymmetry.
  - **anchor:** “money is *Vermögen*.”
- **stakes:** Whether analysis foregrounds communication or the unequal ownership of actionable possibilities.
- **author_preference:** framing_b

#### CF3 — The entrepreneur

- **contested_aspect:** Whether innovation is heroic individual action or collective mobilization.
- **framing_a:**
  - **metaphor:** ENTREPRENEUR IS HEROIC REVOLUTIONARY
  - **source_domain:** Heroism/revolution
  - **advocates:** Schumpeter
  - **implications:** Exceptional instinct and leadership drive innovation.
  - **values_served:** Individualism, leadership, competitive achievement.
  - **anchor:** “a ‘dream to found his private empire’.”
- **framing_b:**
  - **metaphor:** ENTREPRENEUR IS PROPHET WITH A CONGREGATION
  - **source_domain:** Religious movement/social coalition
  - **advocates:** Deutschmann
  - **implications:** Innovation requires interpretation, workers, networks, experts, and collective faith.
  - **values_served:** Cooperation and distributed creativity.
  - **anchor:** “innovation is always a social process.”
- **stakes:** Attribution of creative agency and rewards, especially the recognition of workers’ contribution.
- **author_preference:** framing_b

#### CF4 — Capitalist order

- **contested_aspect:** Whether equilibrium or creative destruction best represents capitalism.
- **framing_a:**
  - **metaphor:** ECONOMY IS AN EQUILIBRATING SYSTEM
  - **source_domain:** Mechanics/static balance
  - **advocates:** Neoclassical economics
  - **implications:** Stable prices and rational allocation are analytically primary.
  - **values_served:** Order, calculability, optimization.
  - **anchor:** “in equilibrium there are no profits.”
- **framing_b:**
  - **metaphor:** CAPITALISM IS CREATIVE DESTRUCTION
  - **source_domain:** Revolution/destruction and reconstruction
  - **advocates:** Schumpeter, Marx as reconstructed by Deutschmann
  - **implications:** Profit depends on disequilibrium, innovation, and recurrent institutional disruption.
  - **values_served:** Dynamism and historical explanation.
  - **anchor:** “how it creates and destroys them.”
- **stakes:** Whether theory explains capitalist administration or capitalist transformation.
- **author_preference:** framing_b

### `source_domain_analysis`

#### Primary domains

1. Religion and theology  
2. Machinery and movement  
3. Spatial access and containment  
4. Natural cycles and waves  
5. Revolution and warfare  
6. Medicine and addiction  

#### Domain characteristics

- **Religion**
  - **domain_type:** social
  - **what_it_highlights:** faith, ultimate promises, ritual continuity, transcendence, charismatic mediation.
  - **what_it_obscures:** institutional diversity and differences between belief and material coercion.
  - **ideological_valence:** Strongly critical; it demystifies capitalism’s claim to secular rationality.

- **Machinery**
  - **domain_type:** mechanical
  - **what_it_highlights:** systemic compulsion, motion, dependence among components, danger of stoppage.
  - **what_it_obscures:** contingency, contestability, and moral responsibility.
  - **ideological_valence:** Ambivalent; it criticizes systemic necessity while potentially naturalizing it.

- **Keys, tickets, carriers, containers**
  - **domain_type:** spatial/physical
  - **what_it_highlights:** access, portability, stored potential, command over options.
  - **what_it_obscures:** the social construction of ownership and exclusion.
  - **ideological_valence:** Initially liberal-emancipatory, then class-critical.

- **Cycles and waves**
  - **domain_type:** temporal/natural
  - **what_it_highlights:** recurrent emergence, stabilization, crisis, and renewal.
  - **what_it_obscures:** irreversible losses and unequal exposure to crisis.
  - **ideological_valence:** Dynamic and anti-equilibrium, but liable to naturalize disruption.

- **Revolution/war**
  - **domain_type:** military/social
  - **what_it_highlights:** struggle, victory, destruction, transformation, leadership.
  - **what_it_obscures:** maintenance, care, compromise, and ordinary cooperation.
  - **ideological_valence:** Schumpeter’s usage is heroic; Deutschmann qualifies it through collective agency.

- **Addiction/pathology**
  - **domain_type:** biological
  - **what_it_highlights:** compulsion, overcharge, unsustainability, need for withdrawal.
  - **what_it_obscures:** organized interests and institutional mechanisms.
  - **ideological_valence:** Explicitly anti-capitalist in the conclusion.

### Counter-evidence

- The author explicitly preserves a difference between capitalism and traditional religion: capitalism is “no longer based on a fixed distinction between the mundane and the transcendent world.”
- Rationality is not wholly denied; it becomes phase-specific. A “stable market allowing rational strategies” emerges during institutionalization.
- The entrepreneur is not treated solely as a prophet or hero; he “depends much more on his workers than Schumpeter assumes.”
- Myth does not mean pure fabrication. Paradigms connect “a concrete technological artefact” with “the imagination of its inherent possibilities.”
- Money does not affect rich and poor identically: for the rich it is “a vehicle of freedom,” while the poor experience it as “a disciplining medium.”
- The article’s cycle is not simple repetition: “The latter does not simply repeat the old myth.”
