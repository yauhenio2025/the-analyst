# Epistemological Method Detector: three-paper evaluation, 2026-09-05

The production readings identify how these papers argue more directly than the original questions do. They distinguish texts that establish a target's position, philosophical warrants, borrowed historical research, and assertions about social groups. The main remaining problem is attribution and scope: the readings sometimes mistake a discussed method for the author's enacted method, or judge a philosophical argument by the evidential requirements of an empirical study. The appropriate revision is to distinguish these roles explicitly, without making a dataset or a complete replacement theory mandatory for conceptual criticism.

I personally read the three source papers and substantive notes, all three original-question outputs, both ordinarily completed production outputs, and the mechanically recovered Harris output. The separate source audit by the reading agent supplied additional checks. These observations precede the Sonnet verdicts. They compare the whole production treatment—questions, method cards, synthesis instructions, and check—with the original questions; they do not identify the critic's isolated effect.

**Harris: enacted criticism versus an advocated epistemology.** The old output correctly identifies mediated experience, constitutive power, Fanon's challenge to recognition, and Horkheimer's transformative standard. But it tends to describe the epistemology Harris endorses as though it were the procedure demonstrated by this paper. Old F6 turns the interdisciplinary program of first-generation critical theory into Harris's own validation procedure; F24 similarly imports Thompson's repertoire despite the explicit optionality near the conclusion. The new opening instead inventories philosophical reconstruction, adversarial synthesis, quoted target claims, and illustrative counterexamples. It appropriately asks for evidence for the article's empirical assertions about academic and activist reception, rather than for its hypothetical nurse/banker counterexample.

The [source](../../data/study/sources_ideas/harris2026_eight_arguments_against_honneth.txt) supports that distinction: conceptual criticisms occupy the eight arguments, while reception assertions at lines 715–769 need a separate evidential basis. The original's Husserlian alternative follows the old question menu, not the paper's own undertaking. The new reading substantially avoids this diversion, although its residual sentence about bracketing the natural attitude adds little.

Self-application is less secure. The new prose suggests that adopting Horkheimer's normative conception reproduces the monism Harris rejects. A criterion for critical theory's political adequacy is not automatically a single explanatory mechanism for every pathology. The critic rejects F24 on related grounds, but the prose retains the accusation. Likewise, criticism can expose Honneth's missing adjudication rule without constructing a complete rule for every pluralist successor. The latter is a legitimate future obligation only where Harris actually proposes a successor as adequate.

The final question also narrows Harris's case excessively to recognition monism. Section 3.4 explicitly challenges recognition's primacy even where other approaches are used (567–578). One can contest that argument, but it must be reconstructed before asking whether the paper merely assumes all non-monistic successors fail. The new reading is useful because it separates evidential kinds; its categorical diagnosis still needs the independent-route qualification already identified by the commitment memo.

**Zambrana: method is both object and practice.** The old reading is already substantively strong. It distinguishes adopting Rose's speculative method from rejecting Rose's verdict on Marx, recognizes the three-stage procedure, and credits Smallwood for historical reconstruction. Its extended Husserl/Harding discussion, however, distracts from the actual inference. Old F23 also manufactures a tension between contingent formation and categorical historical description: contingency does not make an achieved historical result unknowable.

The new opening is better organized around evidence roles: primary philosophical passages establish concepts and interpretive claims; secondary scholarship arbitrates readings; Smallwood supplies historical premises. Its testimony section correctly distinguishes a structural account of enslavement from knowledge of enslaved people's experience. Absent first-person testimony limits the latter without automatically invalidating the former. This is a real success for the new questions.

The reading then contradicts its own inventory by treating the historical conclusion as exceeding a purely textual remit. The [source](../../data/study/sources_ideas/zambrana2025_philosophy_in_the_severe_style_rose.txt), lines 76–95, explicitly includes Smallwood in the procedure; lines 402–427 supply a valorizing circuit and the development of financial, commercial, and legal institutions. Smallwood's 2008 book and 2020 lecture both appear. One may ask whether these premises discriminate internal capitalist relations from enabling conditions; one cannot infer inadequacy merely from the absence of Zambrana's own archives. The critic makes exactly this useful correction in weakened F13, while the prose retains “largest exceedance.”

Other attribution errors remain. Added F24 anchors Zambrana's enacted method in a passage describing Hegel's severe style through Rose; her own procedure provides better evidence. F14 calls “truth of capital” unlocated although its context identifies apparent autonomy and Ramas San Miguel's interpretation. F21 treats Marx's acknowledged restriction as ineffectual reflexivity, when that restriction motivates the extension (443–449). F22 needs to retain the final proposal's “would track” scope (465–466). A programmatic extension is not a falsely reported completed investigation.

**Chen: whose testimony, and evidence for which claim?** Both readings identify the crucial distinction between collective belief about progress and belief constituting progress. Both retain Chen's concession that reasons and standards survive rejection of neutral criteria. The new reading provides a clearer authority map: Jaeggi establishes the target; Kuhn supplies a community model; Wellmer supports a distinction between truth and rightness; Giddens supplies the childhood example; Searle models institutional facts. Social actors are mostly assigned intentions rather than heard. These distinctions answer the session's testimony question concretely.

Two evidential burdens must remain separate. The [source](../../data/study/sources_ideas/chen2025_progress_without_progress_jaeggi.txt), lines 525–531, makes contestable claims about collective beliefs and majorities; evidence about actual groups would help. The constitutive thesis at 549–564 needs a philosophical bridge from institutional status functions to normative progress. Surveys alone could not establish that bridge, and philosophical inquiry is entitled to attempt it. The new reading sometimes treats both gaps as consequences of lacking empirical research.

Conditional scope is improved but imperfect. F13 preserves the alternative's dependence on retaining scientific/social parallelism. Counter-evidence then incorrectly calls this the condition of the “strongest negative conclusion”; it conditions the positive alternative. F24 anchors supposed self-reflection in a dilemma addressed to Jaeggi (596–598). Its self-application prose further asserts that reasons cannot correct collective belief; lines 574–578 do not establish that exclusion. Asking how correction works is justified. Declaring it impossible is stronger than the source.

The revision priorities are therefore specific. E1 should name the inquiry's object, enacted procedure, advocated method, and role of each evidence type before judging adequacy. E2 should distinguish evidence about a theorist's position from authority credited for a substantive claim. E3 should match the required warrant to interpretive, empirical, constitutive, and normative claims. E4 should compare professions and practice concerning the same claim, retaining conditional and programmatic scope. E5 should identify the owner and kind of a standard before declaring self-exemption. Finally, check corrections must reach dependent prose; a corrected ledger attached to an unrevised accusation is not a reconciled reading.
