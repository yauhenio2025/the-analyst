## The attribution failure, traced

The key identity is `reporter:src_3b3e8bc4ae44b89b3d39c265/F15`, in the saved CoinDesk article by Krisztian Sandor. Its quoted sentence about Republican interest in dollar hegemony is journalist narration. Adjacent directly attributed Ardoino statements discuss Treasury purchases, inclusion and serving both parties; they do not make the intervening journalist's formulation his own statement.

The historical source reading and F15 finding already label this as Ardoino's argument, and the saved `speaker` field names him. The original batch map repeats it as participant positioning. Union routing correctly preserves the literal source span, but the new adjudication's E1.F6 relies on it as Ardoino's interested systemic-benefit claim. The new memo's source map repeats that attribution. No source check establishes it as his direct statement. The final memo does not cite F15 in its main argument.

All completed stages pass their mechanical support checks. Those checks establish known identities and literal source support; they do not certify the reading's interpretation or speaker assignment. This result is therefore a consequential attribution regression in the completed union answer, even though it does not claim that dollar-hegemony effects were empirically demonstrated.

The distinction matters for follow-up: this trial reused the old source reading by design. It does not establish that a fresh reading using the current multimedia criticism method will make the same mistake. It does show that supplying that method at later synthesis stages did not repair the inherited attribution.
