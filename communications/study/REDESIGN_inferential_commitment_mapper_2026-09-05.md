# Redesign: Inferential Commitment Mapper (2026-09-05)

> Third engine under the shape (`REDESIGN_method_shape_2026-09-04.md`). Lineage Brandom (scorekeeping, material inference, incompatibility), Sellars, Toulmin. Current definition: five dimensions (explicit, implicit, conflicts, dependencies, hidden implications) with questions such as "which implicit commitments would embarrass the author" and "would the author be comfortable with all the practical implications", which ask about a mind, not a text. The method itself is sound and distinct from Argument Architecture: that engine maps what supports a claim (backwards, grounds → claim); this one maps what accepting a claim commits a reader to (forwards, claim → entailments and exclusions).

## 1. The ideal output

**One paper.** A reader who is deciding whether to accept the text's position wants: (1) the commitments the text undertakes, in its own words with their verbs (C1, C2 …); (2) for each load-bearing concept, what accepting it commits one to by the concept's inferential role *as the text uses it* ("militarised neoliberalism" commits a reader to neoliberalism surviving state intervention; "geopolitical fetishism" commits one to class relations being causally prior), anchored where the text draws or presupposes that inference; (3) the incompatibilities: commitments the text holds that exclude each other (bipartisanship as class condensation and as electoral wedge), both anchored, with how the text handles each (acknowledged, bracketed, unnoticed); (4) the dependency order: which commitments nothing in the text supports (foundational), which are derived, and the single point of failure; (5) the consequences the text draws for practice or policy and the ones it stops short of, anchored to where it stops ("beyond the scope"); (6) the synthesis: what a reader signs up for, at what price, and the one commitment to test first. Tables: commitments (id, text, verb, kind, foundational or derived), entailments (concept → commitment → anchor), incompatibilities (pair, how handled).

**Corpus.** The commitments the texts share (a school's score), the entailments one text draws that another refuses, and the incompatibilities that appear only when two texts are read together.

**Depth.** Every load-bearing concept has its entailments listed; every incompatibility quotes both sides; the single point of failure is named; consequences are the text's, anchored where it draws or stops.

**Weak output.** Argument-structure mapping under different labels; "the author would be uncomfortable"; entailments that are the analyst's politics rather than the concept's inferential role in this text.

## 2. Dimensions and questions
- **I1 declared commitments**: the theses the text undertakes, with the verb ("we argue", "constitutes"); which it presents as its own and which it takes from a cited source; which it defends at length and which it asserts once.
- **I2 entailed commitments**: for each load-bearing concept, what accepting it commits a reader to, by the inferential role the text gives it; where the text draws that inference itself; where it presupposes it.
- **I3 incompatibilities**: pairs of commitments (declared or entailed) that exclude each other; both anchors; how the text handles the pair (acknowledged and addressed / acknowledged and bracketed / unnoticed).
- **I4 dependencies**: commitments nothing in the text supports (foundational); the chain from them to the main thesis; the commitment whose failure takes most with it.
- **I5 consequences**: what the text says follows for practice, policy or method; the consequence the text stops short of, with the sentence where it stops; normative vocabulary inside descriptive claims.
- **X6 (corpus) shared score**: commitments held across texts; entailments drawn in one and refused in another.

## 3. Method cards
- **Brandom, scorekeeping (I1, I2).** Do: keep the text's score: list each commitment it undertakes and the entitlement it claims for it (argued here, cited, presupposed); for each key concept, write the inference the text treats as good by the concept's content, not by logic, and anchor the sentence where it is drawn or assumed.
- **Brandom, incompatibility (I3).** Do: for every pair of commitments, ask whether holding one precludes entitlement to the other in the text's own terms; quote both; say what the text does with the pair.
- **Toulmin (I4).** Do: find what nothing in the text supports and mark it foundational; follow the chain to the thesis; name the single point of failure.
- **Sellars (I5).** Do: keep the space of reasons and the space of causes apart: where the text explains by causes what it evaluates by reasons, or vice versa, quote it; where a consequence for practice follows and the text stops, quote the stop.

## 4. Desks
Commitment ids (C1…) are what the spine cites; the incompatibility table is the reader's decision aid; the dependency rows draw the diagram.
