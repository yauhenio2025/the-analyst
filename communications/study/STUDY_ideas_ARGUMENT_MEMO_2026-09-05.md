## Harris: a useful inference structure with imperfect reference integrity

The baseline is substantively strong. Old F8–F9 distinguish Honneth's refusal to revise from the impossibility of a coherent revision; F15–F17 reconstruct the constitutive-power objection; F23 notices the stronger claim about **any** pathology; F26 separates asserted activist reception from evidence. It already preserves subordinate recognition, temporal development, and the later engagement with labour. Its proposed “clear comparative preponderance” imports a proof standard instead of establishing what Harris's own claims require.

The checked reading improves the organization of that assessment. C1–C10 distinguish abandonment from the eight supporting claims and the identity-of-theory rule. I1 asks whether their accumulation entails unsalvageability; I4 tests felt misrecognition as a sign using both rival causes and injustice without the sign. A3.F13–F14 and critic-added F30 make this an answered critical question, not merely a scheme label. A4.F23 reads the burden from “impossible,” “fatal,” and “unsalvageable.” Readers can reuse these distinctions to assess grounds against the actual conclusion, rather than simply receiving a longer critique.

Three qualifications matter. First, checked C7 says recognition politics “displaces” reparations, whereas source 643–650 says reparations “risk being displaced.” Critic-added F34 restores the operative verb in a row; it does not repair the categorical prose. Second, “C9 is needed” for the political injunction overstates reception evidence's necessity: source 726–728 already calls the conceptual failure a sufficient reason for abandonment. Third, the charitable strongest-form paragraph drops recognition's **primacy**, although the reading elsewhere correctly retains it. Source 567–578 expressly attacks recognition as the primary entry point “regardless of whether other approaches are also used.” Supplementation alone does not answer that argument.

The IDs are not wholly reusable. Prose **S2** means that a non-monist reconstruction ceases to be Honnethian; ledger **A2.F20/S2** means that phenomena inaccessible to monism remain inaccessible to any recognition-led reconstruction. Those are different premises. The first is also explicitly asserted at source 365–371: the disputed warrant is its authority over reconstructed descendants, not whether Harris states it. The critic usefully adds missing C4/C6/C7/C9 claim rows (F32–F35) and corrects the classification question in F6. It does not reconcile this prose–ledger collision.

## Zambrana: historical discrimination, with an attribution failure

The old reading already distinguishes Rose's method from her interpretation of Marx (F7–F15), and labels Marx's resource/scope relation a productive tension (F23–F24). Smallwood supplies both a circulation comparison (F17–F18) and institutional grounds (F19); slavery's historical role is not derived from Marx alone. Its “clear and convincing” standard is again imported.

Checked I4 usefully separates the historical bridge from the philosophical reconstruction: failure to establish capital's genesis would leave a revisionary Marx interpretation standing. F14–F17 identify labour, circuit, creation claim, and reconstructed warrant; F24 asks whether primitive accumulation's strongest versions already include continuing expropriation. That question is justified by source 487–492. F20 also preserves “seemingly unmediated”: financial appearance is not proof that production ceases to matter.

But checked **F23** certifies an attack as faithful because it supposedly quotes Lukács's own formulation. Its anchor, “To call for the ‘dissolution of reification’,” is **Rose criticizing Lukács** (237–242). Actual Lukács testimony appears at 272–274. Quotation membership cannot establish whose standpoint the quoted sentence represents. The row needed either the direct passage or an explicit mediated-attribution qualification.

The reconstruction also creates some of its own targets. Its W2 combines M–C–M′, surplus value, and institutional construction; **S2/F21** reduces this to conformity with the formula being sufficient. A merchant-circuit counterexample would challenge that reduced warrant, without by itself answering the fuller grounds (402–427). Likewise, borrowing Rose's criteria while reversing her Marx interpretation is treated as an unresolved framework-transfer gap, although Zambrana expressly stages the corrective procedure (76–84; 249–306). The relevant test is whether her distinction and textual application succeed, not whether reversing a theorist's verdict inherently makes their criteria inseparable. Finally, F27 calls present continuity “promised,” while F8 correctly recognizes the conclusion's prospective “suggesting a sphere of action” (465–466). A traced contemporary case is a valuable extension, but its absence needs that scope qualification.
