## The alleged incompatibility needs an additional premise

F21 says the early systematic synthesis conflicts with the later restraint on totalizing self-descriptions. Its own prose is more careful: **if** capitalism-as-religion becomes a total representation, it risks violating the late epistemic limit. That conditional identifies a worthwhile reflexive burden. The ledger's categorical conflict does not establish the antecedent.

D01P:116 explicitly limits the theoretical observer to reconstructing how actors create and transform structures. The same paragraph then offers capitalism-as-religion as a systematic basis for that analysis. At 26–28 the paper already criticizes a context-free observer and proposes a negative comparison rather than a universal definition of religion. D22:37–39 rules out a total external standpoint; 87 nevertheless permits relatively impartial sociological discussion. A systematic, limited reconstruction is not thereby a transparent view of society's entirety. The final should ask whether the early analysis exceeds its declared limits, with a particular inference as evidence, rather than treat systematic ambition itself as proof of totalization.

The stronger conclusion that coherence requires a diagnostic comparison instead of the early comprehensive synthesis is consequently under-supported. No demonstrated retraction appears here. The output could preserve the reflexive question while reporting continuity in epistemic restraint and a changed application of it.

## The developmental contrasts overlook explicit early passages

F2 describes money as appearing “first as generalized power and later as an absolute purpose and imperative.” But D01P:48 already derives money's becoming the “final destination of all social action” from its status as a general means. The same essay uses “absolute means” at 58 and develops the growth imperative at 70–72. D22:67 restates that connection under the unbounded-market account. The change is its explanatory setting, not a newly discovered passage from means to imperative.

The alleged practical shift is qualified by the final's own best evidence. F14's late containment claim is sound by itself. The comparative prose, however, contrasts early renewal through myth cycles with later refusal of promises. F29 then accurately quotes D01P:136: “But no new utopias will show the way out,” alongside weaning off absolute wealth. D01E:107 likewise ends with taking leave of the religion of money. These early passages describe growth's operation while refusing to prescribe another redemptive growth vision. D22:87 specifies sobriety, containment, and sociology's role; it does not introduce the refusal itself. A changed practical emphasis is plausible, but the current contrast conflates capitalism's mechanism with the author's recommendation.

The omitted historical qualification is also consequential. F25 derives a practical requirement for workers' freedom and cooperation from the early argument. D22:95 acknowledges slave labour's central role in colonial capitalism and the first industrial revolution, then restricts the formal-freedom thesis to the cooperation demanded by subsequent industrial revolutions. That is exactly the kind of qualification a shared-score engine should expose. It does not simply refute the creativity thesis or prove an announced revision, but it constrains an unqualified corpus-wide inference about capitalism and free labour.
