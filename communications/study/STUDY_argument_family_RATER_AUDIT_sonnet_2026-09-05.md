## Five concrete reason failures

1. **DS candidate Ganzinger: endorsing an erroneous checker rationale.** The `anchoring` reason says weakening F8 to “aimed to show” correctly tracks source hedging; `hallucination_risk` credits removal of a purported authorial admission of uncertainty. But the original finding and final table identify a reader-proposed challenge. G:647–648 and 692–754 supply an argument for the necessary successor; its adequacy can be contested without treating the argument as absent. The final prose recognizes that derivation while the checked reason claims an absence of necessity proof. Sonnet's `coherence` assurance that the weakening is reconciled throughout misses that conflict.

2. **CF candidate Harris: treating rejected findings as proof of discipline.** The `anchoring` and `hallucination_risk` reasons praise rejection of F4/F5/F7/F11 as protection against unsupported attribution. F4 performs inferential work without literal if/then syntax; its classification as reductio can be questioned without excluding the counterexample. F5/F7/F11 contain genuine but mechanically shortened quotation prefixes. F7/F11 are re-added as F15/F16 with the same shortened anchors. The prose continues to reference rejected IDs, contrary to the judge's explicit ledger/prose consistency claim. Its own risk reason acknowledges that F4's examples are directly quoted. The valid recognition of Harris's footnote-3 even-if route must be separated from this approval of failed checking.

3. **CF original Ganzinger: crediting the analysis with a source-only observation.** The `non_obviousness` reason attributes de Boer's reciprocity model being superior to Hegel's linear presentation to the analysis's “F16 reconstruction.” That comparison occurs in source G:468–478, but nowhere in the delivered analysis, which was re-read completely to check this claim. F16 concerns the uniqueness of self-division. The reason thus rewards an observation the analysis did not make. Its praise of the actual conceptual-genre distinction and necessity question is well-founded.

4. **DS original mixed control: importing a requirement, then rewarding the resulting conflict.** The `non_obviousness` reason calls non-publicity versus informational erasure a genuine residual tension. The policy requires removing addresses from public copies, while preserving restricted originals. Total erasure is an additional requirement, not a conflict generated within that stated arrangement. An analyst may test broader privacy requirements, but must identify the addition. Sonnet correctly recognizes the original's partial-accountability qualification and the fragment/inventory limits; those genuine strengths do not validate F11's residual-tension claim.

5. **CF original Deutschmann: endorsing false chronology and converting qualification into tension.** The `coherence` reason expressly praises the 2022 essay's greater caution as consistent throughout. D01P:136 already rejects new utopias and any guaranteed future of creative destruction. A later denial of ultimate reconciliation also concerns a different consequent from a conditional depression prediction. The `non_obviousness` reason additionally calls slavery versus later-industrial formal freedom an internal tension, although D22:95 expressly distinguishes historical stages; original F15 preserves that qualification. Candidate Deutschmann's judge recognizes Tenbruck's provisional premise but does not detect its continued false practical genealogy or the omitted unfree-labour counterfactual. Its broad assurance that cross-text revisions have evidence is weaker than a specific endorsement of that chronology.

Those scores cannot demonstrate that additional descriptive rows improve the task's negative conclusion. Conversely, scope flags do not certify absence, and administrative inconclusiveness does not establish semantic uncertainty.

The reasons are useful secondary observations, but the examples show why they cannot certify quotation interpretation, whole-output consistency, or corpus genealogy. No comparison winner or causal account of rater bias follows from this audit.
