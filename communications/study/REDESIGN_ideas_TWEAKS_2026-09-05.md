# Ideas study: revisions justified by the reading memos

These post-study revisions are now applied to the production checkout, after the original 52 jobs and four bounded synthesis follow-ups completed. They were developed in isolated worktrees and integrated with the runtime repairs as combined patch `22ee7f9a833667443013244d34f26bb5d30a3aca4ccf91bd1e2b38d28caa9a43`. All four operationalizations load with six dimensions each; 186 affected-path tests and 13 study-script guard tests pass on main. Those checks establish implementation compatibility, not improved model performance. Baseline `374325c24e6b10a1` judges the earlier production treatment. The follow-up tests only the corpus instruction and narrow auxiliary cleanup where its input changes; it does not validate the revised document or P6/X6 extraction questions.

The revisions come from the actual final readings in the [Conditions](STUDY_ideas_CONDITIONS_MEMO_2026-09-05.md), [Argument](STUDY_ideas_ARGUMENT_MEMO_2026-09-05.md), [Commitment](STUDY_ideas_COMMITMENT_MEMO_2026-09-05.md), and [Epistemology](STUDY_ideas_EPISTEMOLOGY_MEMO_2026-09-05.md) memos. The reader who audited the sources independently reviewed the proposed questions, cards and answer shapes. A corpus instruction was subsequently added after the first completed corpus reading exposed a specific selection failure, described below.

## Match the answer format to the question

Several existing questions already ask for the right distinction, while their required row format forces a stronger verdict. Commitment I3 asks about productive tensions but requires every row to say one commitment excludes another. Epistemology E3 asks about position but serializes the diagnosis as exceeding what that position could know. E4 requires a profession set against a practice even where they concern differently scoped claims. Conditions D5 requires a survivor and a casualty whether or not the evidence warrants both.

The revised formats permit the distinctions their questions need. I3 records incompatibility, productive tension, qualified compatibility, or contrast with an opponent, with each claim's owner and scope. E3 records actual sources/inferences and the required warrant; E4 permits alignment or qualification as well as tension. D5 permits a route to remain intact. This leaves judgment to the model while avoiding a required accusation in the serialization contract.

## Epistemological Method Detector

E1 now starts from what the paper investigates and the procedure it enacts, distinguishing a method studied or advocated from one used. The evidence inventory distinguishes primary textual support for an interpretation from scholarship supplying a historical premise. It retains the bracketing question when relevant, without treating its absence as a defect. These changes respond to the old readings' Husserlian detours and to the new Zambrana output's partial return to treating historical scholarship as outside a philosophical paper's method.

E2 carries the same distinction into testimony: quoting Jaeggi to establish the target of Chen's criticism differs from crediting Kuhn or Searle for a substantive inference. The absence of an actor's testimony must be tied to the knowledge it limits. E3 matches interpretive, empirical, constitutive and normative claims to the warrant each needs. Claims about what a majority actually believes and claims about what recognition constitutes need different support.

E4 compares the same claim under the same conditions, distinguishing a tentative extension from a completed demonstration and an unstated revision condition from an explicitly unrevisable commitment. E5 identifies the owner and kind of a standard before applying it. A normative criterion is not itself an explanatory monism; criticizing an inadequate theory does not by itself promise a complete replacement. An acknowledgement can express selective agreement without requiring abandonment of the remaining criticism.

## Inferential Commitment Mapper

I2 distinguishes accepting a conclusion from endorsing its complete derivation. A row must name the actual acceptance set and additional premises. Accepting Zambrana's reading of Marx does not by itself establish Smallwood's historical premises; accepting Harris's rejection need not accept monism's constitutive status if another fatal objection supplies the route.

I4 accordingly traces branching support and records what survives removing a premise. A foundational role and a lack of support are different properties. The synthesis no longer requires a single point of failure or two to three unavoidable entailments where none can be established.

I3's productive-tension question is retained, with a more faithful relation format rather than a duplicate question added to I2. I5 separates required capacities, recommended or illustrative resources, and possibilities, while separately marking whether a consequence is drawn by the text or extended by the analyst. This protects Harris's explicit optionality about Thompson and distinguishes Chen's transfer of a scientific-community argument from its source's original claim. I1 and the opening preserve conditional force.

## Conditions and Argument

Conditions D5 subtracts a framework premise by premise and tests independent grounds before declaring the conclusion defeated. The corresponding card, answer shape and synthesis brief distinguish losing a formulation from losing one derivation or all support. This responds to Harris's separate objection to recognition's primacy and to Smallwood's historical route to slavery's internality.

Argument compares strength only for the same claim under the same conditions, retaining if-clauses. Chen's conditional psychologist alternative cannot fairly be made unconditional and then contrasted with its later plausible formulation. The synthesis permits independent routes where no single inference carries the whole case, and requires stable C/G/S/I meanings and one canonical finding identifier. This addresses the observed S2 collision and the recovered Chen output's shorthand aliases.

## Corpus synthesis must cover the collection

The first [Conditions/Deutschmann reading](STUDY_ideas_CORPUS_CONDITIONS_DEUTSCHMANN_2026-09-05.md) is largely a standalone analysis of the first paper. Its 28 findings have 29 matching quotations, but none comes from the second paper, and it retains only one of the 13 verified cross-document relations. All three full sources and all 177 retained findings were present in the actual synthesis request. An intact handoff and matching anchors therefore do not establish a successful corpus reading.

The applied `CORPUS_READING` instruction is conditional on multiple supplied documents. It starts with a compact position map for each document, then organizes the substantive reading around supported cross-document relations. It requires document representation and anchors for every document covered by a cross-document claim, while leaving room to explain an assessment limit. It distinguishes passage objects, scopes and roles; repetition and reargument from presupposition; and composition dates, edition dates and argumentative development. This also answers the first final's weak claim that money's religious substitution appears as a later development when both early papers already contain it.

The instruction is used in synthesis and direct corpus one-call prompts; single-document prompts receive no corpus instruction. The [protocol](STUDY_ideas_PROTOCOL_2026-09-05.md) records a bounded four-call follow-up after the fixed matrix completes. It adds the corpus instruction and, where needed, mechanically removes positive auxiliary references mistakenly classified as rejected by the frozen deep path. All source text and every pre-synthesis request must remain unchanged, with exact replay checks and separate input hashes. This does not isolate the instruction's effect in affected cases. Per-document token coverage alone will not count as a successful genealogy.

The completed corpus readings also justify revising the future P6/X6 extraction questions. P6's original answer format permits only presupposition, missing definition or renaming in the second text. Both Conditions corpora show why it must also permit repetition and reargument. The revised format allows coexisting relations and separately records a changed application, inferential role or scope qualification. Self-citation can coexist with reargument. An omission needs a stated inspection scope and must distinguish missing vocabulary, missing argument and an explicit refusal; it does not establish abandonment.

X6 now aligns claim objects, conditions and scope before comparing commitments. Distinct claims can be incompatible through an inference, but the row must state that inference and its additional premises. It distinguishes descriptive pressure from endorsed remedies, emphasis from replacement, and qualified continuity from incompatibility. Commitment/Deutschmann supplies concrete tests: the early refusal of new utopias, money's already-imperative character, and the later qualification concerning slave labour. The source reader independently reviewed both revised corpus blocks. These changes affect future extraction, and are excluded from the four frozen synthesis follow-ups; those calls cannot validate the revised P6/X6 questions.

## Runtime repair is a separate change

The [anchor audit](STUDY_ideas_ANCHOR_AUDIT_2026-09-05.md) covers deterministic defects: explicit soft-hyphen line wraps, unrecognized secondary-anchor fields, trim provenance, ignored explicit critic replacements, and auxiliary references parsed as findings. These fixes must preserve source membership checks, strict declared keys on corpus rows, ordinary foreign-executor-key compatibility, genuine duplicate-ID rejection, and raw receipts.

The desk handoff also retains malformed-quote findings in its unverified section and recognizes the corpus namespaces explicitly defined by the composer. A final row descended from `P6`, `X6` or a corpus critic addition still needs a valid document pair even if its displayed dimension changes. This addresses the actual incomplete F8/F14 descendants; it does not infer a substantive cross-document relationship from prose or fix the application's document-map dispatch.

Neither those repairs nor these questions guarantee semantic entitlement. A quotation can match exactly and still support a different speaker, scope, inference, or historical relation. Likewise, applying the critic's ledger rulings does not rewrite the preceding one-call prose. That limitation stays explicit; no code rule will pretend to reconcile substantive prose judgments.

The critic's context-reading duty now explicitly preserves speaker, negation, conditions and scope. Exhaustive claims such as “entirely” require evidence for their breadth, rather than one matching example. The immediate case is the checked Conditions/Zambrana finding that attributes the historical support entirely to one lecture, although the paper also uses Smallwood's book. This is a model instruction to assess support, not a lexical rule that rejects findings containing those words.
