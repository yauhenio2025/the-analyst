## Within-document arguments

### EJST: from money's paradox to organized innovation

The target is an economic sociology capable of explaining the creation and destruction of institutions. Religious ethics influencing economic conduct is distinguished from capital possessing intrinsically religious qualities (21–23). Parallel failures to define religion and money motivate the comparison: functional definitions are too expansive, institutional definitions too restrictive; faith or trust cannot be replaced by the institutions supporting it (25–33). The disciplinary division between economics and sociology helps explain why money's difficult status was neglected (31).

Simmel supplies the intermediate concept: *Vermögen* combines command over actual and imaginable goods, social independence, and temporal/spatial options (41–51). “the option to have options” (51) is valuable beyond any purchase. This is not a claim about precious-metal backing. Nor do equal monetary quantities have equal significance for rich and poor: subsistence dependence removes the latter's freedom of choice (53). Money both signifies value and must itself be privately transferred as something scarce; identifying it solely with communication loses this paradox (55).

Deutschmann reconstructs Marx's capital circuit as a practical response: hoarding fails to realize monetary power, consumption dissipates it, while investment promises its return with an increment (57–61). Finite sums cannot exhaust qualitatively unlimited possibilities; growth repeatedly displaces the contradiction. Schumpeter then supplies innovation and temporary monopoly, but his heroic entrepreneur neglects employees and the coalitions needed to realize an innovation (69–73).

The proposed Marx–Schumpeter combination is selective. Creative labour is indispensable, but hours of labour do not guarantee profit; Deutschmann explicitly rejects Marx's quantitative law of value (75). Workers' freedom and capital's dependence on their cooperation qualify a purely command-based account (77). Technological and organizational myths coordinate heterogeneous participants, motivate cooperation, and legitimate asymmetries. Their social resonance enables bounded rationality without supplying an independently rational foundation for the paradigm itself (79–81).

Institutionalization stabilizes knowledge and markets, yet locks in investments and reduces innovative profitability. Success thus prepares decline; successor myths reorganize predecessors' blind spots and material legacies (83–91). The conclusion preserves the religious analogy while distinguishing capitalism's moving boundary of transcendence from religions' comparatively fixed one (99–103). Its final criticism is already disenchantment with the religion of money (107), not celebration of unlimited innovation.

### Promise: cosmological institutions and the possible exhaustion of mediation

Promise shares much of that sequence, but its distinctive passages matter. Benjamin supplies the opening challenge; Tenbruck supplies a critique of supposedly context-free concepts of religion. The proposed common term is explicitly “a *negative* one” (28): both phenomena determine the conditions of their observation. This is a methodological restriction, not an exhaustive definition equating every religion with capitalism (20–42).

The Simmel–Marx argument again joins generalized monetary possibilities to class asymmetry, creative labour, and compulsory accumulation (48–74). Promise more explicitly argues that money crosses subsystem boundaries because politics, science, art, and religion require financing (66). It also distinguishes profit from interest and rejects the sufficiency of higher exploitation through the example of an economy producing only cement (80).

Civilizational theory gives the entrepreneurial argument a further level. Institutions represent cosmological horizons imperfectly; the gap permits their criticism and transformation. Castoriadis, Eisenstadt, and Arnason are credited for this approach, while Giddens's duality is judged insufficient here (86). Deutschmann's extension is to treat absolute wealth as a secular cosmological horizon underlying private property (88; note 154). Money's capacity to circulate between institutionalized markets creates the indeterminate “meta-market”; entrepreneurs interpret this horizon through manageable projects (90).

Myths now include consumption and lifestyles, and the account explicitly limits the observer to reconstructing actors' creation and transformation of structures (98–116). Myth-mediated rationality is historically achieved, not an all-purpose explanation of development. The detailed cycle culminates in a trade-off between rational stabilization and profitability (106–110).

The ending tests whether that cycle can continue. Financial globalization and market radicalism demand immediate success while weakening the institutions and reciprocity that entrepreneurship needs (130–134). Withdrawal into financial assets is offered as a possible symptom, not established through a new empirical investigation. If creative destruction stops, dependence of ordinary reproduction on growth threatens a depressive spiral. The conclusion rejects another redemptive utopia and proposes relinquishing the collective obsession with absolute wealth (136). The conditional diagnosis should survive any synthesis.

### 2022: unbounded markets and society's missing observer

The chapter identifies its own position as secular sociological critique, distinguishing it from theological denunciation of idols (11–15). Capitalism means a society organized through unbounded markets; the existence of markets alone is insufficient (21). A reconstructed history links Enlightenment projects, subsequent liberal narratives, and unintended institutional consequences. Smith is specifically distinguished from later reception that turned his approach into a social construction recipe (23–27).

Spatial, social, material, and temporal unbounding explain why money cannot remain a specialized economic medium (29–31). Access to productive conditions, especially creative labour, expands property claims from what exists to what could exist. Cooperative arrangements remain boundary conditions. The slavery note acknowledges unfree labour's central historical role, then restricts the free-labour argument to the cooperation required by subsequent industrial revolutions (95).

The new organizing problem is society's inability to observe itself as a totality. Luhmann supplies the internality of observation; Hayek supplies dispersed knowledge that no observer possesses in full. Deutschmann draws the parallel without alleging influence between them (37–39). Liberalism's success produces a society-wide self-description that cannot simply depict the whole it invokes (41). Religions supply a constructed external observer; the comparison explicitly concentrates on Abrahamic traditions (47). Their contributions to universal ethics, rationalization, and scientific distance are acknowledged (49).

Removing theological authority leaves the observation problem intact. New economic and sociological descriptions can conceal the unity they claim to represent (51–57). Capitalism's universal inclusion rests on minimal exchange reciprocity, not the absence of sociality; universality and moral minimalism are connected (63–65). Money then assumes functions of representing social unity and managing contingency (67).

Creative labour and changing imaginations retain the mobile-transcendence argument (69–75). Technological achievements can satisfy earthly wishes while continually generating more. The principal driver is expanding private-property possibilities, not science or instrumental reason acting independently; science and technology help actualize those possibilities (75). The closing prescription is practical containment and relatively impartial sociological discussion, with neither total observational mastery nor historical reconciliation promised (81–87).

## Cross-document findings and limits

**Continuity with changed explanatory role.** The detailed definition of monetary possibility in `deutschmann2001_capitalism_as_religion`, 51, “the option to have options”, can be paired with `deutschmann2022_interpretation_of_capitalism_as_religion`, 75, “expansion of the possibility space of private property rights”. The early concept explains money's intrinsic value and growth; the later formulation also explains why science is subordinate to a property-driven dynamic. This is strong conceptual continuity, not proof that the later chapter presupposes this particular article: 2022 re-explains the mechanism and cites Deutschmann 2019 (27, 67, 119).

**Luhmann's role changes without a simple conversion.** `deutschmann2001_promise_of_absolute_wealth`, 66, criticizes a money theory that “lacks sensitivity for the hidden influence of money”, while already borrowing the notion of cipher. `deutschmann2022_interpretation_of_capitalism_as_religion`, 37–39, makes internal observation and self-description central; 67 nevertheless retains the criticism of restricting money to scarcity regulation. Acceptance of one component does not entail endorsement of the entire theory.

**The comparison is reformulated and delimited.** `deutschmann2001_promise_of_absolute_wealth`, 28, rules out an abstract, suprahistorical common definition. `deutschmann2022_interpretation_of_capitalism_as_religion`, 47, says “I am not speaking of all religions,” while 57 develops the parallel through social intransparency. This is a demonstrable methodological reformulation and explicit scope qualification. Neither passage establishes a recantation of the earlier negative comparison. Non-observability was present from the start.

**The normative ending persists.** Pair `deutschmann2001_promise_of_absolute_wealth`, 136, “no new utopias will show the way out”, with `deutschmann2022_interpretation_of_capitalism_as_religion`, 87, “What is needed is not new promises but sobriety and realism”. The later text specifies containment and sociology's role; it does not discover sobriety after youthful optimism. The 2001 financialization warning and 2022 ecological crisis also have different immediate objects.

**Free labour receives an explicit historical qualification.** `deutschmann2001_capitalism_as_religion`, 77, says the worker “must be free in order to be creative”. `deutschmann2022_interpretation_of_capitalism_as_religion`, 95, acknowledges that “unfree labor, especially slave labor, played a central role” in colonial capitalism and the first industrial revolution. It then locates the need for formally free cooperation in later industrial revolutions. This genuinely qualifies the historical scope of the thesis, without announcing a self-revision or abandoning the creativity mechanism.

**Universalism changes the comparison's scale, not its recognition of power.** `deutschmann2001_promise_of_absolute_wealth`, 62, treats poverty and wealth as differing “in qualitative terms”; 66 already extends money's influence across all subsystems. `deutschmann2022_interpretation_of_capitalism_as_religion`, 65, joins “Moral minimalism and social universalism” while explicitly preserving structurally conditioned power inequalities. The later emphasis is worldwide inclusion through exchange rather than richer individuals' expanded options. Universal inclusion does not imply equal effective freedom; this is not an explicit reversal of the earlier class analysis.

**There is a tension to investigate, not a demonstrated reversal.** `deutschmann2001_promise_of_absolute_wealth`, 134, says radicalism “tends to paralyse the working of the myth cycle”; `deutschmann2022_interpretation_of_capitalism_as_religion`, 75, says the promises' dynamic “cannot be stopped” from within. The former is a qualified claim about productive coordination and a possible halt; the latter concerns structural pressure to expand. Stalled realization and persisting imperative can coexist. Calling these flatly inconsistent requires additional argument.

**Inheritance must remain claim-specific.** `deutschmann2001_capitalism_as_religion`, 75, rejects a quantitative Marxian theory while rehabilitating creative labour. `deutschmann2022_interpretation_of_capitalism_as_religion`, 75, closes with “nothing needs to be added to Marx's insights even today”, specifically after discussing freedom-guaranteeing property becoming collective compulsion. This does not reinstate the rejected law of value. Likewise, citation of Castoriadis at Promise 86 establishes use of a civilizational approach, not uptake of any particular essay in the separate Castoriadis corpus.
