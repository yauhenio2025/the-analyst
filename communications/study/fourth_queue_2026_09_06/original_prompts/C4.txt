# Causal Architecture Analyzer

Maps how a concept figures in causal claims: what it causes, what causes it, feedback loops, necessary/sufficient conditions, and causal mechanisms. Reveals the concept's role in the author's causal reasoning.

Core question: How does this concept figure in causal claims - what does it cause and what causes it?

Extraction focus: causal_claims, cause_effect_relationships, causal_mechanisms, feedback_loops, conditions, counterfactuals

Steps: FIND CONCEPT AS CAUSE: Where does the author claim this concept causes other things? Look for causal verbs: causes, leads to, produces, generates, enables, drives, results in | FIND CONCEPT AS EFFECT: Where is this concept treated as caused by other things? Look for: caused by, results from, produced by, enabled by, determined by | IDENTIFY FEEDBACK LOOPS: Where does the concept appear in circular causal relationships? Look for mutual causation, self-reinforcing dynamics | EXTRACT CONDITIONS: What are the necessary, sufficient, enabling, or blocking conditions? Look for: requires, depends on, only if, whenever, unless, prevents | MAP MECHANISMS: How does causation work? Look for process descriptions, intervening steps, mediating factors | NOTE COUNTERFACTUALS: Where does the author use 'if X then Y' reasoning about the concept?

Pay attention to causal language: explicit causal verbs, conditional statements, counterfactuals, and mechanism descriptions. Distinguish between correlation claims and causation claims. Note the author's certainty level for each claim.
