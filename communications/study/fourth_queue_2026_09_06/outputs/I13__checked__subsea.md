# Reading

## What changes, and what does not

The paper’s most useful distinction is among **completed ownership changes**, **procurement awards and consortium reconfigurations**, and **reported or preliminary arrangements whose completion is not established**. Huawei’s 2020 sale of a majority stake in HMN Tech to Hengtong is presented as an effected ownership transfer [F1]. The SeaMeWe-6 record instead turns on an initial supplier selection displaced by a late-2021 award to SubCom, followed by investor withdrawals and replacement participation [F2] [F5] [F6] [F7]. In France, the November 2024 ASN deal covers 80% for €350 million; acquisition of Nokia’s remaining 20% is stated only as a later intention [F13].

PEACE and EMA require a further status distinction. PEACE became operational in 2022 and is identified as built by HMN Tech and owned by Peace Cable International Network Co. Ltd. [F8] [F18]. By contrast, the EMA contract is attributed to reports, and Orange’s connection to EMA consists of signed memoranda of understanding rather than demonstrated final investment or ownership rights [F10] [F11].

This is a selective inventory from one two-case article, not a supplied corpus or a comprehensive transaction census. It documents particular links among cable suppliers, telecom companies, consortium members, financiers, and states. It does not establish complete market ownership, operational control from ownership alone, or future deal outcomes.

## How the documented transactions connect

The SeaMeWe-6 transactions form the clearest connected sequence. The original consortium included Orange, Telecom Egypt, Mobily, Microsoft, China Telecom, China Unicom, and China Mobile [F15]. It initially selected HMN Tech [F2], whose approximately $500 million subsidized bid competed with SubCom’s $600 million proposal [F3] [F4]. The late-2021 vote shifted the award to SubCom [F5]. China Telecom and China Mobile then withdrew with approximately 20% of the investment volume, while China Unicom remained; two Malaysian and Indonesian telecom firms entered and existing members increased their contributions [F6] [F7].

PEACE and EMA connect some of the same Chinese market actors to parallel cable development, but they do not share the same status. PEACE is an operational cable built by HMN Tech, owned by a Hengtong subsidiary, and associated through partnership with China Mobile, China Telecom, and China Unicom [F8] [F9] [F18]. EMA is supported only as a reported $500 million HMN Tech contract and as the subject of Orange’s memoranda with the Chinese state [F10] [F11]. The paper expressly links the projects by stating that PEACE closely mirrors SeaMeWe-6’s route and that EMA was launched in reaction to US interference in SeaMeWe-6 [F17]. That is a source-supported relation among the projects, not evidence that their ownership structures or commercial terms are identical.

The ASN record is a separate ownership sequence. Nokia acquired Alcatel-Lucent in 2016 [F12]. After an earlier 2012 effort involving a possible Orange acquisition or FSI investment ended without agreement [F14], the French state’s APE struck the November 2024 deal for 80% of ASN at €350 million, with the remaining 20% left for an intended later purchase [F13]. The reported state holdings in Orange provide context for France’s existing corporate connection but are not themselves a new transaction [F16].

These cases document supplier switching, consortium exit and entry, preliminary cross-border cooperation, parallel cable projects, and state acquisition of a strategic supplier. They do not demonstrate market-wide consolidation or establish a forecast of future transactions.
