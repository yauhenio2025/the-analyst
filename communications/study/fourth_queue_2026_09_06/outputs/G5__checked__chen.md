## Main distinction and evidence limit

The paper’s most useful citation-use distinction is between sources used to reconstruct a pragmatist theory of progress and sources turned against that theory. Chen identifies Jaeggi’s thesis as progress through accumulated problem-solving experience, with regression understood as systematic blockage. [F1] Kitcher and Dewey supply important components of this model: nonteleological “progress from” and the growth of further experience. [F4] [F6] Kuhn then has a double role. The comparison between social and scientific crisis initially strengthens the analogy, but Kuhnian incommensurability becomes Chen’s reason for arguing that paradigm changes cannot necessarily be understood as linear accumulation. [F7] [F8] [F9] Chen later reuses Kuhn’s community-based conception of science to defend a psychologist conception of progress. [F13]

Only Chen’s article is supplied. It establishes how this text attributes propositions to, groups, supports, and criticizes other works; it does not establish that those cited works cite one another. Because the cited works were not separately supplied, Chen’s representations of them cannot be checked for fidelity. A single paper and bibliography also cannot establish field-wide influence, reciprocal citation, or prestige. Accordingly, `hub_score`, `authority_score`, and `internal_density` are indeterminate.

## Consequential citation pattern

Kuhn is the hinge of the citation structure. Chen first aligns Kuhn with Jaeggi through a common sequence of malfunction, crisis, and revolution. [F7] That alignment makes scientific paradigm change a model for transformation between forms of life. Chen then reverses the force of the comparison: if different paradigms cannot be compared directly by a neutral standard, the replacement of one form of life by another cannot automatically be described as linear accumulation or enrichment. [F8] [F9]

Kuhn is subsequently invoked constructively. Instrumental knowing-how may grow despite theoretical discontinuity. [F10] More importantly for Chen’s alternative, the community-based and collectively operated character of scientific activity makes attention to scientists’ psychological elements rational. [F13] The paper therefore moves from Kuhn as a source of analogy, through Kuhn as a source of criticism, to Kuhn as a resource for a psychologist conception.

Other citations are organized around this pivot. Kitcher and Dewey articulate the problem-solving and experiential-growth model. [F4] [F6] Roth represents an effort to constrain what counts as successful problem resolution, which Chen rejects. [F11] Lakatos states the psychologism objection that Chen resists. [F12] Chen then applies an intentionality-relative conception of social facts to argue that collectively classifying a social movement as progressive contributes to constituting its social character. [F14] Allen’s paradox places the resulting proposal within a broader dispute over retaining progress as a forward-looking idea without affirming a self-congratulatory history. [F15]

The resulting network is argumentative rather than bibliometric. Jaeggi’s prominence and Kuhn’s repeated invocation establish their importance within Chen’s exposition only. They do not establish reciprocal citation, direct influence, authorial intent, concealment, or field-wide authority.
