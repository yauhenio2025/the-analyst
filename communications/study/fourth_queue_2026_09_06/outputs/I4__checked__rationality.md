# Reading

## Judgment: an obstructed systemic outcome, not a fully shared social good

The clearest documented cooperation problem is internal to capitalist reproduction. Employers individually benefit from minimizing wages and maximizing productivity, but generalized wage suppression weakens the consumer demand needed to absorb collective production. The text therefore describes an incentive conflict between firms’ cost-minimizing conduct and the system-level outcome of sustained production and employment. It does **not** establish that workers and owners regard this outcome—or the distribution supporting it—as a common good on equal terms. Capitalism’s own stated ends are increased production and reduced costs, while workers contest wages, hours, and managerial control. [F1] [F2] [F3]

The mechanism is explicit: left to itself, cost minimization points toward “the lowest possible wages for the highest possible productivity”; generalized movement in that direction risks immiseration, overproduction, and capitalism “drowning in its own production.” [F3] The problem is compounded by decentralized investment: enterprises must invest enough collectively to employ productive capacity, but each firm acts on uncertain expectations, so “nothing guarantees” adequate aggregate investment. [F4] This is best described as a composition and coordination problem reinforced by distributive conflict—not straightforward free riding, because the text does not identify actors enjoying a common benefit while deliberately withholding a required contribution.

The strongest counter-passage is that capitalism has nevertheless “somehow or other functioned within its limits and according to its own finalities.” [F9] The text explains that resilience through conflict-driven institutional correction rather than spontaneous market coordination. Working-class struggles forced wage increases and shorter hours, thereby creating mass consumer markets; political reactions forced state demand management after severe crises. [F6] [F7] Their reported result was the postwar “longest phase of practically uninterrupted capitalist expansion.” [F8] These are observed historical responses, not proof of a permanent cure: the later neoliberal phase dismantled controls, maintained high unemployment, and restored short-term profit as the dominant guide. [F5]

### Counter-evidence

Capitalism’s survival and productive performance are the strongest challenge to a simple cooperation-failure diagnosis. [F9] The text also attributes productive efficiency to technology, cultivated consumer demand, and market expansion, so labor–capital accommodation is not presented as the sole cause of system persistence. The supported conclusion is therefore conditional: private cost minimization and decentralized investment obstruct stable aggregate demand and employment unless counteracted; they do not invariably prevent capitalist production altogether. [F3] [F4] [F6] [F9]

### Open questions

- Does sustained capitalist production count as a genuinely shared outcome when workers contest its distribution, working conditions, and social purposes?
- How broadly did particular firms benefit from higher mass purchasing power relative to the wage costs they individually incurred?
- What institutions maintained postwar wage coordination and demand management, and why did they lose political support?
- Which environmental constraints were attempted, by whom, and through what enforcement mechanisms?
- The source supplies no participation rates, firm-level comparisons, or quantitative causal estimates separating wage policy, state demand management, technology, and postwar conditions.
