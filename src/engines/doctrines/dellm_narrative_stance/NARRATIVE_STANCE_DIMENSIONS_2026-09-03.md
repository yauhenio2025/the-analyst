# Narrative stance — the dimensions an author can choose between

*3 September 2026. Part 1 of the narrative-stance program. Grown from the owner's ten
dimensions, the creative-nonfiction shelf (`NARRATIVE_STANCE_SOURCES_2026-09-03.md`), the
packs (structural-lenses 1.1.0, narrative-foundations 1.1.0, document-foundations 1.1.0),
the deep-structure corpus (twelve elements, five tests) and the storytelling notes; then
pruned. Illustrations are from the two versions of "The AI We Deserve": the published
original (`reviews/2026-09-03-ai-we-deserve/original-capture.md`) and the program's
re-edit after the independent reader's cuts (`document-essay-form.md`).*

## How the list was grown and pruned

Twenty candidates were on the table: the owner's ten; the eight the owner already saw
(spine, scene ratio, revelation schedule, carrier, ending, proportion, coined terms,
genre's signposts); and from the shelf, entry point and loop direction, the lead's
scale, strand joints bare or bridged, the I's warrant, political mode (assert or
witness), frame tightness, exterior load, temporal compression, reconstruction licence,
and structure-from-within versus imposed matrix.

Pruning rule: a dimension survives only if it has two nameable poles, a cost on each
side a reader would feel, and a position an author could state in one sentence before
the restructuring begins. Candidates that failed the rule were folded, never dropped:

| Folded | Into | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Modal status of the counterfactual (owner's 8) | 5 · Epistemic status of examples | the same choice — what an instance is allowed to prove — applied to an imagined instance |
| Entry point and loop direction; the lead's scale | 12 · Revelation schedule and 4 · Opening ethos | where the reader enters is where the first disclosure lands |
| Strand joints, bare or bridged | 2 · Reader steering | a bridge is a signpost |
| Political mode: assert or witness | 3 · Mode | the same axis in its political register |
| The I's warrant (measure, norm, professional, tuning fork) | 7 · Narrator function | the warrant follows from the function |
| Frame tightness; exterior load | 13 · Carrier and container; 15 · Proportion | a loose frame is a proportion decision about connective tissue |
| Temporal compression; reconstruction licence | boundaries, not choices | fidelity limits the program already holds |
| Structure from within vs imposed matrix | 17 · Form–content correspondence | it is the architect's honesty check, not an author choice |

What remains: **sixteen dimensions an author can set** and **one the architect must
declare and the reader verify** (17, form–content). Each has a key (used by the `stance`
declaration in Part 2), two poles, a middle where one exists, and a genre default.

Genre defaults are in §"Defaults by genre" at the end; "enact, never announce" is
dimension 2's continuous pole, which the essay genre defaults to and a report does not.

## 1. Thesis placement — `thesis`
**Poles.** `stated`: the claim comes first and everything after tests it (logic
visible). `discovered`: the question is formulated in front of the reader and the claim
is earned late (thinking visible). **Middle.** `promised`: the question and the
organizing concepts are stated up front, the answer withheld — the point placement rule's
compromise.
**Costs and gains.** Stated: the reader knows at once what to hold; the piece reads as a
defended thesis, and every case becomes confirmation. Discovered: the reader shares the
discovery and owns the conclusion; a reader in a hurry never reaches it, and a consulting
reader cannot find it.
**Sources.** `FIT_POINT_PLACEMENT`, `STAGE_INTRO_POINT` (the point-first default and its
price); `NAR_EMPLOT_AS_REVELATION`; Kidder & Todd · Beginnings (the heart of the story
as a place to arrive at) and · Essays (if I knew my own mind I would make decisions);
`VERIFY_SCAFFOLD_PASSAGE`.
**Illustration.** The original poses its question in the third section ("If we could
turn back the clock … what kind of … agenda might have emerged?") and states its answer
in the last ("Democratic AI requires actual democracy"). The re-edit's first page ends
on a law — a technology built without an organized political counterweight serves
whoever paid for it — and asks whether today's debate "even notices"; the cases that
follow confirm it.

## 2. Reader steering — `steering`
**Poles.** `signposted`: signposts, refrains, labels and bridges tell the reader what each
part proves. `continuous`: the parts are placed so their adjacency carries the meaning;
enact, never announce. **Middle.** `one_brilliance`: no refrain, no labels, but one
summarizing statement where the reader would ask how to feel, placed at the turn or the
close.
**Costs and gains.** Signposted: a consulting reader finds every part's job; a
straight-through reader is interrupted to be told the argument is happening. Continuous:
the reader makes the connections and owns them; a joint may be missed, and a program that
cannot see the joint reports a seam.
**Sources.** `LENS_JUXTAPOSITION_CARRIES_JUDGMENT`, `NAR_BURY_THE_GENERALIZATION`,
`DOC_STRIP_SCAFFOLDING_AND_LISTICLE_FORM`, `VERIFY_STRAND_AND_SIGNPOST` (signposts as a
misorder symptom); McPhee · Structure (the white space); Kidder & Todd · Being Edited
(take the spin off; the brilliance).
**Illustration.** The original has no signposts; its turns are an essayist's ("It is here
that we should step back and ask"). The re-edit as first delivered had "Call this Test
One", "That is the second test concluded", "Three tests, one law"; the independent reader
cut them, and what survives are bridges that still name the meaning of the next part
("failures that confirm the same law from the opposite direction").

## 3. Mode — `mode`
**Poles.** `interrogative`: open questions that invite the reader in and may stay open.
`declarative`: assertions that conclude. **Middle.** `mixed`: questions open a movement,
assertions close it. The political register is the same axis: bear witness in the
details, or assert against and for.
**Costs and gains.** Interrogative: engagement without belief; a reader who wanted a case
feels the writer never decided. Declarative: authority and momentum; questions the
record cannot settle get settled anyway.
**Sources.** Kidder & Todd · Essays (trust and disagreement coexist; essays let you
second-guess yourself) and · Beyond Accuracy (Orwell against bearing witness);
`DOC_COMMIT_ON_CONTESTED_GROUND` (the pull toward the declarative where the evidence
supports a side).
**Illustration.** The original runs questions in series: why did so few radical
innovations emerge; could an anti-systemic AI exist; does any of this matter today; is
the app "the equivalent of me driving a car where a train might do just as well?" The
re-edit keeps those questions where their paragraphs survive and adds verdicts around
them: the founder "is not admitting a failure here; he is boasting of an efficiency";
"the missing piece was never a better algorithm or a fairer price."

## 4. Opening ethos — `opening`
**Poles.** `earn`: concession before critique, the quiet beginning, the reader trusted —
credibility with skeptics. `seize`: open on the harm, the boast, the scene of the wound —
momentum. **Middle.** `scene`: open inside a particular that contains the whole and let
it carry both credibility and pull.
**Costs and gains.** Earn: the skeptic stays; the impatient reader leaves during the
concession. Seize: the reader is pulled; the skeptic hears a prosecutor from line one and
discounts what follows.
**Sources.** `SHAPE_CONCESSION_FIRST`, `FIT_ENTRY_POINT`, `STAGE_OPENING_CONTRACT`,
`DOC_OPEN_ON_THE_CASE_NOT_THE_PROMPT`, `NAR_OPEN_ON_THE_KICK`; Kidder & Todd · Beginnings
(the quiet beginning as a check on overreaching); McPhee · Structure (leads as promises).
**Illustration.** The original opens on the technology's rise and the author's own
delight — "The hawkers do have a point"; a language app built in an afternoon — before
"new agonies". The re-edit opens on "the founder's own confession", the boast about
bypassing the scientific establishment, and the harms follow.

## 5. Epistemic status of examples and counterfactuals — `examples`
**Poles.** `clue`: examples are clues, failures, lessons; a counterfactual is an act of
imagination ("if we could turn back the clock"). `proof`: examples are confirming
instances of a law; a counterfactual is a quasi-empirical exercise "tested against the
record". **Middle.** `test`: an example tests a hypothesis openly and is allowed to fail
it; the counterfactual is run as a thought experiment whose limits are stated.
**Costs and gains.** Clue: honesty about what history can show; the reader may ask what
was proved. Proof: force; a reader who knows the cases sees the fit forced, and the piece
becomes falsifiable by one counter-instance it never faced.
**Sources.** `NAR_TURN_ON_THE_GAP` (expectation against result, never a moral);
`NAR_RUN_HINDSIGHT_AS_IRONY` (when the outcome is public, do not pretend to test it);
`NAR_DRAMATIZE_THE_ABSTRACTION`; Gutkind · Reflection (reflection rises from the scene);
McPhee · Elicitation (quotation leaves judgment to the reader); the deep guide's five
tests (inversion, cross-domain, prescription, reflexivity, rival absorption) as the
honest form of "proof".
**Illustration.** The original: "history abroad does offer some clues"; "the path to
ecological reason is littered with failures"; the app has "elements of eolithism … but I
think this is far from the best we can hope for". The re-edit: the labs are "failures that
confirm the same law from the opposite direction"; Chile is "what a built Humanity Lobby
looks like"; the app must show that a claim "should survive contact with an actual case";
the counterfactual "has to be tested against the record".

## 6. Concepts and agents — `agents`
**Poles.** `coinage`: concepts stay concepts — hedged coinages used sparingly ("what
could be called the Efficiency Lobby"), a name given once and then used as shorthand.
`cast`: concepts become a cast — a recurring antagonist that acts, wants and stays "in
control", and an absent protagonist whose absence explains — so a genealogy becomes a
plot. **Middle.** `faced`: the force is real and shown acting, but only through the
agents the record names (the official who signed, the funder who paid) and their stated
reasons — never as a character with motives the record does not give it.
**Costs and gains.** Coinage: precision and modesty; no momentum, and the reader may not
feel the force. Cast: momentum and memorability; the force acquires intentions the record
does not show, and the piece can be dismissed as conspiracy-shaped. Faced: force with
evidence; more names, less sweep.
**Sources.** `NAR_GIVE_THE_FORCE_A_FACE`, `NAR_CONSTRUCT_AGENTS_BY_CHOICE`,
`DOC_AUDIT_IMPLIED_VALUE_DISTRIBUTION`; the deep guide's agency framework (actors,
forces, systems); McPhee's fair antagonists (the dragline operator, the dam-builder).
**Illustration.** The original coins the Efficiency Lobby mid-essay and hedges it ("what
could be called"), uses it a handful of times, and mentions "no Humanity Lobby, so to
speak" once. The re-edit moves both to page one, makes the Efficiency Lobby "the only
organized party in the room" that "did not need to plan this outcome", and makes the
Humanity Lobby "the absent counterweight" the whole essay is about building.

## 7. Narrator function — `narrator`
**Poles.** `testimony`: the first person supplies credential, colour and scale — a witness
who was there and enjoyed it. `case`: the first person is a case study, an instance the
argument examines and can rule on. **Middle.** `gauge`: the first person is the skeptical
reader's stand-in, a person of ordinary limits whose understanding changes; the change is
the spine. The narrator's warrant follows: the idiosyncratic self, the norm, the
professional self, the tuning fork.
**Costs and gains.** Testimony: warmth and authority; the anecdote proves nothing and can
look like a boast. Case: the anecdote does work; the reader senses a person turned into
an exhibit, and the author judging himself. Gauge: credibility for a larger-than-life
subject; egocentrism if the "I" outgrows the subject.
**Sources.** Kidder & Todd · Point of View (the ladder from absent third to first-person
major; scale decides), · Essays (the I's warrant); `LENS_VIEWPOINT_SCHEDULE`,
`DOC_CONTROL_VIEWPOINT_AND_DISTANCE_ARCHITECTURE`, `LENS_HUMAN_CARRIER`,
`NAR_EMPLOT_AS_REVELATION`.
**Illustration.** The original's app is a credential and a pleasure in the first section
("In over two decades of language study, I've never used a tool this powerful") and
returns later as reflection ("I started out much like the carefree Stormian flâneur").
The re-edit moves the app after the theory and announces its function: "that claim
should survive contact with an actual case … an app I built … and then watched come up
against the same forces that captured the Coordinator."

## 8. Certainty register and tolerance for tension — `certainty`
**Poles.** `standing`: hedges kept, contradictions left standing, "I think" and "perhaps"
allowed to stay. `smoothed`: qualifications trimmed and tensions resolved so the parts
fit the thesis; "the pattern is general". **Middle.** `declared`: the contradiction is
named once as the subject's dimension and never resolved by the prose.
**Costs and gains.** Standing: honesty and voice; a reader may think the writer has not
decided. Smoothed: a clean argument; a knowing reader spots the smoothing and trusts less;
the author's texture flattens toward the fluent mean.
**Sources.** `NAR_STATE_DIMENSION_AS_CONTRADICTION`, `DOC_PRESERVE_THE_AUTHORS_TEXTURE`,
`NAR_ARM_THE_COUNTERARGUMENT`; Kidder & Todd · Essays (self-doubt fortifies the essay),
· Beyond Accuracy (the mind can hold contradictions a story cannot).
**Illustration.** The original: "I think it is not a coincidence"; "Some of this focus on
goals can be traced to funding imperatives … But a deeper reason lies"; "There are, of
course, other positions"; Winograd "to his credit". The re-edit adds "The pattern is
general" and "It only needed to remain … the only organized party in the room", and its
summary of the app section resolves a tension the original left open.

## 9. Spine — `spine`
**Poles.** `chronological`: the parts follow time and the themes hang at their moments.
`thematic`: the parts are themes or cases and the chronology looks after itself; the
essay's variant is associative — the order of a mind. **Middle.** `drawstrings`: a
chronological lead and close around a thematic middle.
**Costs and gains.** Chronological: propulsion and orientation; themes scatter and a
known route is a beaten path. Thematic: themes read whole; the reader loses the clock
and the sense of getting somewhere.
**Sources.** `SHAPE_CHRONOLOGICAL_SPINE`, `SHAPE_THEMATIC_CLUSTERS`,
`SHAPE_CHRONOLOGICAL_DRAWSTRINGS`, `FIT_ORGANIZING_PRINCIPLE_MENU`,
`NAR_REORDER_BY_STRUCTURE_NOT_CHRONOLOGY`, `NAR_MATCH_SHAPE_TO_EXPLANATION` (a shape
asserts a kind of causality); McPhee · Structure (chronology traditionally wins; the
tanker; the towboat); Kidder & Todd · Structure (mess with chronology only for a reason).
**Illustration.** The original runs roughly with time — the 1950s founding, Dreyfus,
Winograd in the 1980s, a step back to Storm's 1953 essay, the app, Latin America in the
1970s — with thematic gatherings inside. The re-edit is thematic: law, debate, founding,
vocabulary, case, counter-history, conclusion; Storm moves ahead of the app because the
vocabulary must precede the test.

## 10. Scene ratio — `scene_ratio`
**Poles.** `scene_led`: witnessed particulars carry the argument, information rides inside
them; half or more of the text is scene. `summary_led`: claims about classes of things
carry it, with particulars as illustration. **Middle.** `alternating`: descend to a
witnessed case, climb to the claim, never sit on the middle rungs.
**Costs and gains.** Scene-led: the reader feels the mechanism; the general claim may go
unstated. Summary-led: coverage and economy; "officials" and "tensions rose" — neither
dramatic nor meaningful.
**Sources.** `NAR_ALTERNATE_SCENE_AND_SUMMARY`, `NAR_WITNESS_THE_MECHANISM`,
`DOC_SHAPE_SCENE_SUMMARY_RHYTHM`; Gutkind · Building Blocks (the highlighting test; the
dance).
**Illustration.** Both versions are summary-led with two scenes: the app ("Vergesslichkeit,
au revoir!") and Storm's stonefield idyll in his own words. The re-edit stages one more —
the founder's boast read as a confession — and otherwise keeps the ratio.

## 11. Revelation schedule — `revelation`
**Poles.** `strongest_early`: the strongest disclosure is spent at the opening (the
inverted pyramid; hindsight declared). `strongest_late`: the strongest disclosure lands
last-but-one; the opening reveals something strong but not the strongest. **Middle.**
`promised`: the opening poses the question the strongest disclosure answers, and the
answer waits. The entry point is part of this: enter on the disclosure, or on the road
to it.
**Costs and gains.** Early: orientation and force for readers who will not finish; the
rest is aftermath. Late: pull and pay-off; a reader who stops early leaves with nothing.
**Sources.** `NAR_SCHEDULE_REVELATIONS`, `NAR_WITHHOLD_EXPOSITION`,
`NAR_RUN_HINDSIGHT_AS_IRONY`, `SHAPE_INVERTED_PYRAMID`, `SHAPE_FLASHBACK_RETURN_LOOP`;
Gutkind · Framing (promises kept; the withheld outcome); McPhee · Structure (the bear at
three-fifths).
**Illustration.** The original's strongest disclosures — the founder's boast at the
midpoint; that Flores commissioned Cybersyn before co-building the Coordinator; that
Hawkins carried Storm's essay from Californian leftists to Papert — land in the second
half, two of them in parentheses. The re-edit spends the boast on page one and keeps the
other two late.

## 12. Carrier and container — `carrier`
**Poles.** `person`: the argument rides on a person (a life, a practice) and the person's
story frames the parts. `idea`: the argument rides on a concept or mechanism and the
cases are its instances. **Middle.** `case`: one case is the container — the elastic scene
that holds the others — and the idea is what the case shows.
**Costs and gains.** Person: credibility and warmth; the systemic claim may stay implicit
and the person may be reduced to an exhibit. Idea: reach and generality; abstraction,
and a reader who never sees the mechanism act. Case: both, if a case is elastic enough
to hold the rest — otherwise the rubber band breaks.
**Sources.** `LENS_HUMAN_CARRIER`, `LENS_FRAME_FROM_FOCUS`, `SHAPE_TWICE_TOLD_TALE`,
`SHAPE_CONVERGING_ARMS`; Gutkind · Framing (parallel narratives; which story frames);
Kidder & Todd · Point of View (a saint made credible by a narrator); the realized forms
(a doctor carrying the two-colour map; a zoologist carrying the wild places).
**Illustration.** The original's carrier is an idea — ecological reason against
instrumental reason — borne by a genealogy of thinkers, with the author's app as colour.
The re-edit's carrier is a law borne by three cases and a cast (the Lobby, the
counterweight), the app promoted to the middle case.

## 13. Ending's contract — `ending`
**Poles.** `resonance`: end on a particular that recalls the opening in a new key, no
commentary after. `summary`: end by restating what was shown and what follows from it.
**Middle.** `verdict_then_image`: the stated conclusion the genre needs, once, then the
particular.
**Costs and gains.** Resonance: the reader is sent back to the beginning; the reader who
wanted the point stated leaves unsure. Summary: certainty; the flag at the bottom, and
the reader released rather than moved.
**Sources.** `STAGE_ENDING_CONTRACT`, `NAR_END_ON_THE_KEY_IMAGE`,
`NAR_DECLARE_THE_ENDING_VERDICT`, `DOC_CLOSE_WITHOUT_THE_WRAP_UP`; McPhee · Structure
(look back upstream; endings as gifts); Kidder & Todd · Being Edited (generalization near
the end as sharpening, never a moral).
**Illustration.** Both versions end on the same last paragraphs — the pragmatists,
Dewey, "perhaps it's not too late to still pursue that alternative path" — a resonant,
hedged close. The re-edit as first delivered had "Three tests, one law" before it; the
reader cut it, but the app section still closes on a summary: "It was, and remains, the
absent counterweight."

## 14. Proportion — `proportion`
**Poles.** `crowd_particulars`: the space goes to cases, scenes and quotation; the
argument is leapt. `crowd_argument`: the space goes to the reasoning and the connective
tissue; the cases are compressed. **Middle.** `by_consequence`: the decisive material
thickens whatever kind it is, and bridges shrink to a line; sometimes a true event is
made invisible because the page would amplify it.
**Costs and gains.** Crowd particulars: the reader remembers; the argument may not be
recoverable. Crowd argument: the argument is airtight; the reader feels nothing and the
piece narrates itself.
**Sources.** `LENS_PROPORTION`, `NAR_PROPORTION_SPACE_TO_THE_DECISIVE`,
`DOC_MATCH_SECTION_SPACE_TO_WEIGHT`; McPhee · Structure (a thousand details; costume
jewelry); Kidder & Todd · Beyond Accuracy (making some things invisible), · Being Edited
(timepassers, exteriors).
**Illustration.** The original crowds Storm — his biography, Veblen, the stonefield in his
words, Duchamp and Eno, the education that unlearns ingenuity, Hawkins — about a third of
the essay, and leaps Latin America in a few paragraphs. The re-edit keeps the Storm
crowding and adds connective tissue around it (a paragraph announcing the vocabulary a
Humanity Lobby might have spoken; a paragraph summarizing what the app proved).

## 15. Coined terms — `terms`
**Poles.** `defined_first`: a coinage is defined at first use, before the material that
earns it. `earned_late`: the coinage arrives after the picture is built, as the name for
what the reader has already seen. **Middle.** `hedged_first`: named early with a hedge
("what could be called"), defined by accumulation.
**Costs and gains.** Defined first: the reader has a handle; the term is an empty label
until the material fills it, and a term defined before its evidence reads as a thesis.
Earned late: the term lands with force; a consulting reader cannot look it up.
**Sources.** `NAR_WITHHOLD_EXPOSITION`, `LENS_POINT_OF_NEED`, `NAR_DRAMATIZE_THE_ABSTRACTION`;
McPhee · Structure (the refrain barely explained in the lead, then transferred).
**Illustration.** The original coins "ecological reason" only after the picture ("We
might call it, in contrast, ecological reason"), introduces "eolithism" through Storm's
own words, and hedges "the Efficiency Lobby" at first use. The re-edit names the
Humanity Lobby on page one and announces eolithism before the Storm section as "the
vocabulary a Humanity Lobby might have spoken".

## 16. Genre's expectation — `genre_expectation`
**Poles.** `expects_signposts`: the readers' genre reads by consulting — abstract,
summary, headings, stated conclusions, exposition up front — and a piece that withholds
them fails its readers. `expects_flow`: the readers read straight through and are
insulted by a map. **Middle.** `both`: a consulting path (an abstract, a contents map, a
verdict section) laid over a flowing body.
**Costs and gains.** This dimension is not a taste but the reader's contract; its cost is
whichever reader is ignored. It sets the defaults of dimensions 1, 2, 11, 13 and 15
below, and the read stage's readers (mode: straight_through, consult, both) decide it.
**Sources.** `DOC_LAYER_OPTIONAL_DEPTH`, `FIT_ORGANIZING_PRINCIPLE_MENU`; the read prompt's
readers and modes; Kidder & Todd · Being Edited (exteriors served when wanted); McPhee ·
Structure (a beaten route calls for the reverse of the expected shape).
**Illustration.** The original is a magazine essay for straight-through readers; the
program's report-shaped instruments (numbered parts, tests, a law up front) treated it as
a document its readers would consult — the mismatch the independent reader named ("makes
the piece feel like a defended thesis rather than an essay").

## 17. Form–content correspondence — `form_content` (declared by the architect, verified by the reader)
**Poles.** `enacted`: the prose enacts its own argument — an essay for wandering wanders,
an essay for precision is precise. `designed`: the form is chosen for a fixed goal and the
materials selected to serve it. **Middle.** `borrowed`: a form borrowed from elsewhere
whose logic is the subject's (a board game for a board-game economy).
**Costs and gains.** Enacted: the reader feels the argument in the shape; a shape derived
too literally becomes shaped verse. Designed: control; the risk the owner named —
restructuring an essay in exactly the mode it critiques.
**Why not an author setting.** An author cannot set it directly: it follows from the
other sixteen. The architect declares it per candidate ("this option enacts the
argument; that one designs around it") and the independent reader, who sees no plan,
reports whether the delivered document's form contradicts its content.
**Sources.** `LENS_STRUCTURE_FROM_MATERIAL` (structure from within vs imposed),
`LENS_BORROWED_FORM`, `LENS_FRAME_FROM_FOCUS` (frame reflects focus); McPhee · Structure
(a structure is not a cookie cutter; shaped verse); Howarth's organic vs mechanic.
**Illustration.** The original argues for eolithic noticing and wanders — the Duchamp and
Eno aside, the "Incidentally" of the last paragraph. The re-edit is design mode: a fixed
goal (the law) and materials selected to serve it; the independent reader's verdict was
that "the frame keeps interrupting to certify that the argument is happening."

## Defaults by genre

The read stage declares the genre and the readers; the start form lets the author
override any cell. `essay` is the owner's; `report`, `paper` and `brief` are the
program's other three home genres.

| Key | essay | report | paper | brief |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| thesis | discovered | stated | stated | stated |
| steering | continuous | signposted | signposted | signposted |
| mode | mixed | declarative | declarative | declarative |
| opening | earn | seize | earn | seize |
| examples | clue | test | test | proof |
| agents | coinage | faced | faced | faced |
| narrator | testimony | absent | absent | absent |
| certainty | standing | declared | declared | smoothed |
| spine | thematic | thematic | thematic | thematic |
| scene_ratio | alternating | summary_led | summary_led | summary_led |
| revelation | strongest_late | strongest_early | strongest_early | strongest_early |
| carrier | idea | case | idea | case |
| ending | resonance | summary | summary | summary |
| proportion | crowd_particulars | by_consequence | by_consequence | crowd_argument |
| terms | earned_late | defined_first | defined_first | defined_first |
| genre_expectation | expects_flow | expects_signposts | both | expects_signposts |
| form_content (declared) | enacted | designed | designed | designed |

Two notes. `narrator: absent` is a value the essay never takes by default and the other
genres rarely leave; it is listed so the author can set it deliberately. `spine:
thematic` for the essay means the associative order of a mind, not the report's
thematic clusters — the pole is the same, the middle (drawstrings) is where an essay with
a journey in it lands.

## Where each dimension is set, and who is held to it

| Set by the author up front (genre defaults) | Declared by the architect per candidate | Held to by the writer and checked by the independent reader |
|---|---|---|
| all sixteen, as defaults the author may override | thesis · steering · opening · spine · scene_ratio · revelation · carrier · ending · proportion · form_content (each candidate says which of the author's positions it keeps and which it changes, so options differ in stance and not only in order) | thesis · steering · mode · examples · agents · narrator · certainty · terms (the writer executes the declared positions; the reader, blind to the plan, reports where the delivered document sits and names any breach in plain words) |

The declaration itself — schema, defaults, prompts and tests — is Part 2
(`IMPLEMENTATION_TRACKER.md`, "Narrative stance").
