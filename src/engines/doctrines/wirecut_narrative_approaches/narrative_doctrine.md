# The narrative approach — the third axis

You may be handed a NARRATIVE APPROACH: the film's STRUCTURE. It is a
third axis, orthogonal to the two registers you already know:

- the **editorial register** is the VOICE (wire-clean, warm,
  prosecutorial…);
- the **conceptual register** is the RELATION TO IDEAS (teach / trace /
  illustrate / none);
- the **approach** is the STRUCTURE — where the film enters, what
  altitude it holds, where evidence lives, how it ends, how long it
  runs.

They compose. The approach's contract binds the film's shape: its
ladder, its zoom direction, its POV and tense, its hook type, its
evidence texture per act, its length window, and its ENDING CONTRACT —
the named close the final beats must land. The beat-function vocabulary
(HOOK, CONTEXTUALIZE, IMMERSE… TEASE) is unchanged — approaches
redistribute which functions dominate and in what order; they never
invent new ones.

## Declaring the approach

When the approach is YOURS to judge, choose the ONE approach whose
contract this document actually supports, and put the judgment on the
record:

- `approach_rationale` must argue from the document — name what you
  found IN THE SOURCE'S OWN WORDS (quote it). A rationale that could
  have been written without reading the document is not a rationale.
- `approach_runner_up` is REQUIRED and must name a DIFFERENT approach
  with its own rationale: the second-best telling and what it would
  have cost the film. A silent default has to argue against a named
  alternative, on the record.
- The grounding law outranks every card: an approach the source cannot
  support honestly is not available for that source — a portrait needs
  a person the text actually FOLLOWS, a correction needs a myth the
  source itself stages, a case needs an episode with its reversals. Do
  not conjure structure the document cannot underwrite.

When the editor CHOSE the approach, apply it and declare exactly that
approach — the choice was theirs; your judgment lives inside its
contract, and your rationale says how the source serves it.

## The five clauses every telling carries

Every card's contract now names five things (the storytelling study,
2026-09-02, `communications/STORYTELLING_STUDY_2026-09-02/`). Whatever
the card, the film must have them, and the spine's `telling` block
declares them:

1. **The question.** One dramatic question in the source's terms — posed
   by the hook or the first turn, tested in the middle, answered at the
   close; at the human scale as well as the systemic where the source
   supports it. A film that cannot say its question in one sentence has
   two films or none.
2. **The centre.** One declared movement near the middle where the
   outcome's opposite shows or the cost is glimpsed — a mirror, a
   restatement of the question at full weight, or a warning. The pattern
   interrupt lives here.
3. **The face on the stake.** One named person or community from the
   source whose fate the outcome decides, on screen before the centre and
   again at the close — or the recorded refusal "the source names no
   one", in which case the film stays systemic and SHORTER. Where the
   hook is an event, the beat before it belongs to the person it lands on
   (Stein: "the reader must know the people in the car before he sees the
   car crash"). The force that resists also wears a name and a documented
   decision where the record gives one.
4. **Need-to-know.** The one comprehension fact early; the film's secret
   last or nearly. Telegraph the DESTINATION (a date, a confrontation the
   source promises); PREPARE the turn with a documented early detail whose
   meaning lands later; never announce it ("but it would all go wrong" is
   a defect). Name a force early, show it whole late. Every movement ends
   on something unresolved and the next re-enters elsewhere or quietly —
   a movement that answers its own question in its last beat loses the
   viewer at the cut.
5. **The verdict of values.** The ending contract additionally names
   which value wins and by whose account — right wins, wrong fails, right
   loses, wrong wins, an ironic lean, or an honestly open record — and the
   close carries it as consequence or image. The meaning is spoken at
   most once, late, in the source's words, never as a moral; where the
   record shows an intention/result gap, both halves are placed adjacent
   and the narrator never says "ironically".

Two orders govern all five: **feeling before argument** (the first minute
makes the viewer feel something the documented events can produce, before
any claim is made — plan the feeling per movement), and **prepare, do not
announce**. Grounding outranks every clause: a question, face, secret or
verdict the source does not carry is unavailable, and saying so is the
job.

## The telling block

The spine declares the film's telling in plain words, one field each —
`question`, `handle` (the film in one sentence), `whose_film`, `face`,
`opposer`, `disclosure` (what the viewer knows at the start:
question_first / outcome_first_how_later / staircase / answer_first —
the last only when the source itself IS the reveal), `held` (the secret
and where it is released), `engine` (mystery / suspense / irony /
curiosity / none — irony only when the source reports what the actors
believed at the time), `set_up_beats` (0–2; 1 whenever the hook is an
event that lands on someone), `turn_prepared_by` (omen / plant /
dated_statement / none), `centre`, `reveals` (screen order, each
outranking the last), `antagonism` (weight and rendering — `best_case`
by default; `stated_flatly` only when the source itself reports the view
that thinly), `ending_verdict`, `controlling_idea` (a value and its
cause, with its source anchor), `charged_image` (the film's one
documented object or place — the source's natural object, never an
emblem — where it first appears and where it returns as a glance), and
`stance` (anchor / classify). Each movement also declares its `rung`
(`particular` — a documented moment; `claim` — a stated generalization
anchored by one concrete; never the middle rung) and its `feeling` (what
the viewer feels there). These are declarations, not scores: the writer
executes them, the benches judge the finished film against them, and the
operator reads them in review.

## How the axes compose (and where they collide)

Most register pairings compose freely — a helicopter film can speak
wire-clean (news_brief) or warm (human_story); a correction can be
playful or prosecutorial. The exceptions, recorded so they are
surfaced instead of silently fighting:

| | Natural voice pairings | Collisions (surface, don't forbid) |
|---|---|---|
| Helicopter view | explainer, news_brief | human_story (voice wants a person the structure demotes) |
| One scene first | human_story, explainer | news_brief (inverted pyramid contradicts in→out) |
| The case | accountability, historical | teaser (a case withholding its outcome becomes clickbait) |
| The portrait | human_story | accountability (prosecuting your own subject needs an editor's intent, not a default) |
| The timeline | historical, explainer | teaser |
| The verdict | accountability | human_story |
| The open question | explainer, accountability | news_brief (a brief answers up front) |
| The numbers | news_brief, explainer | human_story |
| The correction | explainer, accountability | — (the form is voice-elastic) |
| The object | historical, explainer | news_brief |
| The hindsight | historical, accountability | teaser (the outcome is GIVEN; withholding it breaks the card) |
| The choice | accountability, explainer | teaser |

Conceptual-register interactions are simpler: `concept_piece` and
`argument_trace` compose with any approach (the taught idea's
plant/anecdote/return slots into the approach's acts — the helicopter
teaches its mechanism, the scene-first film teaches through its
scene); `narrative`/`conjunctural` films simply carry no concept
apparatus. The concept budget stays a LENGTH law (1 idea ≈ 1 minute of
teaching time).

Two registers still carry structural habits from before this axis
existed (`human_story` leans portrait, `historical` leans timeline).
Register+approach agreement is the default; a deliberate mismatch is
legitimate when the editor asks for it — apply both contracts and let
the VOICE clauses of the register and the STRUCTURE clauses of the
approach each govern their own axis.

## Structure declarations

The spine's movements each declare their `zoom` (in / out / hold_low /
hold_high) and `evidence_texture` (scene / aggregate / document /
testimony / number) — these are your structural promises, and the
benches judge the finished script against them. Your answer — spine
round and board alike — declares, at the TOP LEVEL, its
`hook_content` (what KIND of opening it makes — a scene, a number, a
claim, a question, a myth, an object, a system outcome; read your own
first movement and name it, exactly one of those strings, never
null/omitted) and its `ending_contract` (the approach's authored
close). Approach-specific
declarations exist where a contract demands one: the single `pivot`
movement of a scene-first film, the `date_label` on every timeline
movement, the `candidates[]` of an open question, the
`portrait_subject` / `case_bounds` / `object_subject` declarations
with their verbatim source anchors, the `hindsight_belief` of a
hindsight film (what the actors believed at the time, the outcome, and
the passage that reports the belief) and the `choice_options` of a
choice film (both options, the decision, and the passage that carries
the alternative).
