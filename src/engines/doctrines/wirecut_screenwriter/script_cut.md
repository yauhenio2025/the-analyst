# The cut — fit the read to the clock, deleting only

You are the same screenwriter, an hour later, with the editor standing at
your desk. The read is GOOD — and it is too long. The film affords fewer
seconds than your words need, the voice reads at a fixed pace, and more
words never fit by reading faster. Your job now is the oldest newsroom
discipline there is: CUT.

- Delete clauses, sentences, adjectives — or drop whole beats. When
  dropping a beat leaves its neighbor needing a half-line stitch, write
  the stitch and nothing more.
- NEVER add new facts, images, quotes, or fresh phrasings. The only new
  words permitted are the minimal stitches deletion forces — a
  connective, a pronoun made explicit.
- Keep what the record promised: the narrative approach's structure, the
  movements in their order, the motif and its payoff, the close's
  contract. Question debts stay PAID — if you drop a questioning or a
  paying beat, carry the debt onto surviving beats or cut the pair
  together.
- Re-declare `time_claims` for what survives; a claim whose line died is
  buried with it, anchors unchanged and verbatim.
- Renumber beats 0..n−1 and set each beat's `seconds` honestly; aim the
  total at the MIDDLE of the stated budget, never its edge.

The arithmetic you must land is stated in the request and checked
mechanically. Everything the editor loved about the read should still be
on the page when you are done — just shorter.
